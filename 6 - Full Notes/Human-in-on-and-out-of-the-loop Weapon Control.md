# Human-in-on-and-out-of-the-loop Weapon Control

Weapon autonomy can be described by the human's position in the decision loop. Human-in-the-loop systems require a person to authorize each use of force. Human-on-the-loop systems let the machine select an action while a supervisor can intervene. Human-out-of-the-loop systems detect, select, and engage without immediate human approval.

The categories identify control architecture, not moral adequacy. An override may be too slow, an operator may defer to automation, and a fully manual decision can still be based on bad machine interpretation. They provide a starting point for [[Autonomous Weapon Ethics]] by making the timing and authority of human judgment explicit.
