# Robotic Spacecraft Revolution

The robotic spacecraft revolution was the shift from assuming that humans must navigate, point instruments, and make observations to using remotely operated machines for most space science and Earth services. Miniaturized electronics and instruments that could survive vibration, vacuum, radiation, and temperature extremes made long-lived unmanned missions practical.

Robots are much cheaper to sustain than people and can travel to environments or distances that make life support prohibitive. They enabled planetary flybys and landers, orbital observatories, weather systems, communications, and navigation. Human missions remained important for prestige, maintenance, and studying the human body, but machines produced most of the reach and infrastructure of spaceflight.
