# Arbitrary-Basis Quantum Measurement

A qubit can be measured in any orthonormal basis of its two-dimensional state space, not only in $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$. The state is first expressed as amplitudes along the chosen basis vectors, and the squared magnitudes of those amplitudes determine the possible outcomes.

Measurement statistics are therefore basis-dependent. A state that is definite in one basis can be an equal superposition in another, as a photon with one polarization can have probabilistic transmission through a differently oriented filter. The measuring device defines which distinction becomes classical information.
