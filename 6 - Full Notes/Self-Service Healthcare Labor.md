# Self-Service Healthcare Labor

Self-service healthcare labor is work shifted to patients when technology asks them to collect measurements, enter records, maintain devices, interpret outputs, coordinate information, or perform tasks previously handled by an institution.

Some of this work can increase control and make care fit daily life. It becomes problematic when a service reduces its own cost or workload while presenting every transferred task as empowerment, especially if the patient receives no time, support, or authority in return.

Evaluation should ask who now performs the task, who is responsible when it fails, and whether the new arrangement improves health or merely relocates administration. The answer depends on the person's condition, resources, and relationship with care rather than on the presence of an application alone.
