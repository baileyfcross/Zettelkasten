# Weber's Tactile Distance Illusion

Weber's tactile distance illusion is the perception that the same physical separation between two skin contacts is larger on a body region with finer [[Haptic Spatial Acuity]]. A distance placed on the hand or face can therefore feel longer than that distance on the forearm or leg.

Orientation also matters at some sites: transverse and longitudinal separations can be judged differently. Receptive-field geometry and anatomical landmarks help shape the result. Tactile-display spacing must consequently be calibrated for its body location instead of assuming that centimeters on the skin have a uniform perceptual scale.
