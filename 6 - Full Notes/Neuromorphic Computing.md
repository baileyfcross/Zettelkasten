# Neuromorphic Computing

Neuromorphic computing builds hardware around many interconnected, low-power [[Spiking Neuron|spiking units]] rather than using only a centrally sequenced processor. Signals are exchanged as events over time, and the units can communicate in parallel while maintaining local state.

Kelleher discusses this architecture as a possible way to reduce energy demands for some neural computations. Its computational organization is distinctive, but a chip's resemblance to biological neurons does not make it a complete model of a human brain or automatically replace ordinary deep-learning hardware. The book also notes the challenge of creating algorithms and software for such large parallel systems.
