# Boundary Layers and Flow Separation

The boundary layer is the near-surface region where viscosity changes flow velocity from zero at the solid surface toward the free-stream value. Its thickness is often defined at the point where velocity reaches about 99 percent of free stream. An adverse pressure gradient can make this layer separate, weakening lift and increasing drag as the aircraft approaches [[Angle of Attack and Critical Stall]].
