# Square Root

A square root of (a) is a number whose [[Square]] equals (a). The radical notation (sqrt{a}) denotes the nonnegative square root when (a\geq0); for example, (sqrt{25}=5) because (5^2=25).

A positive number also has a negative value whose square is the same, which is why solving (x^2=25) gives (x=5) or (x=-5). If a natural number is not a perfect square, its square root is irrational. Negative real numbers do not have real square roots.
