# Steel Beam Shear Strength

In an I-shaped steel beam, the web carries most of the vertical shear while the flanges carry most of the bending. A stocky web can reach shear yielding, whereas a slender web may buckle before yield. Web depth-to-thickness ratio therefore determines which nominal shear-strength expression applies and whether stiffeners or post-buckling action can be used.

High shear can also reduce available flexural strength in regions where both effects are large. Concentrated reactions and loads introduce separate local web checks, so adequate overall shear capacity does not prove that a support region is safe. Beam design follows the shear diagram to identify maximum demand, considers holes or copes that interrupt the web, and coordinates stiffener and connection details with the assumed force path.
