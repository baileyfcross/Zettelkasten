# Null Hypothesis

A null hypothesis specifies the skeptical scenario against which an observed result is compared, commonly that a treatment produces no population difference. It is a model of what the data-generating process would look like without the effect being investigated.

Evidence is evaluated through the [[Null Distribution]] of a chosen statistic. Failing to reject the null does not prove it true; it means the observation was not sufficiently inconsistent with that model under the test's assumptions.

The decision rule is deliberately conservative: the significance threshold limits the long-run probability of rejecting a true null, while failure to reject leaves open a Type II error. The hypotheses, statistic, and rejection region should therefore be defined from the research design before the observed result is used to make the decision.
