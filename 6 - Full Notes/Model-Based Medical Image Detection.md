# Model-Based Medical Image Detection

Model-based medical image detection encodes expected object shape, appearance, acquisition physics, or anatomical arrangement and searches for image evidence that fits the model. The prior structure can make detection more robust when raw intensity alone is ambiguous.

The advantage is interpretability and the ability to impose clinically plausible constraints. The risk is model bias: unusual anatomy or pathology may not match what the designer expected. The book contrasts this approach with [[Data-Driven Medical Image Detection]], suggesting that useful systems often combine physical or anatomical models with patterns learned from data.
