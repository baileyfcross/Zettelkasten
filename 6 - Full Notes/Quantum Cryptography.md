# Quantum Cryptography

Quantum cryptography uses properties of quantum states to construct security mechanisms, especially the exchange of cryptographic keys. Its protection comes from how preparation and measurement affect a physical state rather than only from the assumed difficulty of a classical mathematical problem.

Superposition supplies multiple possible measurement outcomes, [[Entangled Quantum State|entanglement]] creates correlated systems, and measurement in an incompatible basis disturbs the transmitted information. [[Quantum Key Distribution]] turns those properties into a protocol in which communication errors can reveal interception. Classical Java programs can illustrate the control flow, but the source stresses that such simulations do not reproduce genuine quantum behavior without quantum hardware.
