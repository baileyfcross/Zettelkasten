# Translation Quality Criteria

Translation quality balances several goals that can conflict. A translation should preserve the source's information, tone, and structure while remaining grammatical, idiomatic, and easy to read in the target language. The correct balance changes with the audience, domain, era, and intended use.

These criteria are difficult to formalize because multiple target sentences can all be valid. A literal rendering may preserve words while losing meaning, whereas a paraphrase may improve comprehension while moving away from form. Machine translation evaluation therefore uses partial proxies such as adequacy, fluency, comprehension, and reference overlap rather than a single complete definition of a good translation.
