# Soviet Space Program

The Soviet space program grew from military missile institutes and competing design bureaus rather than a single civilian agency like NASA. Secrecy concealed organization, failures, and even cosmonaut identities until missions succeeded, allowing the state to present a carefully controlled series of technological firsts.

Early launch strength produced Sputnik, the first human orbital flight, and several lunar and planetary firsts. Internal competition, leadership changes, and the absence of a coherent successful lunar-landing system later weakened the Moon effort. The program shifted toward long-duration orbital stations, a capability that survived into the Russian era.
