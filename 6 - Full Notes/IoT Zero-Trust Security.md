# IoT Zero-Trust Security

IoT zero-trust security avoids treating a device or network location as inherently trustworthy. Each connection and requested action must be authenticated, authorized, and evaluated in context, even when the endpoint is already inside a home, factory, vehicle, or enterprise network.

This approach responds to the impossibility of making every operating system, firmware image, application, and protocol airtight. Encryption, certificate-based identity, restricted ports, monitoring, analytics, and anomaly detection provide overlapping evidence and controls. The objective is to limit what a compromised component can reach and do rather than assume a trusted perimeter will prevent compromise.
