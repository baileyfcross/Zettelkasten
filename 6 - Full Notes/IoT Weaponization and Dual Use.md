# IoT Weaponization and Dual Use

IoT weaponization and dual use describe how connected sensing, robotics, drones, additive manufacturing, and autonomous coordination can support beneficial tasks or be repurposed for crime and warfare. A robot that searches rubble, a drone that monitors crops, and a sensor swarm that maps weather can use capabilities similar to systems for spying, delivering weapons, or selecting targets.

Connectivity increases reach and coordination, while autonomy can separate a human decision from the later physical act. This complicates accountability: a person may issue an initial mission, but a machine can interpret conditions and act much later. Governance must therefore address permitted uses, meaningful human control, authentication, and consequences rather than classifying a technology as inherently peaceful or hostile.
