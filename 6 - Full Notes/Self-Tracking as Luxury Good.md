# Self-Tracking as Luxury Good

Self-tracking as a luxury good positions a wearable or service as a fashionable marker of status rather than only as an instrument for measurement. Design, brand, exclusivity, and visible consumption become part of the product's value.

This framing makes tracking appear optional and expressive for affluent consumers even when similar technologies are used as requirements in workplaces or health programs. It can also connect medically suggestive data with a device governed primarily as a consumer accessory.

The luxury position influences whose body and lifestyle appear in marketing. When young and already fit users define the imagined customer, the product may neglect people with chronic illness or fewer resources who could benefit from a carefully designed health tool.
