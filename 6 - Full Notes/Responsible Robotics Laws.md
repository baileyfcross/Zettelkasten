# Responsible Robotics Laws

Responsible robotics principles place the primary ethical burden on people and institutions rather than on imagined machine wisdom. A robot should be deployed only when the complete human-robot work system meets legal, professional, safety, and ethical standards; it should respond appropriately to different human roles; and its situated autonomy should support safe, smooth transfers of control.

This framing treats design, deployment, supervision, and handoff as human responsibilities. It is more practical than [[Asimov Three Laws of Robotics]] because it does not require a machine to solve morality internally. It also makes organizational standards and human authority part of the system being evaluated.
