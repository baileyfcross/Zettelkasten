# Tracking Burden

Tracking burden is the time, attention, interpretation, maintenance, and emotional effort required to produce and use a personal record. Automatic sensors reduce some entry work, but they still require charging, synchronization, correction, review, and decisions about what the output means.

Burden can change the activity being observed. Food logging may favor packaged products that are easier to enter, while a discouraging weight number may prevent continued reflection. In health settings, new tools can also transfer clerical and interpretive work from institutions to patients.

A proportionate practice uses the simplest measure that can answer the question, starts with a [[Brief Self-Tracking Experiment]], and stops when the expected learning no longer justifies the effort.
