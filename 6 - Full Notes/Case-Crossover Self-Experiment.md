# Case-Crossover Self-Experiment

A case-crossover self-experiment compares a person's condition during an event with the same person's condition during another period. The individual serves as their own comparison, reducing the importance of stable differences between people.

For example, someone investigating headaches might compare sleep, food, stress, and activity before headache episodes with the same variables on symptom-free days. The design is helpful for identifying candidate triggers, especially when an ordinary population average does not describe the individual's pattern.

The comparison remains vulnerable to changing routines, expectations, missing observations, and several factors moving together. It is a structured aid to [[Symptom Debugging Through Tracking]], not proof that the strongest association is the underlying cause.
