# Convolution Theorem

The convolution theorem states that the Fourier transform of a [[Convolution Integral]] is the product of the individual Fourier transforms. Thus, if $C=f*g$, then the reciprocal-space functions satisfy $C(k)=F(k)G(k)$ under a consistent transform convention.

This converts an integral relation in the original domain into ordinary multiplication in transform space. The reverse correspondence also turns multiplication in the original domain into convolution of spectra, making the theorem a bridge between filtering and spectral analysis.
