# Twin Paradox

The twin paradox compares an observer who remains in one inertial frame with a traveler who departs and later returns. During each uniform-motion segment, either twin can describe the other's clock as slow. The situations cease to be symmetric because the traveler changes velocity to reunite, switching inertial frames and changing which distant Earth events count as simultaneous.

The invariant resolution uses [[Proper Time]]. Both twins share departure and reunion events, but they follow different [[Worldline|worldlines]]. In flat spacetime, the unaccelerated straight timelike path between those events accumulates more proper time than the bent path containing the turnaround. The stay-at-home twin is therefore older at reunion.

Acceleration identifies where the traveler changes frames, but the age difference is calculated from the entire spacetime paths, not from a special biological effect of acceleration.
