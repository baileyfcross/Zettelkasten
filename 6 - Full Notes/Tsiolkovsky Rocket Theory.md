# Tsiolkovsky Rocket Theory

Tsiolkovsky rocket theory established that reaction propulsion works in a vacuum and used Newtonian mechanics to calculate the motion of a vehicle whose mass decreases as propellant is expelled. Konstantin Tsiolkovsky also recognized the value of high-energy liquid propellants and multiple stages for reaching spaceflight velocities.

His publications appeared in Russia around the turn of the twentieth century and initially circulated only narrowly. Their importance lies less in an immediately buildable machine than in showing that space travel could be treated as a quantitative engineering problem. The theory provided the mathematical foundation later expressed through the [[Variable-Mass Rocket Equation]] and [[Rocket Staging]].
