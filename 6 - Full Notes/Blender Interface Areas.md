# Blender Interface Areas

A Blender workspace can span one or more windows, and each window is divided into areas. Every area holds an editor, while regions such as a header, Toolbar, or Sidebar provide controls belonging to that editor.

Areas share a common layout system regardless of editor type. Their borders can be dragged to resize them, and corner widgets or border commands can split one area into two or join neighboring areas. This lets a workspace be adjusted without opening overlapping dialogs.

In Blender 2.77, dragging the cross-hatched corner splits an area into two copies, after which either copy can become another editor type. Dragging the corner back into a neighboring area joins them again. The operation edits the interface layout, not the scene data shown inside it.
