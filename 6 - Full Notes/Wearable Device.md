# Wearable Device

A wearable device is computing equipment designed to be carried on the body, often as a watch, band, clip, garment, or accessory. It can use sensors to collect measurements while remaining integrated into ordinary movement and routines.

Wearability is social as well as technical. Miniaturized electronics, lower-power components, and wireless connections make the device possible, while familiar forms such as a wristwatch make it imaginable as something a person would display and repeatedly wear. Fashion, status, comfort, and workplace expectations can therefore affect adoption as much as sensor capability.

A wearable may cross consumer, employment, and medical settings. Its physical form does not determine whether the collected data are harmless wellness information or consequential health and surveillance records.
