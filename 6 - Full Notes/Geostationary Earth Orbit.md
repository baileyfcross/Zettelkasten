# Geostationary Earth Orbit

Geostationary Earth orbit is a circular, zero-inclination orbit about 22,300 miles above the equator whose period matches Earth's rotation. A satellite there appears fixed over one longitude, allowing a ground antenna to point continuously at the same place in the sky.

The geometry is especially useful for communications and continuous observation of a broad region. Three well-placed platforms were proposed as a route to near-global communications before robotic electronics made the idea practical. The orbit is a limited shared resource because satellites require assigned positions and radio spectrum while all occupy the same ring.
