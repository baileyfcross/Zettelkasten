# Social Media Syndromic Surveillance

Social media syndromic surveillance groups public posts into symptom patterns that may indicate emerging health events. Text classification, location inference, and temporal aggregation turn scattered messages into counts or alerts for a region and period.

Unlike confirmed case reporting, the method observes self-expression under uneven participation. Language is informal, location can be missing, and repeated or copied posts can inflate counts. The surveillance pipeline must distinguish personal symptom reports from commentary and quantify uncertainty before the output informs public-health action.
