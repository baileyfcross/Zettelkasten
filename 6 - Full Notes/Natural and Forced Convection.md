# Natural and Forced Convection

Natural convection arises when temperature-dependent density differences create buoyant motion without a fan or pump. Warm fluid tends to rise and cooler, denser fluid tends to descend, forming circulation that enhances heat exchange near a surface.

Forced convection uses mechanical motion to drive the fluid and can raise the effective [[Convective Heat Transfer Coefficient|convection coefficient]] substantially. A boundary-coefficient model may represent either regime, while a [[Buoyancy-Driven Thermal Flow|coupled flow model]] resolves the motion explicitly.
