# Turing-Decidable Language

A language is Turing-decidable when a [[Turing Machine]] computes its characteristic function: it halts on every input with 1 for membership and 0 for nonmembership. The requirement to halt in both cases distinguishes decision from mere acceptance.

Turing-decidable languages are also called [[Recursive Language|recursive languages]]. A language is decidable exactly when both it and its complement are [[Turing-Acceptable Language|Turing-acceptable]].
