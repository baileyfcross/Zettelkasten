# Proper Time

Proper time is the time recorded by a clock that travels between two timelike-separated [[Spacetime Event|events]]. In the clock's instantaneous rest frame, the two events occur at the same spatial position, so the [[Invariant Spacetime Interval]] reduces to a time measurement along the clock's own [[Worldline]].

For motion at speed $v$ relative to an inertial frame, $d\tau=dt/\gamma$, where $\gamma$ is the [[Lorentz Factor]]. The coordinate-time interval $dt$ can differ between observers, but every observer calculates the same proper time for a specified path.

Proper time is path-dependent. Between two reunion events, an inertial straight worldline accumulates more proper time than a path that changes velocity, which is the geometric core of the [[Twin Paradox]].
