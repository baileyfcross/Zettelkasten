# Steady and Transient Thermal Behavior

A thermal system is steady when its reported properties no longer vary with time. A transient model retains time as an independent variable and describes heating, cooling, storage, curing, or other evolution before equilibrium is reached.

A stationary answer can reveal the eventual operating condition but cannot reconstruct how the system got there. When peak temperature, warm-up time, or exposure history matters, [[Stationary and Time-Dependent Thermal Studies|time-dependent analysis]] is necessary even if a final steady state also exists.
