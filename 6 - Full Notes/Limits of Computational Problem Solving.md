# Limits of Computational Problem Solving

Computational problems face two fundamental limits. Some have no algorithmic solution, while others have algorithms whose time or storage grows so rapidly that no available machine can finish them in a useful period.

Good computational thinking identifies these limits early and seeks alternatives such as [[Algorithmic Heuristic|heuristics]], approximation, changed representations, or a narrower problem. It also recognizes social and political questions that computation can illuminate but cannot settle.
