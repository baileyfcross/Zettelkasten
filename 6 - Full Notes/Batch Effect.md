# Batch Effect

A batch effect is systematic variation caused by when, where, or how samples were processed rather than by the biological condition being studied. Dates, reagent lots, instruments, operators, and laboratories can each create shared shifts among measurements.

The effect becomes especially damaging when batch membership aligns with the outcome, because technical structure can imitate a biological difference. Experimental randomization, metadata, exploratory analysis, and model-based adjustment provide complementary protection.
