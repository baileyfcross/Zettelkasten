# Haptic Sensing

Haptic sensing combines information from touch with [[Kinesthesia]], the sense of limb position and movement. Skin receptors report contact, vibration, temperature, and deformation, while signals from muscles, tendons, joints, and motor commands describe how the exploring limb is moving and exerting force.

Because the hand normally moves to acquire this information, haptic sensing supports both perception and action. It identifies object properties while continuously helping the nervous system adjust posture and grip. Removing that feedback makes ordinary manipulation clumsy and forces vision to supervise movements that are normally automatic.
