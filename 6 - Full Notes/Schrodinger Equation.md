# Schrodinger Equation

The Schrodinger equation governs the evolution of a nonrelativistic quantum state. In one dimension its time-dependent form is

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\left(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}+V\right)\psi.$$

The wavefunction itself is not a material wave; $|\psi|^2$ supplies a probability density for measurement outcomes.

When the potential is time independent, separation writes the wavefunction as a spatial state times a phase factor and produces an energy eigenvalue equation. Continuity, single-valuedness, normalization, and boundary conditions select physically admissible solutions; infinite wells and other bound potentials admit only discrete energies because most formal solutions fail those conditions.
