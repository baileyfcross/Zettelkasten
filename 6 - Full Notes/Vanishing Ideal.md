# Vanishing Ideal

The vanishing ideal I(V) of a subset V of affine space consists of all polynomials that evaluate to zero at every point of V. It is an [[Ideal]] because sums and polynomial multiples of vanishing functions also vanish.

An [[Affine Variety]] is recovered as the common zero set of an ideal. The relationship reverses inclusion: adding equations shrinks the point set, while adding points reduces the collection of polynomials that vanish everywhere.
