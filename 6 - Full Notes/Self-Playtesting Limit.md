# Self-Playtesting Limit

Self-playtesting can reveal state frequencies, dominant strategies, and mechanical failures, but it cannot reproduce the experience of a person who lacks the designer's knowledge. The designer knows hidden information, intended goals, and correct interpretations even while trying to ignore them.

Assigning distinct strategic personas can make solo testing more systematic, especially for a multiplayer analog game. The results are still evidence about system states rather than dependable evidence about surprise, comprehension, or fun, so outside playtests remain necessary.
