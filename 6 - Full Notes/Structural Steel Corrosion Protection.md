# Structural Steel Corrosion Protection

Steel corrosion is an electrochemical process sustained by moisture and oxygen and accelerated by salts, chemicals, crevices, trapped water, and incompatible metals. Protection starts with detailing: drain water, avoid pockets, provide access, isolate galvanic couples, and eliminate surfaces that cannot be prepared or maintained.

Coating systems depend on environment and may include shop primer, barrier coats, zinc-rich paint, or galvanizing. Surface preparation is essential because mill scale, rust, oil, and poor profile can prevent adhesion. Enclosed dry interior steel may need little protection, while exterior, industrial, or intermittently wet members need a system matched to exposure. Inspection and planned recoating turn corrosion control into a life-cycle process rather than a one-time specification.
