# Vector Triple Product

A vector triple product nests two cross products, such as $a\times(b\times c)$. Its result lies in the plane spanned by the two vectors inside the outer expansion rather than generally being perpendicular to all three vectors.

The identity $a\times(b\times c)=b(a\cdot c)-c(a\cdot b)$ rewrites the nested cross product as a difference of two scalar-weighted vectors. Parentheses matter because the cross product is not associative: $(a\times b)\times c$ has a different expansion.
