# Human Spaceflight Firsts

Human spaceflight firsts are milestones whose political value comes from being achieved before a rival: first person in space, first full orbit, first woman, first spacewalk, or first landing on another world. They turn complex engineering into a simple public comparison.

The Soviet Union's early sequence, including Yuri Gagarin's 1961 orbit, created pressure on the United States to choose a later objective large enough to reset the competition. Because a first cannot be repeated, the race encouraged compressed schedules and risk while giving individual spacefarers exceptional symbolic status.
