# User Control of Personal Data

User control of personal data is the practical ability to understand, access, correct, object to, or otherwise influence how one's information is processed. Control requires usable mechanisms as well as formal statements of rights.

Big-data inference complicates control because derived attributes may affect a person even when they never supplied those attributes directly. Systems must therefore account for both observed and inferred data.

In self-tracking, control has several technical levels. A person may be allowed to view a dashboard but prevented from downloading the observations, obtaining minimally processed sensor readings, or connecting another service through an interface. Practical control includes export and portability as well as the ability to understand how a score was produced. It also matters when a device is implanted in the person's body or used to manage a condition, because excluding the person from their own data conflicts with demands that they take responsibility for their health.
