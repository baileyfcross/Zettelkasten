# Center-of-Momentum Frame

The center-of-momentum frame is an inertial frame in which the total spatial momentum of a system is zero. It is often called the center-of-mass frame, although total momentum is the defining quantity in relativistic problems.

In this frame the system's total [[Four-Momentum]] has only an energy component. Its Minkowski norm therefore gives the system's [[Invariant Mass]] directly. Because that norm is frame-independent, calculations made in the center-of-momentum frame can be transformed to any other inertial frame without changing the physical result.

Collision and decay problems are often simplest in this frame: outgoing spatial momenta must sum to zero, while total energy equals the system's center-of-momentum energy. Internal motion can make the system's invariant mass exceed the sum of the rest masses of a final bound object.
