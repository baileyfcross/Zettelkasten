# Haptic Sign Language Reception

Haptic sign language reception allows a deaf-blind person to follow signing by placing one or both hands over the signer's hands and passively tracing their movement. It is most useful when the receiver learned visual sign language before losing vision.

Tactile reception is slower and produces more errors than viewing the same signs because the hands cannot capture every spatial detail simultaneously. Even so, skilled users achieve practical communication by using movement, hand configuration, and linguistic context together. The method preserves an existing language rather than inventing an unrelated tactile code.
