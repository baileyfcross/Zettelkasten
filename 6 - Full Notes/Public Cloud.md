# Public Cloud

A public cloud offers services to the public or to organizations generally, most often through the Internet. A provider pools resources across consumers and commonly charges recurring operating expenditure with little or no consumer capital investment.

Public reach and shared scale can improve convenience and cost flexibility. A consumer must still determine where data may be stored, which legal or regulatory requirements apply, how tenants are isolated, and what happens if the provider becomes unavailable.

AWS is an example of this deployment model: customers obtain logically isolated resources from a provider-operated global infrastructure and can select regions and services to address latency, resilience, data residency, and compliance requirements.
