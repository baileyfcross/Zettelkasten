# Electrostatic Field

An electrostatic field is a time-independent electric field produced by stationary charge. Its curl is zero, so it can be represented as the negative gradient of a scalar electric potential.

Gauss's law and material permittivity determine how charge shapes the field. The potential satisfies Poisson's equation in charged regions and Laplace's equation where volume charge is absent.

Symmetry determines whether integral Gauss-law calculations are practical in Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinates. Boundary-value solutions can instead determine the scalar potential first and recover the field by differentiation, with surface or volume charge fixing discontinuities and source terms.
