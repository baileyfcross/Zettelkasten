# Valid Argument

An argument is valid when its [[Conclusion]] is true in every case where all of its premises are true. Validity concerns the logical form of the inference, not whether the premises happen to be factually true.

A counterexample assignment that makes every [[Premise]] true and the conclusion false proves invalidity. A formal proof establishes validity by deriving the conclusion through a sequence of accepted inference rules such as [[Modus Ponens]].
