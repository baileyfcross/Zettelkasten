# Hexagonal Binning

Hexagonal binning divides a two-dimensional scatterplot into hexagonal cells and encodes the number of observations in each cell. It replaces severe point overplotting with a density summary while treating neighboring directions more evenly than a square grid. In conditioned or matrix displays, a shared color scale is needed for density comparison across panels. Binning hides individual observations, so rare points may need a separate overlay.
