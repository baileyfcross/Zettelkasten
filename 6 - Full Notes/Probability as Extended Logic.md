# Probability as Extended Logic

Probability can be treated as an extension of logical reasoning for cases where knowledge is incomplete or uncertain. Logic distinguishes conclusions that follow or do not follow from premises, while probability assigns degrees of plausibility to alternatives conditioned on what is known.

Certain and impossible propositions remain boundary cases within the probabilistic calculus. The extension matters because real models omit influences, so rational computation must represent more than absolute truth and falsehood.
