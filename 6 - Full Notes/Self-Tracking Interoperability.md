# Self-Tracking Interoperability

Self-tracking interoperability is the ability of devices, platforms, and services to exchange personal measurements while preserving their usable meaning. A successful connection needs agreement about format, identity, units, time, and the interpretation of each field.

Interoperability lets a person combine manual observations with sensor records, move among services, or connect consumer and clinical tools. It can also enable firms to build coaching or analysis on data produced by another company's device.

Technical connection does not settle governance. The person should understand which parties receive the data and be able to authorize or revoke the exchange. The boundary between clinical and nonclinical records can further complicate standards, obligations, and permitted uses.
