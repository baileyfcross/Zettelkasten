# Direct Proof

A direct proof of p → q assumes p and uses definitions, established results, and valid deductions to obtain q. For a universally quantified claim, the argument begins with an arbitrary member of the domain so that the conclusion applies to every member rather than a special case.

Direct proofs often succeed by translating abstract properties into their defining equations and then transforming those equations until the desired property becomes visible.
