# Research Report Structure

A research report structure makes the chain from problem to conclusion inspectable. The problem section establishes the theory, hypotheses or questions, definitions, and background. The methodology describes the sample, procedures, instrument, variable measurements, analysis plan, and any pilot or pretest work.

The results section reports the evidence and the decisions made from it, with enough detail to show how the analysis follows from the design. Interpretation explains what the results mean, considers alternatives and limitations, and relates them to prior work. Conclusions then answer the original research question without expanding beyond what the evidence supports.

This organization is argumentative rather than ceremonial. Every methodological choice should serve the stated problem, and every conclusion should be traceable through an interpreted result to an observation produced by the design.
