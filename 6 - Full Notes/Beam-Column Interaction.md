# Beam-Column Interaction

A beam-column carries axial force and bending moment at the same time. The combination arises from frame action, eccentric reactions, transverse loads, imperfect alignment, and lateral loading. Compression reduces the moment capacity that remains available and can magnify deflection, while axial tension interacts differently because it does not drive compression buckling.

Interaction equations compare fractions of available axial and flexural strength, including bending about one or both principal axes. Required moments must include applicable second-order amplification rather than only first-order analysis results. Connection eccentricity and unbalanced beam reactions can create moments even in nominally braced frames. Adequacy therefore depends on member strength, stability method, unbraced lengths, moment gradient, and the frame model used to obtain demand.
