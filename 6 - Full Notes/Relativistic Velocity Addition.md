# Relativistic Velocity Addition

Relativistic velocity addition combines collinear velocities without allowing their result to exceed the speed of light. If an object moves at $u$ in one frame and that frame moves at $v$ relative to another, the second frame measures

$$u'=\frac{u+v}{1+uv/c^2}$$

for velocities in the same direction under the chosen convention.

At speeds much smaller than $c$, the denominator is nearly one and the rule approaches ordinary addition. Near the light-speed limit, the denominator prevents a sum such as $0.8c+0.9c$ from becoming superluminal.

The rule is simplest in terms of [[Rapidity]]. Successive collinear [[Lorentz Boost|boosts]] add their rapidities, and converting the combined rapidity back through the hyperbolic tangent produces the nonlinear velocity formula.
