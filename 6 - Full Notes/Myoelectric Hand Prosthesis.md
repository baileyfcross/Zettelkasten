# Myoelectric Hand Prosthesis

A myoelectric hand prosthesis records electromyographic activity from muscles in a residual limb and uses those signals to control an electrically powered hand. It can provide more grasp types with less gross body effort than a cable-driven prosthesis and can generate substantial pinch force.

Its central limitation is often missing [[Prosthetic Sensory Feedback]]. The user must watch the hand and infer device state from motor sound or vibration, increasing cognitive load and slowing ordinary tasks. More degrees of motion do not automatically create dexterity when control and touch information remain difficult to learn and interpret.
