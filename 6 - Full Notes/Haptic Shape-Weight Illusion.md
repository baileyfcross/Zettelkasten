# Haptic Shape-Weight Illusion

The haptic shape-weight illusion occurs when objects of equal mass and volume feel different in heaviness because their shapes produce different perceived sizes. A cube may appear larger than an equal-volume sphere and consequently feel lighter when lifted.

The effect is closely related to the [[Size-Weight Illusion]] and illustrates how geometric interpretation changes a material judgment. It persists after grip forces adjust to actual weight, so the explanation cannot be reduced to one mistaken lift. Shape, expected density, and learned relations among size and mass remain part of the percept.
