# Quantum Lattice Gauge Theory Dimension Roadmap

Quantum simulation of lattice gauge theory can progress through spatial dimension rather than attempting full $(3+1)$-dimensional Standard Model dynamics immediately. One-dimensional systems are already used for real-time and variational demonstrations, $(2+1)$-dimensional models retain gauge-field degrees of freedom and important phenomena, and $(3+1)$-dimensional theories remain the long-term target.

The lower-dimensional stages are not merely miniature hardware tests. Models such as compact $(2+1)$-dimensional QED exhibit asymptotic freedom and confinement, while non-Abelian $(2+1)$-dimensional SU(2) forces an implementation to handle both matter and gauge fields. These systems expose encoding, truncation, state-preparation, symmetry, and time-evolution problems that recur at higher dimension.

Progress along the roadmap should therefore be judged by which physical and computational difficulty a benchmark preserves, not just by qubit count. [[Two-Plus-One-Dimensional QED Quantum Benchmark]] is useful because it sits between small proof-of-principle systems and the much harder goal of quantum chromodynamics.
