# Minimum Content of Natural Law

The minimum content of natural law describes basic constraints that any durable legal order must address because people are vulnerable, resources are limited, and cooperation is necessary. It does not collapse law into morality, but recognizes practical conditions that law cannot ignore.

The argument opens a place for intrinsic limits within legal positivism. Privacy may be connected to those conditions when some protected independence is necessary for agency and social life.
