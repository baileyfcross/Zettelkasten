# Structural Integrity Against Progressive Collapse

Structural integrity is the ability of a building to remain tied together and avoid disproportionate collapse after localized damage. It depends on continuity, redundancy, connection ductility, and alternate paths that can redistribute force when one member or support becomes unavailable. A collection of individually adequate gravity members does not automatically provide this system behavior.

Integrity requirements may prescribe continuity of framing, minimum connection capacities, column splices, and ties around or through the structure. The intent is not to make every component indestructible but to prevent a local event from propagating without resistance. Details should provide deformation capacity and a complete path for redistributed forces, while transfer structures and discontinuities deserve special attention because their loss affects unusually large regions.
