# Algorithmic Privacy

Algorithmic privacy concerns the effects of automated analysis on a person's control over information and exposure. A system can threaten privacy without publishing a secret: it may combine ordinary traces, infer sensitive characteristics, predict behavior, or make consequential classifications invisible to the person involved.

Protection therefore covers both data and processing. People need intelligible information about automated use, safeguards against inappropriate reuse, and ways to challenge decisions. [[Meaningful Digital Consent]] helps, but it cannot carry the entire burden when collection is pervasive or participation in digital infrastructure is difficult to avoid.
