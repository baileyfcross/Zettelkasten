# Diffusion-Reaction Model of Random-Circuit XEB

After averaging random single-qubit gates in the doubled-circuit representation, a two-qubit entangling gate acts as a stochastic transfer matrix on vacuum and particle variables. Particles may stay in place, diffuse to a neighbor, duplicate, or recombine in pairs; vacuum alone cannot create or annihilate an isolated particle.

Each gate ensemble sets a diffusion rate $D$ and reaction rate $R$, while noise and omitted gates remove probability when particles encounter defective spacetime locations. The ideal process relaxes toward a binomial particle distribution with density $3/4$. Weak homogeneous loss largely rescales that equilibrium, whereas localized cuts behave as sinks and deform its shape, explaining why [[XEB Fidelity Proxy Conditions]] fail for a spoofed circuit.
