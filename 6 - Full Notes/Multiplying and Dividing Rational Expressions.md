# Multiplying and Dividing Rational Expressions

Rational expressions multiply by multiplying numerators and denominators. Factoring first allows common factors to be cancelled across the product. Division by a nonzero rational expression changes to multiplication by its [[Reciprocal]].

For each operation, every original denominator must be nonzero, and a divisor must also have a nonzero numerator so that its reciprocal is defined. Cancelling before expansion usually keeps the calculation smaller. The final expression should be stated together with the restrictions inherited from the original forms.
