# Virtual Harassment and Embodied Harm

Virtual harassment occurs when another participant uses an immersive environment to impose threatening, sexual, or otherwise abusive conduct. The bodies may be graphical, but [[Presence]], spatial proximity, and haptic feedback can make an unwanted act feel directed at the person rather than merely displayed on a screen.

This embodied quality complicates a legal distinction based only on physical contact. A virtual grope may not satisfy an older definition of battery, yet it can still produce fear, humiliation, or physiological stress. Multiplayer systems therefore need interaction boundaries, blocking or repelling controls, reporting paths, and ways to prevent another avatar from entering a protected personal space.

The design question is not whether a virtual act is materially identical to its physical counterpart. It is whether the system predictably enables harm and gives the affected person meaningful control and recourse.
