# Plastic Moment Capacity of Steel Beams

As bending increases, the extreme fibers of a steel section yield first at the yield moment. Additional load spreads yielding toward the neutral axis until the full cross-section reaches yield stress and forms a plastic hinge. The corresponding plastic moment equals yield stress multiplied by plastic section modulus, which reflects the fully yielded stress blocks.

Reaching plastic capacity requires a compact section and enough lateral bracing to prevent local or lateral-torsional buckling first. In a simply supported beam, one plastic hinge at the maximum-moment region creates a collapse mechanism; in a continuous system, redistribution can occur until enough hinges form to create a mechanism. Plastic strength is therefore both a cross-sectional property and a deformation-capacity requirement.
