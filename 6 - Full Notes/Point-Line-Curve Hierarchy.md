# Point-Line-Curve Hierarchy

A point marks a location without length, a line connects locations along a straight one-dimensional path, and a curve generalizes that path by allowing its direction to vary. Endpoints and intersections create the vertices through which these entities connect.

In a model, the hierarchy is operational as well as descriptive. Points can receive concentrated conditions, lines can carry one-dimensional physics or bound faces, and curves can represent edges whose continuity and curvature affect meshing.
