# AI and Economic Inequality

AI and economic inequality concerns how automated systems can concentrate wealth, opportunity, data, and decision-making power. Productivity gains may benefit firms and highly skilled groups while job displacement, surveillance, and error fall more heavily on people with fewer resources.

Inequality also affects the ability to escape harm. Wealthier people can purchase privacy, safer environments, better education, or alternatives to automated services, while others must accept systems on terms they did not choose. Ethical assessment should examine distribution and power rather than assuming that an aggregate benefit will eventually reach everyone.
