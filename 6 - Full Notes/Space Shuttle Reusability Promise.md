# Space Shuttle Reusability Promise

The Space Shuttle reusability promise was the claim that recovering and flying major vehicle components repeatedly would drastically lower launch cost and make access to orbit routine. That economic argument helped secure approval after more ambitious post-Apollo plans were rejected.

Operational experience exposed the difference between reusable hardware and inexpensive reuse. Inspection, repair, launch infrastructure, and a large workforce made each mission costly, while the integrated vehicle could not deliver the high flight rate assumed by the business case. Reusability must therefore be evaluated through the complete turnaround system rather than the fact that a vehicle returns intact.
