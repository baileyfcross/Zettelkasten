# Robot Adoption and Labor Cost

A technically automatable task may remain human work when labor is cheaper than acquiring, integrating, and maintaining a robot. The same company can use more robots in a high-wage factory than in a low-wage one even when both produce similar goods.

Adoption depends on the total system: wages and benefits, capital cost, production volume, uptime, training, safety, process redesign, and access to technical support. Falling component prices and rising employment costs can move the boundary over time. Technical capability is therefore necessary but not sufficient for workplace automation.
