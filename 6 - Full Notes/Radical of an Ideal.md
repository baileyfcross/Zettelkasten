# Radical of an Ideal

The radical √I of an [[Ideal]] I consists of all ring elements r for which some positive power rⁿ lies in I. It is itself an ideal and equals the intersection of all [[Prime Ideal]]s containing I.

Taking a radical removes information about powers while preserving the same prime support. An ideal equal to its radical is a [[Radical Ideal]], and the radical of the zero ideal is the [[Nilradical]].
