# Personal Data Interpretation

Personal data interpretation is the work of deciding what a record means in relation to a person's question, circumstances, and experience. A measurement does not contain its own significance. A high step count can indicate desired activity, physical overexertion, occupational demand, or a sensor error depending on why and how it was collected.

Interpretation moves between data and context. A person may compare a value with bodily sensation, annotate an unusual day, revise a category, or discuss a pattern with a clinician or peer. Aggregation can reveal a trend, while a detailed episode can explain an apparent outlier.

This work is often collaborative. A [[Self-Tracking Community of Practice]] supplies methods and comparisons, but the person whose life produced the record retains contextual knowledge that an external analyst lacks.
