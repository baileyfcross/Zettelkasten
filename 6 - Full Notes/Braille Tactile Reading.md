# Braille Tactile Reading

Braille tactile reading decodes characters formed from a compact two-by-three matrix of raised dots by actively scanning them with the fingertips. The cell is small enough to fit fingertip spatial acuity, unlike earlier larger dot systems that required movement across each symbol.

Skilled readers commonly use both hands because one finger can process dot patterns while another tracks spacing and alignment or prepares the next line. Reading remains slower than ordinary visual reading, but the logically constructed cell can represent letters and other symbols across languages and is one of the most successful tactile communication systems.
