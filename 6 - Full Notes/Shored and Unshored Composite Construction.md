# Shored and Unshored Composite Construction

In unshored composite construction, the bare steel beam carries its self-weight, wet concrete, deck, and construction loads before the slab hardens. After composite action develops, the composite section carries later superimposed loads. The final stress and deflection therefore combine pre-composite and post-composite stages rather than treating every load as if it acted on the transformed section.

Shoring supports the fresh floor and lets more load reach the beam after composite action develops, potentially allowing a smaller steel section or less initial deflection. It also adds temporary work, sequencing constraints, and cost. Long-term concrete creep, shrinkage, camber, ponding during placement, and crack-control reinforcement remain relevant. Construction method is thus part of structural analysis, not merely a contractor preference detached from design.
