# Embodied Intelligence

Embodied intelligence is the view that intelligent action depends on having a body that perceives and acts within a material environment. Knowledge is not exhausted by stored propositions or formal rules; it also develops through situated coping, movement, and practical engagement.

This view challenges accounts of intelligence as abstract symbol processing alone. A machine can manipulate representations without sharing the bodily needs, vulnerabilities, and practical situations that give human concepts relevance. Embodiment does not prove that intelligent machines are impossible, but it changes the design question from reproducing detached reasoning to coordinating perception, action, environment, and learned skill.

Embodiment admits degrees and substitutes. A robot can learn through its own sensors and actions, an emulated mind can inhabit a virtual body, and a disembodied learner can approximate grounding through [[Vicarious Embodiment]]. The relevant question is how concepts become connected to patterns of possible perception and action, not whether the system has a human-shaped shell.
