# Practical Quantum Advantage in Generative Modeling

Practical quantum advantage means that a quantum system performs a scientifically, industrially, or socially useful task faster or better than available classical systems under the conditions of the intended application. In generative modeling, “better” can mean producing novel, valid, high-quality samples rather than merely obtaining a formal runtime speedup.

This differs from computational quantum advantage, which emphasizes a complexity-theoretic or experimental separation on a computational task that may not itself be useful. A practical claim must specify the real problem instance, data availability, training and sampling resources, classical competitors, and application-relevant success metrics through [[Generative Model Advantage Race Design]].

The paper does not claim a completed practical advantage. Its simulated QCBM result supports a candidate setting for potential advantage on a synthetic 20-bit task, while hardware execution, realistic data, training cost, stronger classical baselines, and scaling remain open.
