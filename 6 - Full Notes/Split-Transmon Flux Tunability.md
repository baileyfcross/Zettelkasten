# Split-Transmon Flux Tunability

A split transmon replaces one Josephson junction with two junctions in a superconducting loop. The pair forms a dc SQUID whose effective Josephson energy depends on the magnetic flux through the loop, making the qubit transition frequency tunable in situ.

Flux control lets a processor bring qubits into and out of resonance, steer through avoided crossings, and activate gates such as [[iSWAP Gate in Tunable Transmons]] and [[CPHASE Gate through Noncomputational Levels]]. Operating at a maximum or minimum of the frequency-versus-flux curve creates a sweet spot where the first derivative vanishes and low-frequency flux fluctuations have less effect.

Tunability also creates a new noise channel: away from a sweet spot, flux variations directly modulate the transition frequency and cause dephasing. A symmetric SQUID offers a wide tuning range but can become highly flux-sensitive. [[Asymmetric Transmon Flux-Noise Tradeoff]] reduces that sensitivity by deliberately limiting the range.
