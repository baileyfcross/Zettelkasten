# Modular Decomposition

Modular decomposition divides a system into units that own related data definitions and operations behind interfaces. The criterion for a boundary is a design decision or source of change that should be concealed from the rest of the program, not merely the order in which steps execute.

The book's keyword-in-context comparison shows the difference. A top-down solution organizes input, shifting, sorting, and output as a sequence, whereas the modular solution assigns line representation, keyword-line relationships, indexing, shifting, and formatting to modules that can change more independently.
