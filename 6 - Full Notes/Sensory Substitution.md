# Sensory Substitution

Sensory substitution translates information normally acquired through an impaired sensory system into stimulation for another functioning modality. Touch can substitute for aspects of vision, hearing, or vestibular feedback because the skin can represent both spatial patterns and temporal sequences.

Success depends on more than converting a signal. The mapping must preserve information that touch can resolve, the display must fit the target body location, and users often need extensive training. Natural spatial mappings tend to be easier than arbitrary codes; with practice, [[Distal Attribution]] may make a tactile pattern feel like a property of an external object rather than a stimulus on the skin.
