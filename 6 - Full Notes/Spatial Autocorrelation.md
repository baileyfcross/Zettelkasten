# Spatial Autocorrelation

Spatial autocorrelation is the tendency for observations at nearby locations to have related values. Temperature, rainfall, soil properties, and traffic measurements often change smoothly across space, so samples close together do not provide independent repetitions of the same experiment.

This dependence violates the independence assumption of many classical statistical methods. Spatial analysis must model or test the neighborhood relationship instead of treating proximity as irrelevant; otherwise confidence may be overstated and a smooth regional pattern may be mistaken for many separate pieces of evidence.
