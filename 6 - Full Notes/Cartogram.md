# Cartogram

A cartogram resizes geographic areas according to an attribute such as population or electoral votes rather than preserving their physical area. It can correct the visual dominance of large, sparsely populated regions in a conventional map by making display size correspond to the quantity being compared.

That benefit comes at the cost of recognizable geography. Extensive distortion can make locations and shapes difficult to identify, so a cartogram is often paired with an undistorted [[Reference Map]]. The two views separate the thematic comparison from the reader's need to orient the transformed regions.
