# Computing as Phenomena Surrounding Computers

An early justification for computer science held that wherever there are rich phenomena, there can be a science that describes and explains them. Computers and the systems around them produced new empirical behavior worthy of study.

This view answered the objection that a human-made artifact could not support a science. Time-sharing systems, for example, had to be built and observed before reliable theories of their performance could emerge.
