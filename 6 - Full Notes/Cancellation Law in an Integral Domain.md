# Cancellation Law in an Integral Domain

In an integral domain, ac = bc with nonzero c implies a = b. Subtracting gives (a-b)c = 0, and the absence of zero divisors forces a-b = 0.

Cancellation fails in general rings when the cancelled factor is a zero divisor.
