# Two-Plus-One-Dimensional QED Quantum Benchmark

Compact quantum electrodynamics in two spatial dimensions is a useful intermediate benchmark because it is simpler than quantum chromodynamics yet still displays asymptotic freedom and confinement. Its Hamiltonian formulation can also address real-time dynamics, finite fermion density, and topological terms that are difficult for Euclidean Monte Carlo because of autocorrelation or the sign problem.

The benchmark retains both matter and gauge degrees of freedom. Staggered Kogut-Susskind fermions place particles and antiparticles on alternating lattice sites, while electric fields and link operators occupy lattice edges. A finite quantum register requires discretizing and truncating the gauge-field spectrum; Wilson fermions provide an alternative discretization that may reduce the qubits needed for gauge fields.

The physical model therefore tests several coupled design choices: fermion mapping, gauge-field encoding, truncation accuracy, gauge invariance, and the algorithm used for static or dynamical observables. Ground-state work can use variational eigensolvers or imaginary-time methods, while dynamics can use product formulas or variational evolution.
