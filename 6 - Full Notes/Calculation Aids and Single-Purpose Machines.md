# Calculation Aids and Single-Purpose Machines

Early calculation aids automated only a narrow operation. The slide rule multiplied through logarithmic scales but could not add, while mechanical calculators gradually mastered arithmetic without organizing long chains of steps.

Their limits clarify what later made general-purpose computers distinctive: a stored or replaceable program could coordinate many operations and change the function of the same underlying machine.
