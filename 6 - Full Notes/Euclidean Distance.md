# Euclidean Distance

Euclidean distance is the square root of the sum of squared coordinate differences between two observations. It corresponds to straight-line distance and produces circular or spherical neighborhoods in ordinary coordinate space.

The measure is sensitive to attribute scale and tends to support compact, globular clusters. Standardization or weighting may be necessary before it expresses the intended resemblance among cases.

The source extends the familiar two-dimensional formula to gene-expression samples with thousands of coordinates. The same square-root sum of squared feature differences defines pairwise sample distance once each column is interpreted as a point in high-dimensional space.
