# Field Research

Field research studies naturally occurring activity in its ordinary setting. It can reveal how information systems are interpreted, adapted, and embedded in organizational routines that a laboratory or questionnaire would separate from their context.

Observation may be participant or nonparticipant, open or structured, continuous or sampled. Evidence can include field notes, journals, photographs, video, time diaries, event coding, documents, and instantaneous observations. The field journal is the primary contemporaneous record linking what occurred to later interpretation.

The method is vulnerable to selective attention, recording errors, interpretive drift, reactivity, and unrepresentative times or locations. Explicit sampling, coding, reflexive notes, and triangulation make those limitations visible. Field studies are often strongest for exploratory and descriptive questions, although careful comparisons can support explanatory claims.
