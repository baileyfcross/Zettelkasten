# IoT Deskilling and Social Dependence

IoT deskilling and social dependence occur when connected systems perform navigation, memory, monitoring, calculation, household work, or judgment so consistently that people stop practicing the underlying abilities. The immediate task becomes easier, but the person can become less capable when the service is unavailable or wrong.

Dependence is not proof that automation is harmful; tools also free attention and extend human capability. The risk appears when users cannot recognize an error, override the system, or function during an outage. Design should preserve understandable controls, opportunities to verify results, and fallback knowledge instead of making convenience depend on an opaque and permanently available infrastructure.
