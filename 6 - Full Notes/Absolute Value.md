# Absolute Value

The absolute value of a real number is its nonnegative distance from zero on the [[Number Line]]. It is defined by

$$|a|=\begin{cases}a,&a\geq0,\\-a,&a<0.\end{cases}$$

Thus (|5|=5) and (|-5|=5). Absolute value removes direction while preserving magnitude. The piecewise definition also explains why a variable's written sign is not enough to determine its value: (|x|) equals (x) only when (x) is nonnegative and equals its [[Opposite Number]] otherwise.
