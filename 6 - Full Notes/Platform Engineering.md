# Platform Engineering

Platform engineering builds reusable internal capabilities that let product teams deliver and operate software through supported workflows. It treats the platform as a product: the team studies developer needs, defines interfaces, measures adoption and outcomes, and evolves a paved path rather than assembling one-off infrastructure for every project.

An [[Internal Developer Platform]] may combine provisioning, delivery, observability, security, and documentation behind self-service interfaces. The platform should reduce cognitive load without hiding every operational constraint. Its success is measured by safer, faster delivery and a better developer experience, not merely by the number of tools centralized.

The book organizes platform responsibility across construction, services, operations, assurance, and the platform team. This makes the platform an operating capability as well as a tool surface: it must be planned, built, secured, measured, supported, and improved throughout its lifecycle.

Platform engineering is also sociotechnical. Every technical choice changes how people collaborate, what they must understand, and where work queues form. The platform team therefore combines architecture with product discovery, open contribution, and outcome measurement rather than optimizing a technology stack in isolation.
