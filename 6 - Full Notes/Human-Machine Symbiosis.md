# Human-Machine Symbiosis

Human-machine symbiosis envisions computers as partners that extend human cognition rather than replacements that automate people away. J. C. R. Licklider promoted interactive systems in which machines handle storage and calculation while people guide goals and interpretation.

The idea requires rapid feedback and direct access. It motivated time-sharing, graphical interaction, and networking research that moved computing beyond isolated batch jobs.

Robotics carries symbiosis into physical capability. A person and machine can divide sensing, judgment, force, precision, or endurance so the partnership performs work neither could accomplish as well alone. Surgical systems, powered assistance, shared-control vehicles, and remote robots differ in where authority lies, but each should make the human role and the transfer of control explicit.
