# Remote Sensing Temporal Resolution

Remote-sensing temporal resolution describes how often an instrument revisits and records the same area. Frequent observations can reveal rapid change, while a long revisit interval may miss short events or delay a response. The practical measure depends on the platform's orbit or route, the sensor's operating schedule, and whether weather or other interference makes a pass usable.

Temporal resolution matters because remote sensing usually gains meaning through comparison over time. Crop stress, storm damage, urban growth, and melting ice are not single images but changes whose timing must be sampled often enough for the intended decision.
