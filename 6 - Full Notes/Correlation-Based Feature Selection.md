# Correlation-Based Feature Selection

Correlation-based feature selection scores candidate inputs by how strongly they relate to the target or model performance. Pearson correlation can measure linear association, while ablation can reveal the loss caused by dropping one feature.

A strong correlation identifies predictive association rather than a causal mechanism. Features should also be checked for overlap because combinations of user and social signals may outperform either family alone.

More generally, correlation-based selection examines statistical dependencies among variables and with a target. It can identify irrelevant and redundant dimensions efficiently, but pairwise association may miss conditional or nonlinear usefulness.
