# Authentication Factor Categories

Authentication evidence can be grouped into something a claimant knows, something the claimant has, and something the claimant is. Passwords represent knowledge, tokens or registered devices represent possession, and biometrics represent physical characteristics.

Combining independent categories through [[Multi-Factor Authentication]] makes one stolen credential less decisive. Several steps from the same category do not provide the same diversity of evidence as factors based on different relationships to the claimant.

Location and behavioral patterns can supplement these categories as contextual evidence. An unusual place, device interaction, or behavior may trigger a stronger challenge, but such observations can be noisy and privacy-sensitive. They should change the confidence or required assurance of a flow rather than be assumed to provide the same independence as a separate knowledge, possession, or biometric factor.
