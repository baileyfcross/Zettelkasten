# Linear Programming Assumptions

A linear program assumes that its coefficients are known and fixed for the decision being modeled. Its relationships also satisfy proportionality and additivity: doubling an activity doubles its separate contribution, and the total effect is the sum of the activities rather than an interaction between them.

The divisibility assumption permits fractional activity levels, while sign restrictions usually require nonnegative decisions. When certainty is doubtful, [[Linear Programming Sensitivity Analysis]] tests parameter changes; when indivisible choices are essential, an ordinary continuous linear program may only approximate the real problem.
