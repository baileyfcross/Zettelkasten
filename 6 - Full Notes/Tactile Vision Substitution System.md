# Tactile Vision Substitution System

A tactile vision substitution system converts camera images into spatial patterns delivered to the skin or tongue. Early systems used large arrays of vibrating motors on the back, while later devices used compact cameras and electrotactile stimulation to convey object location, shape, orientation, or contrast.

The tactile channel has much lower spatial and information capacity than vision, so image preprocessing and training are essential. A direct pixel-for-pixel transfer cannot preserve every visual detail. With extensive practice, users may develop [[Distal Attribution]], experiencing the pattern as an object located in front of them rather than as stimulation at the display site.
