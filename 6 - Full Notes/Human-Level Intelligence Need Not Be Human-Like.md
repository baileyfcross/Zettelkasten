# Human-Level Intelligence Need Not Be Human-Like

Human-level intelligence describes breadth or performance, whereas human-like intelligence describes architecture, motives, experience, or behavior modeled on people. A system can match humans across many intellectual tasks without reproducing human psychology.

This distinction prevents a performance threshold from becoming an assumption about inner life. An engineered agent may have unusual strengths, distributed computation, unfamiliar goals, and no ordinary body even when its language is persuasive. Treating fluency as proof of human-like thought encourages anthropomorphism and obscures the design features that actually govern behavior.
