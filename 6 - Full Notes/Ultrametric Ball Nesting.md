# Ultrametric Ball Nesting

In an ultrametric space, two balls are either disjoint or one is contained in the other; they cannot overlap only partly. Every point of a ball can serve as its center, and balls are both open and closed in the associated topology.

These unusual properties explain the tree picture. At a chosen distance threshold, a ball acts like a cluster node; raising the threshold encloses it in a larger node, while unrelated nodes stay apart. The resulting order is by set inclusion along a branch, not a total order across all branches.
