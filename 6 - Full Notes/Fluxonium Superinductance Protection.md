# Fluxonium Superinductance Protection

Fluxonium places a small Josephson junction in a loop with a very large inductance, usually implemented as an array of larger junctions. This superinductance reshapes the phase potential and produces spectra that differ substantially from the weakly anharmonic transmon.

The circuit can combine a low transition frequency with large anharmonicity and reduced sensitivity to some loss channels. Its localized wavefunctions and selection rules can suppress particular matrix elements, helping protect a transition while leaving other transitions available for control and readout.

Protection is not free. The junction array increases fabrication and modeling complexity, and the transition frequencies and matrix elements vary strongly with flux bias. Fluxonium illustrates the broader principle of Hamiltonian engineering: coherence, addressability, and controllability are jointly determined by circuit topology rather than optimized independently.
