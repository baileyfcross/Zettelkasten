# Constructive Solid Geometry

Constructive solid geometry builds a complex object by positioning simple solid primitives such as blocks, cylinders, cones, tori, wedges, and spheres, then combining them with Boolean union, difference, and intersection operations.

The construction can be recorded as an expression tree whose leaves are transformed primitive instances and whose internal nodes are Boolean operations. The representation is procedural and generally non-unique: different operation orders or trees can describe the same final shape. This contrasts with a [[Boundary Representation Model]], which stores the faces, edges, and vertices enclosing the completed solid.
