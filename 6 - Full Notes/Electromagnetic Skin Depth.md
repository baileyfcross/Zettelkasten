# Electromagnetic Skin Depth

Electromagnetic skin depth is the distance into a material at which the amplitude of a penetrating sinusoidal electromagnetic field falls to $1/e$ of its surface value. It converts the attenuation part of a [[Complex Propagation Constant]] into a characteristic penetration length.

If the field varies as $e^{-\alpha z}$, then the skin depth is $\delta=1/\alpha$. For a good conductor, the book simplifies the attenuation constant to $\alpha=\sqrt{\omega\mu\sigma/2}$, so increasing angular frequency, permeability, or conductivity reduces the depth over which the field remains appreciable.

Skin depth explains why a microwave wall can often be represented through a surface relation rather than by resolving a large solid volume. The selected approximation must still retain the finite-conductivity loss relevant to the device.
