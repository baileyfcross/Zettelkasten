# Working Backward

Working backward solves a problem by starting from a known final result and undoing earlier operations in reverse order. If a number was tripled and then increased by sixteen to obtain thirty-seven, subtract sixteen first and then divide by three.

The method depends on inverse operations and is the informal foundation of [[Isolating a Variable]]. It is especially useful when a sequence of transformations is known but the starting value is not. Each reversed step must use the correct inverse and account for operations that may not be uniquely reversible.
