# Hyperbolic Rotation

A hyperbolic rotation is a linear transformation that preserves a difference of squares rather than the Euclidean sum of squares. In a time-space plane, its matrix contains $\cosh\chi$ and $\sinh\chi$ and leaves $c^2t^2-x^2$ unchanged.

This is the geometric form of a [[Lorentz Boost]]. A Euclidean rotation moves a point around a circle, while a hyperbolic rotation moves it along a branch of a unit hyperbola. The hyperbola's asymptotes correspond to the light-speed boundary, which no finite rapidity crosses.

The projections of the transformed vector explain several familiar effects: $\cosh\chi=\gamma$ supplies the factor in [[Time Dilation]], and the relation $\tanh\chi=v/c$ connects the hyperbolic angle to measured velocity.
