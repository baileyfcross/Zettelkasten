# Problem-Specific Quantum Advantage

Quantum computers can reproduce classical computations, but known speedups apply to particular problem structures and access models. The existence of an exponential state space does not imply that every algorithm becomes faster, because state preparation, transformation, and measurement constrain which information can be used.

Provable advantage is known for only a limited collection of algorithms, while the best classical method is unknown for many practical problems. Claims outside those cases require empirical comparison against strong evolving classical heuristics, including the cost of quantum data loading, repetitions, compilation, and classical control.
