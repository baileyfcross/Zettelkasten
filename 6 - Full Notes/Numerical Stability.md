# Numerical Stability

Numerical stability describes whether an algorithm prevents small representation and arithmetic errors from growing unnecessarily during a calculation. A stable formulation produces the result for the exact problem or for a nearby problem despite finite precision.

Stability belongs to the algorithm, while conditioning belongs to the underlying problem. Even a stable method cannot make an ill-conditioned problem insensitive, but a poor method can lose accuracy on a well-conditioned problem through [[Catastrophic Cancellation]] or repeated [[Error Propagation]].
