# Genome-Wide Association Study

A genome-wide association study tests genetic variants across the genome for statistical association with a trait or disease. It can discover regions without requiring a single prespecified candidate gene.

The large number of tests demands stringent significance control, and population structure can create spurious associations if ancestry is related to both genotype and outcome. An associated variant may tag a nearby causal mechanism rather than cause the phenotype itself. Replication and functional investigation are therefore essential steps after discovery.
