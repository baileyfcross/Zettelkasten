# Split-Attention Effect

The split-attention effect is the added cognitive burden of mentally integrating related information presented in separate places. Looking back and forth between an interactive object and distant instructions can consume capacity needed to understand the task itself.

Instructions are easier to use when they appear beside or on the relevant object at the moment of action. Integration does not mean showing more information; it means placing the minimum necessary explanation where the player is already directing [[Attention]].
