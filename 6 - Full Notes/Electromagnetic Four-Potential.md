# Electromagnetic Four-Potential

The electromagnetic four-potential combines the scalar electric potential $\phi$ and the magnetic [[Vector Potential]] $\mathbf{A}$ into one covariant spacetime object. Its exact component signs and factors of $c$ depend on the convention, but the essential structure is a temporal scalar component joined to three spatial components.

Taking antisymmetrized spacetime derivatives of the four-potential produces the [[Electromagnetic Field Tensor]]. In ordinary three-dimensional terms, the same derivatives give $\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ and an electric field containing both $-\nabla\phi$ and a time derivative of $\mathbf{A}$.

The potential is not unique. Adding an appropriate gradient changes its representation without changing the fields, which is [[Gauge Invariance in Electromagnetism]]. The [[Aharonov-Bohm Effect]] shows that the potential's phase relations can have observable consequences even where the local fields along a path vanish.
