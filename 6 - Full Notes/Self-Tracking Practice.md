# Self-Tracking Practice

Self-tracking is the practice of recording, analyzing, or reflecting on information about oneself. It is defined by what a person does with a record rather than by a particular device: a paper diary, a spreadsheet, a phone, and a wearable sensor can all support it.

The practice can serve different purposes. A person may compare behavior with a goal, investigate a symptom, cultivate a habit, sharpen awareness of a sensation, or use personal data as material for art. These purposes call for different measures and standards of evidence. [[Qualitative Self-Tracking]] can be more useful than a precise count when the relevant experience cannot be reduced to a reliable number.

Self-tracking also has a social setting. Categories are designed by organizations, meanings are discussed in a [[Self-Tracking Community of Practice]], and records may affect relatives, employers, clinicians, or companies. It should therefore be understood as both a personal practice and a way of participating in institutional systems of data collection.
