# Planetary Flyby

A planetary flyby sends a spacecraft past a target without entering orbit or landing. Instruments collect observations during a limited encounter, while the trajectory may use the target's gravity to redirect and change the spacecraft's path.

Flybys reduce the propulsion needed for arrival and made early exploration of Venus, Mars, and the outer planets feasible. Their weakness is short observation time and dependence on precise navigation after months or years of travel. The Voyager missions showed how carefully arranged gravity encounters could turn a single launch into a tour of several planets.
