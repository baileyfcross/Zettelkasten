# Strengthening Existing Steel Structures

Strengthening begins by establishing the existing structure's geometry, material, connections, condition, load history, and actual load path. Field measurement, document review, testing, and inspection can reveal corrosion loss, altered framing, unknown steel grade, fatigue-sensitive details, and construction that differs from drawings. Capacity cannot be increased responsibly from an assumed modern material specification.

Rehabilitation may add plates, tees, angles, cover plates, stiffeners, braces, or entirely new supports and transfer members. Work sequence matters because an existing member is already stressed; shoring or load removal may be needed before reinforcement can share force. Welding requires confirmation of weldability and fire safety, while bolts need access and net-section checks. New stiffness can also attract load and change system behavior beyond the local repair.
