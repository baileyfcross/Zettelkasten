# High-Dimensional Volume Concentration

High-dimensional volume behaves unlike low-dimensional geometric intuition. For a fixed radius, the volume of a Euclidean ball eventually decreases more than exponentially with dimension, so covering a unit hypercube with fixed-radius balls requires an enormous number of centers.

Volume also concentrates near the boundary. In a $p$-dimensional ball, the outer one-percent shell occupies the fraction $1-0.99^p$, which rapidly approaches one. These effects help explain why local averaging and neighborhood methods need unrealistic sample sizes when observations fill a high-dimensional space rather than a [[Low-Dimensional Statistical Structure]].
