# Hollerith Tabulating Machines

Herman Hollerith encoded census records as patterns of holes in punched cards and built machines that selected and counted cards by recorded characteristics. The system enabled the 1890 United States census to process tens of millions of records far faster than manual tabulation.

Hollerith’s design treated symbols as data rather than merely as numbers for arithmetic. It helped establish large-scale information processing as a central use of computing machinery.
