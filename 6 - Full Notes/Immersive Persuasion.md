# Immersive Persuasion

Immersive persuasion uses control over a person's surrounding virtual environment to influence attention, judgment, or behavior. Unlike a conventional advertisement confined to part of a page, a virtual experience can coordinate space, avatars, gaze, sound, and interaction while measuring how the user responds.

Those measurements can create a feedback loop. Eye and head movement, approach and avoidance, dwell time, and chosen paths reveal what attracts attention; the environment can then adapt its message in real time. The same mechanism can personalize useful guidance or intensify commercial and political manipulation.

The ethical risk comes from combining [[Presence]] with behavioral inference. A participant may consciously evaluate the visible claim while remaining unaware that involuntary reactions are being collected and used to alter the experience. Transparent intent, limits on profiling, and meaningful control over behavioral data are therefore part of interface design, not merely backend policy.
