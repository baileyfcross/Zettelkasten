# Steel Bolt Hole Types and Spacing

Structural bolts may occupy standard, oversized, short-slotted, or long-slotted holes. Larger or slotted holes ease erection and accommodate adjustment, but they can alter permitted joint type, slip behavior, washer requirements, and design resistance. Slot direction relative to force matters because movement parallel to a slot is different from movement across it.

Minimum bolt spacing provides room for installation and prevents overlapping bearing or tearout zones. Minimum edge distance protects the material between a hole and a plate edge, while maximum spacing and edge distance keep connected plies in contact and limit corrosion or buckling between fasteners. Strength calculations use the specified effective hole dimension, including the required allowance beyond nominal bolt size, rather than assuming the bolt diameter equals removed material.
