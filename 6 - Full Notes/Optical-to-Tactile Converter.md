# Optical-to-Tactile Converter

An optical-to-tactile converter captures printed material with a handheld camera and presents the resulting letter shape as a moving vibrotactile pattern on a fingertip array. The Optacon implemented this approach with a dense pin matrix, giving blind readers direct access to print and simple graphics.

The system required one hand to guide the camera and the other to interpret the pattern, and reading was slower than [[Braille Tactile Reading]]. Its historical importance lies in converting arbitrary printed shapes without first recognizing the characters, before optical character recognition and speech synthesis became cheaper and easier to use.
