# Additive XEB and Multiplicative Fidelity

For two independent subsystems, their linear-XEB correlations combine as $\chi_{12}+1=(\chi_1+1)(\chi_2+1)$. When both scores are small, the product term is negligible and the total XEB is approximately $\chi_1+\chi_2$. Fidelity instead multiplies directly, $F_{12}=F_1F_2$, so joining imperfect subsystems drives global fidelity downward.

This mismatch permits a larger disconnected system to receive a higher XEB even while its full-state fidelity decays exponentially. The effect can persist after each subsystem's output statistics look Porter-Thomas-like, so chaotic-looking marginal statistics do not repair the scaling mismatch. [[Circuit-Partitioning XEB Spoofing]] turns this mathematical property into an efficient classical strategy.
