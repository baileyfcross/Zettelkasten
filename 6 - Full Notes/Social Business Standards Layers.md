# Social Business Standards Layers

Social-business standardization can be divided into foundational interoperability and higher-order business behavior. The source places identity, contacts, activity or messaging, integration, and security in the foundational layer. Higher-order standards would describe engagement, behavior, metrics, and domain-specific operations that build on those primitives.

This distinction explains why a provider binding may be portable without being semantically rich. A library can authenticate, read a profile, and publish a message yet still lack common concepts for a campaign, moderation workflow, or organizational relationship. [[OpenSocial Gadget Standard]] and [[OStatus Protocol Suite]] mostly address basic exchange, while the rejected Java social standard discussed by the source aimed at a higher-order API inspired by social events and provider-neutral bindings.
