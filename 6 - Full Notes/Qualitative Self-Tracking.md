# Qualitative Self-Tracking

Qualitative self-tracking records qualities, descriptions, or subjective judgments instead of relying only on numerical measurements. A meal can be labeled healthful, neutral, or indulgent; a mood can be described as pensive or angry; a symptom can be described in the person's own words.

This approach is useful when numerical precision is costly or falsely reassuring. Exact calorie estimates may be less practical than photographs that preserve portion and visual context, while a nuanced mood vocabulary may reveal distinctions that a one-to-ten score erases. The measure should fit the project's purpose rather than imitate laboratory data without its controls.

Qualitative records can also complement sensors. Comparing descriptions with automatic measurements helps retain circumstances that would otherwise be lost and supports [[Data-Elicited Sensation]].
