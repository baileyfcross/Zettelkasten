# Light Cone

The light cone through an event is the set of directions that light can take from or to that event. In a spacetime diagram it is formed by the lightlike lines for which the [[Invariant Spacetime Interval]] is zero, separating timelike directions inside the cone from spacelike directions outside it.

The future cone contains events the original event can influence at or below light speed; the past cone contains events that could have influenced it. Events outside both cones are spacelike separated and cannot exchange a causal signal without exceeding the limiting speed.

A [[Lorentz Transformation]] changes the coordinate axes but leaves the cone itself fixed. That invariance is the geometric reason every inertial observer agrees on the causal classification of an event pair even while disagreeing about its spatial and temporal components.
