# Local Buckling in Steel Compression Members

Local buckling occurs when a thin plate element within a compression member deforms out of plane before the entire member reaches its global buckling or yielding capacity. Flanges and webs are classified through width-to-thickness ratios that depend on support along their edges, material yield stress, and whether the element is stiffened or unstiffened.

A section with slender elements cannot use the full gross-area compression strength without reduction. Local buckling may interact with flexural, torsional, or flexural-torsional member buckling, so design distinguishes the cross-section's plate behavior from the member's overall slenderness. Increasing thickness, reducing outstanding width, adding stiffeners, or choosing a different shape can postpone the local mode and allow more of the section to participate.
