# Velocity and Acceleration Unit Conversion

Velocity has length per time, while acceleration has length per time squared. The book's examples convert ft/s to m/s and ft/s² to m/s² with the same $0.3048\ \mathrm{m/ft}$ factor because seconds remain unchanged in both cases. If the time unit also changed, the denominator would have to be converted once for velocity but twice for acceleration. The unit expression, not the quantity's label alone, determines the factor chain. See [[Compound Rate Unit Conversion]] and [[Dimensional Homogeneity in Equations]].
