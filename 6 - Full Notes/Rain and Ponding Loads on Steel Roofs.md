# Rain and Ponding Loads on Steel Roofs

Rain load develops when water accumulates because primary drainage is blocked or cannot discharge fast enough. Its pressure follows the hydraulic head above the roof surface, including water needed to reach secondary drainage. Roof slope, drain elevation, scupper geometry, rainfall intensity, and deflection all influence the load delivered to steel framing.

Ponding is an instability-like feedback: water causes deflection, the deflection creates a deeper basin, and the deeper basin attracts more water. Flexible beams, girders, and deck can therefore experience loads greater than a simple fixed-depth estimate. Adequate slope, primary and secondary drainage, camber or stiffness, and an explicit ponding check interrupt this feedback before strength or serviceability is lost.
