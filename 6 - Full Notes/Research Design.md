# Research Design

Research design is the plan, structure, and strategy that connects a question to observations and defensible conclusions. It specifies what will be observed, which variables will be measured or manipulated, how cases will be selected, what comparisons will be made, and how the evidence will be analyzed.

The design should maximize the information relevant to the question while controlling experimental, extraneous, and error variation. Control may come from holding a factor constant, random assignment, matching, blocking, repeated measurement, or statistical adjustment. Each mechanism changes the assumptions and the population to which the result can generalize.

Design precedes calculation. The intended analysis, possible alternative explanations, missing-data risks, and conclusions that each outcome could support should be considered before data collection, not reconstructed after seeing the results.
