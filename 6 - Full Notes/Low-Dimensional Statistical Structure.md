# Low-Dimensional Statistical Structure

A low-dimensional statistical structure is a restricted pattern—such as sparsity, grouped sparsity, smoothness, or low rank—around which high-dimensional observations or parameters concentrate. It reduces the effective complexity of a problem even when its ambient representation contains thousands of coordinates.

High-dimensional estimation depends on discovering or exploiting such structure because unstructured data generally require prohibitive sample sizes. The useful structure need not reproduce every feature of the data; it must be strong enough to support the intended estimate or prediction. [[Estimation-Oriented Dimension Reduction]] makes this distinction explicit.
