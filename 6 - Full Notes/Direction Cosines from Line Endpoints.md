# Direction Cosines from Line Endpoints

Two distinct endpoints determine a line direction by subtraction. If the difference vector is (A, B, C), its length is the square root of A squared plus B squared plus C squared, and dividing each component by that length yields the line's direction cosines.

These normalized components describe the angles between the line and the coordinate axes without depending on where the line is located. They can therefore be used to construct the rotations that align an arbitrary line with a principal axis. Translation handles the line's position; direction cosines handle its orientation.
