# Alternative Clustering

Alternative clustering searches for a valid partition that is meaningfully different from a previously known or dominant clustering. The goal is not merely to perturb assignments but to reveal another coherent organization of the same observations.

Difference can be imposed on memberships, feature directions, pairwise relationships, or information shared with the reference partition. A useful alternative must satisfy both novelty and quality; maximizing disagreement alone produces arbitrary groups.
