# Automation Returns to Capital

When automation raises productivity, the resulting gains do not automatically become higher wages. Owners of robots, software, facilities, and intellectual property can capture a larger share of the return while workers face stagnant pay, displacement, or weaker bargaining power.

This distributional effect is distinct from whether total employment rises or falls. A society can produce more while widening inequality if ownership is concentrated. Evaluating robotics economically therefore requires asking who finances the system, who owns it, who bears transition costs, and how the value created by combined human and machine work is allocated.
