# Haptic Viscosity Perception

Haptic viscosity perception judges resistance whose physical magnitude relates force to movement velocity. It is used when mixing, kneading, or moving a tool through fluid and becomes important when a teleoperated robot works inside the body or underwater.

The observer must integrate applied force with limb motion, making changes in viscosity harder to discriminate than comparable changes in force alone. A remote haptic system must therefore reproduce both the movement-dependent resistance and its timing; force without the associated velocity relationship does not convey the same material property.
