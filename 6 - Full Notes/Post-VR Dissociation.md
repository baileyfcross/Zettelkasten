# Post-VR Dissociation

Post-VR dissociation is a lingering sense of detachment, unreality, or reduced presence in the physical world after leaving an immersive environment. A vivid virtual session can make ordinary colors, sensations, or surroundings feel muted, and conflicting sensory patterns can temporarily disturb the person's usual sense of body and environment.

This psychological aftereffect differs from the balance and motion symptoms of a [[Simulator Aftereffect]], although the two can overlap. Susceptibility and intensity vary, and people who already tend toward dissociation or deep absorption may experience stronger effects.

The possibility argues for gradual exposure, breaks, recovery time, and a way to stop before distress becomes severe. Long-term effects were still uncertain in the source's 2019 account, so designers should treat absence of immediate discomfort as incomplete evidence of safety.
