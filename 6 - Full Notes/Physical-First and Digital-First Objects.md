# Physical-First and Digital-First Objects

Physical-first objects do not naturally produce digital data: a shoe, animal, bridge, or printed book must be augmented with a tag, chip, or sensor before a computer can observe it. Digital-first objects such as photos, videos, and ebooks are already encoded and can be copied, transmitted, and inspected through software.

The distinction affects what can be known. An RFID tag on a printed book can reveal its identity or location, while an ebook can reveal detailed patterns of navigation and use. Connecting a physical object therefore does not automatically create the same depth of information as a natively digital object; the available insight depends on what the added sensing system can actually measure.
