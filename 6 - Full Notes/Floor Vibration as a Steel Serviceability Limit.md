# Floor Vibration as a Steel Serviceability Limit

A steel floor can satisfy strength and static deflection limits yet feel unacceptable under walking, dancing, running, exercise, or machinery. Vibration is a serviceability problem because occupant comfort and the operation of sensitive equipment depend on acceleration, frequency, duration, and damping rather than only on peak displacement.

Steady-state vibration comes from continuous harmonic sources and is often addressed by isolating equipment. Transient vibration from human activity decays as damping dissipates energy, but resonance can make the response large when a forcing frequency or harmonic approaches a floor's natural frequency. Long spans, light framing, open-web joists, and low damping increase susceptibility, so evaluation should occur during design rather than after finishes and occupancy make remediation costly.
