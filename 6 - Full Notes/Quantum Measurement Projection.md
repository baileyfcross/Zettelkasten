# Quantum Measurement Projection

Projective measurement decomposes a state relative to orthogonal outcome subspaces. Once an outcome is observed, the state is replaced by its normalized component in the corresponding subspace.

This state change explains why measurement cannot be treated as a passive inspection of all amplitudes. In the polarization example, a photon that passes a horizontal filter leaves horizontally polarized; in [[Computational-Basis Qubit Measurement]], observing $0$ leaves the qubit in $|0\rangle$. Measurement both produces classical data and prepares a new quantum state.
