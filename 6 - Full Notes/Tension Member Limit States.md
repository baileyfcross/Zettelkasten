# Tension Member Limit States

A steel tension member can reach strength through gross-section yielding, effective-net-section rupture, block shear near a connection, or connection failure. Gross yielding spreads ductile deformation across the member's full area, while rupture is a more sudden failure through the reduced section at holes or other discontinuities. Design strength is the lowest resistance among applicable modes.

The connection determines much of the member behavior. Bolt holes reduce net area, staggered holes change the critical fracture path, and connecting only part of a shape produces nonuniform stress called shear lag. Slenderness may also be limited to control handling and vibration even though elastic buckling is not a tension failure. A tension-member design is therefore inseparable from its end detail and the path by which force enters the section.
