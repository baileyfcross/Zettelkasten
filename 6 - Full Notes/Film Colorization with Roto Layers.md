# Film Colorization with Roto Layers

Colorizing a monochrome photograph can be done with separate roto-limited color layers for sky, ground, clothing, skin, and other materials. Reference images help choose plausible colors; masks should overlap or subtract correctly as foreground and background parts meet. Blend modes and lower opacity allow original shading, deterioration, and texture to remain visible rather than replacing the image with flat painted fills. For moving material, articulated shapes need the same tracking discipline as other roto work. See [[Segmenting Moving Roto Shapes]].
