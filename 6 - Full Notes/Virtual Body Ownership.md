# Virtual Body Ownership

Virtual body ownership is the felt attribution of an avatar or displayed body to oneself. A first-person viewpoint, a virtual mirror, and sensory events synchronized with the person's own movements can persuade the brain that a body that looks different from the physical body is nevertheless "mine."

The illusion lets a participant inhabit another age, appearance, social identity, or nonhuman form. It can support perspective-taking, rehabilitation, and new forms of play, but the effect does not guarantee empathy or insight. An abrupt simulation of disability, for example, may center the shock of losing an ability rather than the lived experience it was meant to explain.

Because embodiment can alter later attitudes and self-perception, the represented body and the actions imposed upon it are consequential design choices. The illusion links graphical avatar design to [[Presence]], informed participation, and psychological safety.
