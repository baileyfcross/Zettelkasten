# Psychological Safety

Psychological safety is a shared expectation that people can take interpersonal risks—asking questions, admitting mistakes, challenging assumptions, or reporting bad news—without humiliation or retaliation.

It does not remove accountability or conflict. It makes accurate information available soon enough for a team to inspect problems and improve, instead of rewarding the appearance that everything is on plan.

The current source extends safety into learning, clarity, diversity, and constructive conflict. A team must make room for mistakes during growth, model transparent communication, welcome differences that improve critical thinking, and treat disagreement as information to examine rather than a reason to silence its speaker.
