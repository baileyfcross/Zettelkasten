# Central Limit Theorem

The central limit theorem states that the mean of a sufficiently large random sample is approximately normally distributed around the population mean. Its standard error equals the population standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size.

The approximation concerns the distribution of the sample mean, not a claim that the population itself is normal. Its adequacy depends on sample size and the underlying distribution, so simulation can test its practical accuracy.
