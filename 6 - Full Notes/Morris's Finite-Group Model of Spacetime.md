# Morris's Finite-Group Model of Spacetime

Morris's finite-group model of spacetime is the book's proposed interpretation that the algebraic spaces used for relativity can be generated from the order-two [[Cyclic Group]] $C_2$, its [[Direct Product of Groups|direct product]] $C_2\times C_2$, real numbers, and matrix exponentiation.

In the proposal, exponentiating a matrix representation of $C_2$ yields circular and hyperbolic two-dimensional rotation forms. Structured algebras associated with $C_2\times C_2$ include quaternion and mixed-sign matrix systems; Morris then combines several of the latter to reproduce the signature $t^2-x^2-y^2-z^2$ and compares their commutators with the [[Lorentz Group]]. He also proposes a quaternion-based route to [[Maxwell's Equations]].

This note records an author-specific speculative model, not a standard consequence of special relativity. The experimentally established theory needs Lorentz invariance, Minkowski geometry, and covariant electromagnetism, but it does not require spacetime or electromagnetism to be derived from finite groups in the way Morris proposes.
