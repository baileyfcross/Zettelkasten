# Charge Density Transformation

Charge density is total electric charge divided by volume. Although [[Electric Charge]] itself is invariant between inertial frames, the measured volume of a moving distribution contracts along its direction of motion. A frame in which the distribution moves therefore measures a larger density than its rest-frame density.

For a simple longitudinal volume transformation, the book writes the relation as $\rho=\gamma\rho_0$. The same mixing of density with current appears in the [[Four-Current]], whose temporal and spatial components transform together.

This effect helps explain magnetism relativistically. A current-carrying wire that is electrically neutral in one frame can have unequal positive and negative charge densities in another because the charge carriers and lattice move at different velocities. The resulting electric-field description and the original magnetic-force description are two frame-dependent decompositions of one electromagnetic interaction.
