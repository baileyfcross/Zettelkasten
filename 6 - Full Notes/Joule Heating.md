# Joule Heating

Joule heating is heat produced when current flows through electrical resistance. Its rate is $Q=I^2R$, so raising current increases this loss quickly. Unlike the reversible [[Peltier Effect]] or [[Thomson Effect]], resistance heating does not change into cooling when current direction reverses. In a [[Thermoelectric Generator]], it subtracts from the temperature-difference energy available for useful output.
