# Self-Tracking Community of Practice

A self-tracking community of practice is a set of relationships through which people discuss personal data, exchange methods, interpret results, and establish informal standards for useful projects. It may meet locally, communicate online, or form around a condition, profession, device, or shared curiosity.

The community turns apparently individual knowledge into a social product. Members demonstrate tools, point out confounding circumstances, suggest experiments, and provide vocabulary for experiences that were difficult to name. A peer data coach can help a person identify symptoms, possible triggers, and sources of relief without taking ownership of the person's question.

Communities also shape norms about what counts as good data or acceptable disclosure. Their value therefore comes from both technical advice and the collective negotiation of meaning.
