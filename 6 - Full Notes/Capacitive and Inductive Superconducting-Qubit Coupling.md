# Capacitive and Inductive Superconducting-Qubit Coupling

Superconducting circuits interact through deliberately shared electric or magnetic energy. A coupling capacitor links voltage or charge fluctuations, while mutual inductance or a shared inductive element links current or flux fluctuations. Quantization converts those circuit energies into operators acting on the qubits.

The physical element does not by itself determine the effective gate. After each circuit variable is projected into the qubit basis, its longitudinal and transverse matrix elements determine which terms survive. Device modality, bias point, detuning, and rotating-frame approximations can therefore make similar-looking couplers produce different effective interactions.

Coupling strength must be large enough for fast two-qubit operations but small enough to limit residual interaction, frequency crowding, and unwanted state mixing. Fixed capacitive coupling is simple and underlies gates such as [[iSWAP Gate in Tunable Transmons]] and [[Cross-Resonance Gate]]. Tunable couplers add control hardware but can improve the on/off ratio and reduce idle crosstalk.
