# Sounding Rocket

A sounding rocket carries scientific instruments on a suborbital flight through the upper atmosphere or into near space. It provides minutes of observation at altitudes inaccessible to balloons without the cost and complexity of placing a payload in orbit.

Postwar researchers first adapted captured V-2s, then developed smaller vehicles such as Aerobee and Viking. Instruments could transmit data by radio or be designed to survive impact or recovery. These flights built experience with cosmic rays, solar ultraviolet radiation, the ionosphere, and the harsh mechanical conditions of launch, preparing both scientists and hardware for satellites.
