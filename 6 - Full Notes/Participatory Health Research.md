# Participatory Health Research

Participatory health research involves patients or affected communities in deciding what should be studied, how evidence should be gathered, and how findings should be interpreted or used. Self-tracking tools can make participation practical by preserving everyday observations and enabling distributed contribution.

Meaningful participation is more than enrolling people through an application. Contributors should be able to influence questions, understand the data flow, and receive results or benefits relevant to their community. Their experiential knowledge can expose outcomes or constraints that a professional protocol did not anticipate.

The method still requires attention to consent, representation, clinical validity, and the labor of contribution. A large volunteer dataset is not automatically democratic when the platform alone controls its meaning.
