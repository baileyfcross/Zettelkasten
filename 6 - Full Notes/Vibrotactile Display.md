# Vibrotactile Display

A vibrotactile display uses an actuator to convert electrical energy into oscillatory mechanical displacement of the skin. Electromagnetic and piezoelectric motors can produce cues spanning the frequency range relevant to [[Vibrotactile Sensitivity]], with small inexpensive motors supporting phones, controllers, belts, and garments.

Vibration is effective for alerts because changing stimulation is detected more readily than steady pressure. More complex communication varies location, rhythm, duration, frequency, and amplitude, but physically discriminable changes are not always identifiable in isolation; the code must stay within the user's learned tactile vocabulary.
