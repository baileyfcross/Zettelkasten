# Model Granularity in Statistical Learning

Model granularity describes how broadly one fitted rule is expected to apply. A parametric approach uses one fixed-form global model; a semiparametric approach uses several local models for different regions or kinds of cases; a nonparametric approach relies directly on stored examples and a similarity rule rather than assuming a small fixed structure.

Coarser models are compact and can learn from fewer examples when their assumptions hold. Finer or nonparametric models are more flexible but require enough local data and can be costly at prediction time, as in [[K-Nearest Neighbors]]. The appropriate granularity follows the structure of the task rather than a universal ranking of model families.
