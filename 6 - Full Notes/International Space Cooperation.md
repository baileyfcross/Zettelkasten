# International Space Cooperation

International space cooperation joins nations in financing, building, operating, or using spacecraft and infrastructure. It can distribute cost and expertise, provide partners access to capabilities they could not independently maintain, and create continuing relationships despite wider political tension.

Cooperation also serves strategic purposes. Merging U.S. and Russian station plans helped preserve Russian technical employment and reduce concern that missile specialists would sell expertise elsewhere, while saving an American program near cancellation. The resulting ISS proved multinational systems integration, but unequal funding and leadership meant cooperation did not eliminate national prestige or dependency.
