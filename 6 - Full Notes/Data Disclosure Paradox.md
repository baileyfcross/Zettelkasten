# Data Disclosure Paradox

The data disclosure paradox claims that a prospective buyer cannot value information without seeing it, yet disclosure gives away what the seller hoped to sell. It is often used to argue that data markets need property-like protection.

For large datasets, the problem is weaker than it first appears. Samples, descriptions, contractual safeguards, and the difference between inspecting a portion and obtaining the complete resource can support valuation without transferring the full value.
