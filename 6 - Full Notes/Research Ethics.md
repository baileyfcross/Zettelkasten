# Research Ethics

Research ethics governs how investigators treat participants, evidence, collaborators, clients, and the wider community. Core duties include honesty, fairness, competence, confidentiality, respect for privacy and property, compliance with law and agreements, and avoidance of unnecessary harm.

Ethical choices enter the design before data collection. A researcher must decide what information is necessary, how consent and access will be obtained, who can identify a participant or organization, how records will be secured, and how reporting might expose or misrepresent people. Technical availability of data does not remove these obligations.

Integrity continues through analysis and publication. Selective reporting, concealed conflicts, invented or altered evidence, and claims that exceed the design damage both the immediate study and the shared evidence base on which future research depends.
