# Gauss Backward Interpolation Formula

Gauss's backward formula interpolates equally spaced data for an argument just to the left of a selected central value. It uses a central origin while drawing successive differences from the center and entries immediately below it.

The formula reduces the size of the normalized displacement compared with an endpoint expansion. It is paired with the [[Gauss Forward Interpolation Formula]], and combinations of the two lead to the Stirling and Bessel central formulas.
