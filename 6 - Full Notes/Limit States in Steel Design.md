# Limit States in Steel Design

A limit state is a condition beyond which a member or structural system no longer satisfies an intended requirement. Strength limit states include yielding, rupture, buckling, block shear, bolt failure, and weld failure. Serviceability limit states include excessive deflection, drift, acceleration, or vibration even when collapse strength remains adequate.

Design separates demand from resistance. Load analysis produces required strength or service response, while material and geometry produce nominal resistance that is adjusted by the chosen design method. Each plausible mode must be checked because the smallest usable resistance controls. Ductile modes are generally preferred because they provide deformation and warning, whereas brittle or sudden modes receive more conservative resistance or safety factors and greater detailing attention.
