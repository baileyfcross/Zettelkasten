# Tactile Navigation Display

A tactile navigation display uses stimulation location or motion across the body to communicate a desired direction. Belts, vests, and wristbands can activate a motor on the side toward which the wearer should turn or sequence several motors to create an apparent directional path.

The mapping is easy to learn because body-centered location corresponds naturally to external direction. Additional patterns can encode stop or speed changes, and touch remains private and usable when vision is occupied or sound is masked. The display must still account for clothing, environmental vibration, and the spatial acuity of the chosen site.
