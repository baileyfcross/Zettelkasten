# Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity Units

The book gives dynamic and kinematic viscosity separate conversion categories because their unit dimensions differ. Dynamic viscosity is represented by centipoise or millipascal seconds, while kinematic viscosity is represented by centistokes or square millimeters per second. Its examples happen to have a factor of one between the listed pairs, but that numerical coincidence does not make the two kinds of viscosity interchangeable. The unit expression identifies which conversion category and target unit are appropriate. See [[Algebraic SI Derived Units]].

Dynamic viscosity also measures a fluid's resistance to shear: a larger shear stress is required to maintain the same velocity gradient in a more viscous fluid. Kinematic viscosity divides dynamic viscosity by density, so it describes momentum diffusion rather than shear resistance alone.
