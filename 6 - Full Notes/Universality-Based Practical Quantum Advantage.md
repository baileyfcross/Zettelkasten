# Universality-Based Practical Quantum Advantage

The universality criterion compares fixed, problem-independent algorithms over an entire problem class rather than allowing a classical method to be redesigned for each instance. A universal simulator accepts interactions, an initial product state, and observables as inputs and returns their dynamics without bespoke analytical insight or model-specific code.

Under this constraint, practical quantum advantage occurs when a quantum-enabled universal algorithm produces more accurate results within the same resource budget than a purely classical universal algorithm. A customized classical solution may still win on a selected instance, but its redevelopment cost and lack of reuse are part of the proposed industrial comparison. This criterion is narrower than a complexity-theoretic separation and must be paired with an explicit class, accuracy target, and resource accounting.
