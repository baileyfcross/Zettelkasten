# Special Factorization

A special factorization recognizes a polynomial as one side of a standard algebraic identity. Common patterns include a difference of squares, a perfect-square trinomial, and sums or differences of cubes.

Recognition can shorten work and reveal further factors. For example,

$$x^4-81=(x^2-9)(x^2+9)=(x-3)(x+3)(x^2+9).$$

The first identity exposes another difference of squares, so factorization continues in stages. A pattern should be verified against all signs and exponents rather than applied from visual resemblance alone.
