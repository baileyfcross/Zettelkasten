# Mass-Energy Equivalence

Mass-energy equivalence states that a body of invariant mass $m$ has rest energy

$$E_0=mc^2.$$

The result is the zero-momentum case of the [[Energy-Momentum Relation]]. It identifies rest mass as stored energy rather than as a substance unrelated to other energy forms.

Expanding the relativistic total energy for speeds much smaller than $c$ gives $E\approx mc^2+\tfrac12mv^2+\cdots$. The first term is rest energy and the second is the familiar Newtonian kinetic energy, showing how classical mechanics sits inside the relativistic expression.

Energy added to an isolated system—including heat or internal motion—contributes to the system's [[Invariant Mass]]. Conversely, processes that release energy reduce the mass of the remaining bound system by the released energy divided by $c^2$.
