# Self-Tracking Data Partiality

Self-tracking data partiality is the unavoidable fact that a personal record captures selected aspects of experience rather than a complete life. Every project chooses variables, intervals, tools, and categories, leaving other conditions unrecorded.

Collecting more fields does not remove this limitation. A larger collection is still composed of partial views and may make purposeful attention harder. Sensor availability can also bias selection toward what is technically easy to measure, such as steps, rather than what matters but is difficult to sense, such as stress or social support.

Partiality can be used constructively by asking a narrow question, tracking one or two relevant observations, and treating the result as one perspective. Contextual notes and [[Qualitative Self-Tracking]] can then expose important circumstances that the primary measure misses.
