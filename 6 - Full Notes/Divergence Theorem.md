# Divergence Theorem

The divergence theorem equates the outward flux of a vector field through a closed surface with the volume integral of the field's divergence inside that surface. It converts a global boundary measurement into an accumulation of local source strength.

The theorem requires compatible smoothness and orientation, with the surface normal pointing outward. It underlies integral forms of conservation laws because changes within a volume can be related directly to transport across its boundary.
