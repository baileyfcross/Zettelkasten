# Ramsey and Hahn-Echo Coherence Characterization

A Ramsey experiment uses two nominal $\pi/2$ pulses separated by free evolution. The first creates a superposition, phase accumulates relative to the control frame, and the second converts that phase into a population signal. Its decay time $T_2^*$ includes both irreversible decoherence and slow shot-to-shot frequency variation.

A Hahn echo inserts a $\pi$ pulse halfway through the interval. Quasi-static detuning accumulates phase with one sign before the pulse and the opposite sign afterward, so much of it refocuses at the end. The echo time $T_{2E}$ is consequently often longer than $T_2^*$, although it cannot reverse energy relaxation or fluctuations that change too quickly.

The difference between Ramsey and echo is diagnostic rather than merely a better coherence number. A large improvement under echo indicates substantial low-frequency dephasing. Extending the idea to many refocusing pulses produces the tunable spectral filters described in [[Dynamical Decoupling Filter Functions]].
