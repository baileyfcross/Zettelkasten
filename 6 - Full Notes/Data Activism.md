# Data Activism

Data activism uses collective action to challenge how data are collected, controlled, interpreted, or applied. Participants may demand access to records, expose harmful profiling, create alternative tools, or resist being forced to track.

Self-tracking activists demonstrate that technical systems are not deterministic. A closed medical device can become the focus of an access campaign, a community can reinterpret data outside a platform's script, and artists can use records to question the meanings companies assign to them.

Effective activism connects personal experience with institutional change. It asks not only for an individual workaround but for rules, interfaces, and decision processes that alter who can benefit and who has authority over meaning.
