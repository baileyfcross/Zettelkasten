# Differential Privacy

Differential privacy deliberately introduces randomness so that released data or analysis reveals little about whether one individual's record was included. The corruption is calibrated to protect individual participation while preserving enough aggregate structure for useful generalization.

It addresses the fact that removing obvious identifiers may not prevent reidentification from combinations of other fields. Privacy is obtained through a measurable information tradeoff rather than an assumption that a superficially anonymous table is safe.
