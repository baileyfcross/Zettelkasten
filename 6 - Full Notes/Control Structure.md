# Control Structure

A control structure determines how the steps of an [[Algorithm]] or program are combined and which step executes next. Three forms are sufficient to organize algorithmic procedures: [[Sequence Control Structure|sequence]], [[Selection Control Structure|selection]], and [[Iteration Control Structure|iteration]].

Sequence places steps one after another. Selection chooses between alternatives according to a condition. Iteration repeats steps until a stopping condition is satisfied. Complex algorithms arise from nesting and composing these basic structures rather than requiring fundamentally different forms of control.
