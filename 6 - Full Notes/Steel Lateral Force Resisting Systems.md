# Steel Lateral Force Resisting Systems

Ordinary beam shear connections provide little rotational restraint, so a steel gravity frame normally needs an explicit lateral-force-resisting system. Braced frames use axial action in diagonal members, moment frames use bending and connection rigidity, and shear walls can work with steel framing. A building may combine systems, but their stiffness, strength, and deformation compatibility must be represented coherently.

Braced frames are often economical but compete with doors, windows, and circulation. Moment frames preserve open bays but demand heavier members and carefully detailed connections. System choice controls the response modification factor, drift, foundation forces, fabrication, inspection, and architectural coordination. Location also matters: distributing systems in plan limits torsion, while continuity through height prevents weak or interrupted stories.
