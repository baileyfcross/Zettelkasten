# Model Interpretability

Model interpretability is the degree to which people can understand how a trained model connects inputs to its output. Linear weights and root-to-leaf [[Decision Tree|tree]] rules expose a relatively direct calculation, while the distributed transformations inside a deep network are harder to inspect.

Interpretability helps domain experts validate whether learned relationships are meaningful and lets affected users question consequential decisions. It complements predictive testing rather than replacing it: a readable model can still be inaccurate, and an accurate aggregate score can still conceal unsafe sensitivity, biased evidence, or unacceptable individual errors.
