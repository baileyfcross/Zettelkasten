# Vauquois Triangle

The Vauquois triangle organizes machine-translation architectures by the depth of representation between a source and a target text. Its base is [[Direct Machine Translation]], where pair-specific mappings operate close to words. Higher levels analyze and transfer syntactic or semantic structures, while the apex uses a shared interlingua.

Moving upward promises more reusable and meaning-aware representations, but it also requires harder analysis and generation. The triangle therefore expresses a design tradeoff rather than a simple ranking: when a complete semantic representation is unattainable, a robust lower-level transfer can be more useful than an ambitious system that fails to analyze the input.
