# Lattice-Based Cryptography

Lattice-based cryptography builds security from computational problems over high-dimensional lattices. A lattice consists of all integer linear combinations of a basis, and the large dimensions used by cryptographic constructions make apparently simple geometric search problems difficult.

The source lists shortest-vector, closest-vector, short-basis, and short-independent-vector problems as sources of hardness. These assumptions support [[Post-Quantum Cryptography]] because the resulting schemes are intended to resist attacks based on Shor's algorithm. They also support homomorphic constructions. The [[NTRU Cryptosystem]] is the book's main lattice-related encryption example.
