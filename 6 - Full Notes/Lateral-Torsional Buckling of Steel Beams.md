# Lateral-Torsional Buckling of Steel Beams

Lateral-torsional buckling occurs when an unbraced beam's compression flange moves laterally while the section twists. It can prevent a compact cross-section from reaching its plastic moment, especially for open shapes with long unbraced lengths. The unbraced length, weak-axis stiffness, torsional and warping stiffness, loading position, and moment diagram all influence resistance.

Short unbraced segments can reach plastic strength, intermediate segments buckle inelastically, and long segments buckle elastically. A moment-gradient factor recognizes that a nonuniform moment diagram can be less destabilizing than uniform moment. Effective bracing must restrain lateral movement or twist with adequate strength and stiffness; simply attaching a nominal element near the beam does not guarantee that the compression flange is stabilized.
