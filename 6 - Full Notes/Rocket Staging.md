# Rocket Staging

Rocket staging divides a launch vehicle into sections that operate sequentially and are discarded after their propellant is exhausted. Removing empty tanks, engines, and structure prevents later propulsion from spending energy accelerating mass that no longer contributes to the mission.

The idea follows from the mass-ratio constraint expressed by the [[Variable-Mass Rocket Equation]]. One vehicle has practical limits on how much propellant it can carry relative to structure and payload; stacking stages lets each later stage begin with a smaller mass. Tsiolkovsky identified staging as a path beyond the limitations of a single rocket, and it became fundamental to ballistic missiles and orbital boosters.
