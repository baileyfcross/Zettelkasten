# Pluralistic Computational Thinking Education

A pluralistic approach to computational thinking education accepts multiple entry points: programming, data, machines, design, simulation, and social consequences. Different learners and domains can emphasize different practices while remaining connected to computation.

Pluralism should not erase disciplinary precision. Activities need clear links to representations, machine realization, evidence, and the contexts in which a computational method is useful.
