# Four-Vector

A four-vector is a spacetime quantity with one temporal and three spatial components that transforms under a [[Lorentz Transformation]]. Its components depend on the inertial frame, while its Minkowski inner product with itself is invariant.

With a time-positive sign convention, a four-vector $A=(A^0,\mathbf{A})$ has norm $A\cdot A=(A^0)^2-\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{A}$. This differs from a Euclidean four-dimensional vector because the time and space contributions enter with opposite signs.

Writing a physical law in four-vector form exposes its Lorentz-covariant content. Important examples include [[Four-Position]], [[Four-Velocity]], [[Four-Momentum]], and [[Four-Force]]. Each unifies quantities that a stationary observer may otherwise describe separately.
