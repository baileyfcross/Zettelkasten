# Multispectral and Hyperspectral Remote Sensing

Multispectral sensing records a limited set of spectral bands, while hyperspectral sensing records hundreds of narrow bands. More bands can reveal finer differences in a target's [[Spectral Signature]], such as disturbed soil or distinct vegetation, but they also create a larger processing and interpretation burden.

Band selection must fit the task. The wavelengths useful for plant health are not necessarily those useful for minerals or archaeological traces. Hyperspectral detail is therefore valuable when the application can use it; it is not automatically better when storage, computation, calibration, or classification limits dominate.
