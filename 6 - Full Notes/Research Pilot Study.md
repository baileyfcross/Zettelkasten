# Research Pilot Study

A research pilot study is a small preliminary execution of a proposed study used to test feasibility before committing to the full design. It examines whether data can be reached, what the records mean, where they originate, whether they are complete and understandable, and whether they fit the intended analysis.

Piloting can test recruitment, instruments, task instructions, timing, measurement attributes, coding, data transfer, and candidate methods or models. Its purpose is to discover design failures while revision remains affordable, not to produce a miniature definitive result.

Changes prompted by the pilot should be documented, and pilot observations should not silently enter the confirmatory analysis when exposure to them influenced the final protocol. The value of the pilot lies in improving the study's procedure and assumptions.
