# Queueing State-Space Model

A queueing state-space model represents a service system by its possible occupancy states and transitions caused by arrivals and departures. Transition rates determine how probability flows among those states.

Once steady-state occupancies are estimated, designers can calculate throughput, response time, and overflow risk. This permits capacity to be chosen against a target probability of denying service instead of provisioning for every possible demand.
