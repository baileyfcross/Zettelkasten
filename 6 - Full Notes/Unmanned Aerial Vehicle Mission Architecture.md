# Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Mission Architecture

An unmanned aerial vehicle carries no onboard human pilot. If it is not fully autonomous, it also depends on a ground controller and communication link; autonomous operation adds sensing, state estimation, mapping, planning, and feedback control. Mission suitability follows from the entire system rather than the airframe alone, including payload, battery, propulsion, and the environment to be observed.

Military use makes the system boundary especially visible. An unmanned aerial system includes ground control, data links, operators, imagery processing, and mission procedures as well as the aircraft. Reducing airframe size or pilot risk can improve endurance and deployment, but added sensors, weapons, encryption, and new requirements increase weight, cost, bandwidth, and maintenance. The operational capability belongs to the integrated network, not to the drone in isolation.
