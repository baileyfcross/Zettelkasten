# Remote Sensing Resolution Tradeoff

A remote-sensing system must trade among spatial detail, revisit frequency, geographic coverage, and processing capacity. A platform may observe a small area frequently and finely, or cover a much larger area with less detail or fewer visits. High-resolution measurements also increase transmission, storage, and computation requirements.

The tradeoff prevents a sensor from being everywhere, continuously, at maximum detail. System design should begin with the decision that the observations must support, then choose [[Remote Sensing Spatial Resolution|spatial]] and [[Remote Sensing Temporal Resolution|temporal resolution]] that are sufficient rather than maximizing one dimension in isolation.
