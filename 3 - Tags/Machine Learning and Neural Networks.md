# Machine Learning and Neural Networks

Parent topics: [[Computer Science]] · [[Mathematics]]

Data-driven function learning, generalization, learned representations, and neural architectures for language, images, and other complex inputs.

## Overview Chapter

Machine learning replaces the need to specify every decision rule with a procedure that adjusts a model from experience. The central question is not whether a system can reproduce its training examples, but whether it can use those examples to make warranted predictions or decisions in new cases. [[Machine Learning Foundations]] develops that logic from the ground up. A model supplies a family of possible relationships, its parameters select one member of that family, and a performance criterion supplies the direction of improvement. Because a finite sample rarely determines a unique explanation, every learner also carries assumptions about smoothness, simplicity, locality, or another form of inductive bias. Generalization depends on matching those assumptions and the model's capacity to the process that produced the data. Too little capacity misses structure; too much can fit accidental variation. Validation on unused cases is therefore part of learning rather than an optional report after training.

[[Artificial General Intelligence Architectures]] extends this learning framework from bounded prediction tasks to agents expected to cope across domains. General intelligence requires more than increasing model size: the system must acquire grounded concepts, construct a predictive model of the physical and social world, form or receive goals, and select actions under uncertainty. Embodiment can supply sensorimotor grounding directly, while vicarious experience can approximate it through large records of other agents' interactions. A human-level system need not reproduce human psychology, because comparable breadth of performance can arise from a different architecture, reward structure, or pattern of strengths.

The learner can only use distinctions represented in its inputs. [[Feature Engineering]] covers the construction, transformation, selection, and evaluation of those representations. Raw attributes may be irrelevant, redundant, too costly to obtain, or expressed at the wrong level of abstraction. Selection retains useful measured variables, while extraction creates new coordinates such as variance-preserving projections or learned latent features. Text, images, sequences, streams, and graphs each carry structure that a flat list of arbitrary values can lose. Good representation reduces computation and sample demands while making the important regularities easier for a model to express.

When examples include desired outputs, [[Data Classification]] organizes the methods that map measured features to discrete labels. Linear boundaries, probabilistic models, trees, rules, neighborhoods, support-vector machines, neural networks, and ensembles embody different assumptions about how classes occupy the input space. Their apparent accuracy is inseparable from sampling, labeling, class balance, error costs, and evaluation design. A medical false negative and an authentication false positive need not carry the same consequence, so precision, recall, sensitivity, specificity, and decision thresholds reveal tradeoffs hidden by one overall accuracy number. Active and transfer learning can reduce labeling effort, but their benefit still depends on representative evidence and careful validation.

Neural networks learn by composing many small parameterized transformations. [[Convolutional Visual Architecture]] introduces locality and shared filters so spatial patterns can be detected wherever they occur. Early layers can respond to simple edges or textures, while later layers combine these responses into more abstract parts and objects. Local receptive fields reduce the number of parameters and encode the useful assumption that neighboring pixels or samples are related. Pooling and later dense integration trade exact position for broader recognition, and skip connections help very deep models preserve trainable signal.

[[Quantum Convolutional Neural Networks]] transfer shared local operations and hierarchical pooling to quantum-state inputs. Their convolution layers reuse quasi-local unitaries, while measurement-conditioned pooling removes qubits and applies corrections to those that remain. Read as reverse entanglement renormalization combined with nested error correction, the architecture can drive related many-body states toward a common phase representative or optimize an encoding-decoding pair for a particular noise process. The parameter count can grow only logarithmically with input qubits under translation invariance, although the gate count and the practical difficulty of preparing states and training circuits remain substantial.

[[Quantum-Deformed Probabilistic Neural Networks]] take a different route by encoding binary activations and weights as qubits, entangling them with a learned deformation, and recovering thresholded neuron outputs through quantum phase estimation. General deformation circuits can define distributions that are efficient to sample quantumly but hard to sample classically, although this conditional sampling separation does not by itself establish a learning advantage. The paper's actual image experiments use a low-entanglement nearest-neighbor restriction and a weak-correlation Gaussian approximation; they show modest gains on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, accompanied by substantial parameter growth and no quantum-hardware demonstration.

[[Variable-Structure Variational Quantum Circuits]] address a different bottleneck: the gate layout itself becomes part of the optimization. VAns inserts parameterized blocks at identity settings, simplifies the resulting circuit with algebraic rules and cost-tested deletions, retrains its continuous angles, and then accepts or rejects the structural proposal. By keeping depth and parameter count responsive to the task, the method aims to limit both expressibility-driven and noise-induced barren plateaus. Small simulations report favorable ground-state, autoencoding, quantum-Fourier-transform compilation, and synthetic-noise results against selected hardware-efficient circuits, but they do not yet establish performance on quantum hardware or at large scale.

[[Quantum Dropout Regularization]] changes a quantum circuit temporarily during training rather than permanently searching for a new layout. It samples layers and parameterized operations to replace with identities, updates the shared parameters through the resulting subcircuit, and restores the complete model for inference. Small noiseless regression and classification experiments show improved held-out error at low effective drop rates, while measurements of expressibility and multipartite entanglement remain essentially unchanged in the useful operating regime. This points to reduced gate co-adaptation across an ensemble of sampled subcircuits, not a simple loss of circuit capacity; it also explains why classical parameter-rescaling rules do not transfer automatically.

Ordered data require memory as well as local pattern detection. [[Recurrent Sequence Architecture]] carries a hidden state from one step to the next so a current prediction can depend on earlier input. Gated units such as long short-term memory selectively preserve or discard context, while encoder-decoder systems convert one sequence into another. These mechanisms support speech, translation, and captioning, but recurrence creates long dependency paths and limits parallel computation. They establish the sequence-modeling problem that later attention-based architectures address more directly.

[[Large Language Model Foundations]] follows that transition from statistical sequence models and recurrence to transformer attention. Tokens begin with learned embeddings and position information; attention then lets each position retrieve contextually relevant information from other positions. Multiple heads learn several relationship spaces, causal masking preserves the direction required for next-token prediction, and autoregressive generation repeatedly converts a probability distribution into a growing sequence. Scale expands the patterns a model can encode, but it does not remove dependence on training data, evaluation, or operational safeguards.

[[Quantum Autoencoding and State Compression]] carries representation learning into quantum systems. A parameterized unitary concentrates the support of a low-rank quantum state into a smaller latent register while driving a disposable register toward a fixed reference. When the latent capacity matches the state's rank and training succeeds, the compressed state preserves spectral information that can support fidelity estimation, sensing objectives, or variational thermal-state preparation. Its prospective computational advantage remains conditional on efficient training and controlled reconstruction error, so compression quality belongs in the guarantee for every downstream use.

Not every learning problem supplies a correct answer for each step. [[Reinforcement Learning Methods]] studies an agent that acts in an environment and receives rewards that may be sparse, delayed, and uncertain. The agent must explore enough to learn the effects of unfamiliar actions while exploiting what currently appears best. Temporal-difference methods propagate later reward estimates backward to earlier state-action choices, value models estimate how promising a situation is, and policies select actions. Partial observability adds uncertainty about the state itself, while imitation and inverse reinforcement learning use demonstrations to reduce costly or unsafe blind exploration.

The learned model ultimately depends on a physical and operational execution path. [[NVIDIA GPU Infrastructure]] connects neural computation to CUDA execution, accelerator memory and interconnects, resource partitioning, high-throughput storage and networks, model optimization, inference serving, and production monitoring. These layers do not change what a model means, but they determine whether training finishes efficiently, whether an artifact can be reproduced, and whether inference can meet a service objective under real load. Infrastructure choices should therefore follow the model's measured compute, memory, data-movement, isolation, and reliability requirements rather than the general label of artificial intelligence.

Together, these topics show that machine learning is a complete evidence-and-decision system rather than a single algorithm. Representation determines what can be noticed; model structure determines what can be expressed; training determines which parameters are selected; evaluation determines whether the result travels beyond the sample; and deployment determines what its errors mean. Neural architectures automate more of the representation process, but they remain subject to the same requirements for adequate data, appropriate assumptions, accountable objectives, and testing under the conditions in which their decisions matter.
