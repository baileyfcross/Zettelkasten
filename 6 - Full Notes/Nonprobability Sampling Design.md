# Nonprobability Sampling Design

Nonprobability sampling design selects cases without known random inclusion probabilities. Convenience or accidental samples use available participants; purposive samples seek cases with relevant characteristics; modal-instance samples target typical cases; expert samples recruit specialized judgment; and quota samples fill predefined category counts.

These designs can be appropriate for exploration, rare expertise, early instrument development, or settings without a usable sampling frame. Their practical value does not make them probability samples: conventional sampling-error calculations cannot quantify the unknown selection process.

Claims should therefore identify how cases entered the study, what perspectives may be absent, and which conclusions depend on representativeness. Transparent boundaries are more credible than implying population generality from sample size alone.
