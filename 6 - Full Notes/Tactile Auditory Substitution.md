# Tactile Auditory Substitution

Tactile auditory substitution translates environmental sound or speech features into time-varying stimulation on the skin. A wearable can encode a knock, alarm, or baby's cry as a distinct vibration pattern, or represent syllable timing and phonetic features to support speech reading.

The mapping from an acoustic feature to a tactile cue is usually arbitrary, so users need systematic, long-term training. Touch has useful temporal resolution but cannot reproduce the ear's full bandwidth or waveform analysis. Effective systems select a limited set of meaningful features rather than attempting to transmit the entire sound signal.
