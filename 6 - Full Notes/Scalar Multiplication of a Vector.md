# Scalar Multiplication of a Vector

Scalar multiplication multiplies every component of a vector by the same scalar. It changes magnitude and may reverse direction while preserving the vector's line.

Together with [[Vector Addition]], it must satisfy the vector-space distributive and associative axioms. Weighted sums formed from these operations are [[Linear Combination|linear combinations]].

For a geometrical vector, a positive scalar preserves direction and changes length, a negative scalar also reverses direction, and multiplication by zero produces the zero vector. The component rule and the geometric interpretation therefore describe the same scaling operation.
