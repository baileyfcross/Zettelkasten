# Linear Gauge Protection in Quantum Simulators

An analog quantum simulator implements a target gauge theory through a controllable physical Hamiltonian. The mapping is approximate: the desired gauge-symmetric Hamiltonian appears at leading order, while smaller unwanted terms can violate the local gauge constraints and limit the interval over which the emulation remains trustworthy.

Linear gauge protection adds experimentally accessible one-body penalty terms that energetically suppress gauge-violating processes. The report highlights a tilted Bose-Hubbard superlattice in which this mechanism enabled a 71-site U(1) quantum-link experiment. Gauge invariance remained approximately protected while the system was swept through a phase transition and later used to investigate thermalization and many-body scarring.

The method turns symmetry from a property checked only after simulation into an active stabilizer of the implementation. It does not remove every imperfection, and measured gauge violation remains part of validation, but it extends useful dynamics without requiring full quantum error correction. The protected model is an experimental realization of the finite-dimensional gauge variables described by [[Quantum Link Models and D-Theory]].
