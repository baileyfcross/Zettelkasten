# Time-Independent Perturbation Theory

Time-independent perturbation theory approximates the eigenvalues and eigenstates of a Hamiltonian that differs slightly from a solvable one. Energies and states are expanded in powers of a bookkeeping parameter multiplying the perturbation.

For a nondegenerate level, the first-order energy correction is the expectation value of the perturbing Hamiltonian in the unperturbed state. Higher corrections account for mixing with other unperturbed states and become unreliable when denominators are small or the perturbation is not weak.
