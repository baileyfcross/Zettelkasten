# Welded Plate Girder Design

A welded plate girder is a built-up I-shaped member made from flange plates welded to a web plate. It serves long spans or large loads beyond economical rolled-shape capacity, including bridges, industrial crane supports, stadium canopies, and building transfer girders. Preliminary proportioning balances overall depth, flange area for bending, and web thickness for shear and stability.

Slender webs may buckle in shear before yielding, but transverse stiffeners can divide the web into panels and permit diagonal tension-field action after buckling. Flexural strength must account for web slenderness and flange stability, while component welds transfer longitudinal shear between web and flanges. Bearing stiffeners, splice details, lateral bracing, fabrication tolerances, and duct routing can control the final design as strongly as nominal member strength.
