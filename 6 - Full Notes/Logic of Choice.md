# Logic of Choice

The logic of choice frames health technology as information that enables an individual to select among options. In its simplified form, better data produces better knowledge, the informed person makes the correct decision, and behavior changes.

This model highlights agency but can underestimate the work required to interpret a measure and the social conditions that constrain action. A person may know the recommended choice yet lack time, money, support, or a feasible routine for carrying it out.

The [[Logic of Care]] provides a complementary account in which treatment is repeatedly adjusted through relationships and circumstances. Self-tracking supports choice most effectively when it is embedded in that continuing practice rather than treated as a substitute for it.
