# Consequentialism

Consequentialism evaluates an action by the outcomes it produces for affected parties. Utilitarian reasoning is a consequentialist form that compares how alternative actions increase or decrease overall well-being.

Applying it to software requires identifying direct and indirect stakeholders and considering probable benefits, harms, risks, and costs for each option. The calculation can be uncertain and benefits may be distributed unequally, so consequences should be examined alongside professional duties and rights rather than reduced to one convenient total.
