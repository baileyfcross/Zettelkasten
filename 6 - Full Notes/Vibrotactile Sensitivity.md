# Vibrotactile Sensitivity

Vibrotactile sensitivity describes the skin's ability to detect mechanical vibration as frequency and displacement change. Human skin responds across a broad frequency range but is most sensitive to small displacements in the roughly 200–300 Hz region.

Different [[Cutaneous Mechanoreceptor|mechanoreceptor]] classes dominate threshold detection in different bands, while stronger vibration recruits multiple classes. The relationship is not flat: lower and higher frequencies require larger movement for detection. A [[Vibrotactile Display]] must therefore consider both frequency and amplitude rather than assuming equal physical displacement creates equal perceived intensity.
