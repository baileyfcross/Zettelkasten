# Low-Power Wide-Area Network

A low-power wide-area network provides long-range wireless communication for devices that send relatively small amounts of data while operating under strict energy limits. It is suited to geographically dispersed sensors in agriculture, cities, infrastructure, logistics, and industry where conventional Wi-Fi has insufficient range and ordinary cellular communication may consume too much power.

LPWAN families include LoRa, SigFox, LTE-M, and NB-IoT. They make different tradeoffs among range, throughput, latency, operator dependence, and battery life. Their purpose is not maximum data speed but persistent, economical connectivity for large populations of constrained endpoints.
