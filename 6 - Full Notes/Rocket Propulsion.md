# Rocket Propulsion

Rocket propulsion creates thrust by expelling a high-speed jet while carrying all required propellants inside the vehicle. The exhaust does not push against surrounding air. Vehicle and exhaust receive opposite changes in momentum under Newton's third law, so the system can operate in a vacuum.

Chemical rockets release energy by reacting a fuel with an oxidizer. [[Liquid-Propellant Rocket]] and [[Solid-Propellant Rocket]] systems package and control those materials differently, but both convert hot expanding gas into directed exhaust. The vehicle loses mass as propellant is consumed, making the [[Variable-Mass Rocket Equation]] central to achievable velocity.
