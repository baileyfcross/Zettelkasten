# Asymmetric Error Costs

Two kinds of classification mistake can have very different consequences. In the book's medical example, missing an ill patient may be more serious than sending a healthy patient for additional testing. A single proportion-correct score hides that difference.

Evaluation should therefore reflect the task's actual error costs and decision thresholds. The preferred model is not necessarily the one with the highest unweighted accuracy; it is the one whose mistakes and benefits fit the setting in which people will act on its outputs.
