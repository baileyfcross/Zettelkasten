# Structural Welding Processes

Structural welding fuses base metal with heat and may deposit filler metal through an electrode. Shielded metal arc welding is a manual process common in field work, while submerged arc welding is an automated, high-deposition process suited to controlled shop fabrication. Other arc processes may be selected for productivity, position, material, and quality requirements.

Welding procedure affects heat input, penetration, residual stress, distortion, hydrogen risk, and inspectability. Shop welds are generally easier to position and protect from weather, while field welds can demand more labor and quality control. Material chemistry and carbon equivalent influence preheat and electrode choices. A weld is therefore not merely a line with a capacity; it is a fabrication process governed by qualified procedures and inspection.
