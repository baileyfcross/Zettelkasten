# Data Encryption Standard

The Data Encryption Standard is a symmetric block cipher that encrypts 64-bit data blocks under a 56-bit effective key. Its Feistel-style process applies an initial permutation, splits the block into left and right halves, passes them through 16 keyed rounds, rejoins them, and applies a final permutation. Decryption uses the round keys in reverse order.

DES became unsuitable for new applications as increasing computing power made exhaustive search of its small key space practical. [[Triple DES]] extended the construction by applying DES repeatedly, while the [[Advanced Encryption Standard]] replaced it as the preferred standard.
