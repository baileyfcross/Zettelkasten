# Thermal Expansion Characterization

Thermomechanical analysis applies a small load while recording a specimen's dimensional change through a temperature cycle. The response yields strain and can be used to infer temperature-dependent elastic or viscoelastic behavior.

A dilatometer measures expansion more directly, and the thermal expansion coefficient relates relative dimensional or volumetric change to temperature. These properties become important when a thermal field is passed into a structural model or when expansion affects contact and [[Thermal Boundary Condition|boundary behavior]].

When an expansion coefficient is reported per degree on another temperature scale, its reciprocal-temperature unit must be converted with the interval size, not with the offset used for an absolute temperature reading. Pryor's unit-conversion example treats Fahrenheit and Celsius differences separately from thermometer values. See [[Temperature Readings versus Intervals]].
