# Thermal Sensing by the Skin

Thermal sensing by the skin uses separately distributed warm and cold receptors to detect temperature change. Cold receptors are more numerous, and body regions differ in sensitivity; the fingertips that excel at spatial touch are not necessarily the most sensitive sites for temperature.

During contact, heat flow depends on the starting temperatures and material properties of the object. Metal often feels cooler than plastic because it draws heat from the skin more rapidly. Thermal input also interacts with touch: colder objects can feel heavier, while wetness perception combines cooling with pressure and stickiness cues.
