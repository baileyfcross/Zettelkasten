# Ising Symmetry Breaking in Random-Circuit XEB

The averaged doubled description of a one-dimensional Haar-random circuit can be transformed into a two-dimensional ferromagnetic Ising model. An ideal circuit has a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry and degenerate leading transfer-matrix sectors, while noise or deliberate gate omissions act like symmetry-breaking magnetic fields and open a spectral gap.

Homogeneous device noise behaves as a weak bulk field; partition boundaries behave as strong fields confined to edges between subsystems. In the large-system limit, even a weak bulk field changes the free-energy density more strongly than a fixed set of boundary fields. This explains why a noisy device's XEB can decay less favorably with scale than a partitioned classical algorithm's score, despite each omitted gate being a severe local alteration.
