# Benign and Adversarial Quantum Benchmarking

A benign benchmark interpretation asks whether an honestly implemented but noisy quantum circuit remains close to its ideal target under a specified device-noise model. In that setting, XEB can serve as a practical proxy when errors are weak, independent, and distributed approximately uniformly through the circuit.

An adversarial benchmark interpretation asks whether any classical algorithm can achieve the same score. The competitor may optimize directly for the benchmark rather than simulate the target circuit faithfully, so assumptions that justify [[XEB Fidelity Proxy Conditions]] do not constrain it. A quantum-advantage claim therefore needs a soundness argument against benchmark-specific shortcuts, not only a high experimental score. This distinction complements the claim-strength categories in [[Practical Quantum Advantage Evidence Tiers]].
