# Measurement Label Framing

Measurement label framing is the way a name attached to a recorded value guides its interpretation. A device may calculate several signals and present the result as “sleep quality,” “readiness,” or “productivity,” making a constructed score appear to be a direct property of the person.

Labels compress assumptions about which inputs matter, how they are combined, and which outcome is desirable. If those assumptions are hidden by a proprietary algorithm, the user cannot tell whether the score answers their actual question. Even a manual label can become too coarse as a person's understanding develops, such as treating every unpleasant mood as the same condition.

Useful self-tracking names the observation narrowly, records enough context to revise it, and distinguishes the raw input from the interpretation placed on it.
