# Clinical Validity of Personal Data

Clinical validity of personal data is the degree to which a self-tracked measure represents a medically meaningful phenomenon well enough to support the proposed care decision. A record can be valuable to the person yet fail this standard because the device, label, or inferred relationship has not been validated.

Clinicians consider whether the measure has face validity within biomedical understanding, whether it indicates what its name claims, and whether the observations are reliable enough for interpretation. They must also separate a plausible association from a causal conclusion.

Validity is purpose-specific. A rough mood diary may support a conversation, while changing medication requires stronger evidence and safeguards. The right question is not whether all personal data are clinical, but what evidence the particular action demands.
