# Direct Analysis Method for Steel Frames

The Direct Analysis Method evaluates frame stability by modeling second-order effects, reduced member stiffness, and initial geometric imperfections within the structural analysis. Instead of assigning column effective-length factors from idealized alignment conditions, it commonly designs members with K equal to unity while the system model carries the destabilizing behavior.

Notional lateral loads represent out-of-plumbness, and stiffness reduction represents inelasticity and residual stress that make the real frame softer than an ideal elastic model. The method is broadly applicable to braced and unbraced frames, but only when its analysis requirements are implemented consistently. It turns stability from an isolated column correction into a system calculation connecting member forces, drift, imperfections, bracing, and load combination.
