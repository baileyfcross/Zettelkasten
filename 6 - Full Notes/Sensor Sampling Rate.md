# Sensor Sampling Rate

Sensor sampling rate is the frequency at which a sensing system takes measurements. A higher rate can preserve faster changes and finer timing, while a lower rate produces fewer observations and may miss short events.

The appropriate rate depends on the phenomenon and question. Slowly changing daily activity may tolerate widely spaced samples, whereas a rapidly changing physiological signal may require much denser measurement. More samples also increase storage, battery, transmission, and analysis demands.

Sampling rate is part of the context needed to interpret self-tracking data. A smooth summary can hide gaps or processing, so a meaningful export should distinguish the original sampling schedule from later aggregation.
