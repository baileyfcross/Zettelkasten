# Health-Wellness Boundary

The health-wellness boundary separates regulated medical care from consumer practices marketed as healthy living. Self-tracking tools frequently occupy the gray area between them: a device may encourage exercise as a lifestyle product while collecting measurements that resemble clinical information.

The distinction affects evidence, oversight, privacy, and responsibility. A clinical tool is expected to meet medical standards and fit care protocols, while a consumer product can make broad motivational claims and address customers who choose to buy it. Marketing can blur the line without supplying the validation required for treatment decisions.

The same device may cross the boundary when a clinician, employer, or insurer adopts it. Classification should therefore consider the actual use, claims, data flow, and consequences rather than the product's appearance alone.
