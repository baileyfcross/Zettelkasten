# Public Health Aggregation

Public health aggregation combines many individual observations to study patterns at a population level. Streams from phones, sensors, and personal records can support larger or more continuous studies than occasional surveys and clinical visits.

Aggregation can reveal trends that no individual record establishes, but it changes the meaning and stakes of contribution. The sample may reflect device ownership rather than the target population, measurements may vary across products, and a use accepted for personal care may not fit research expectations.

Useful aggregation documents recruitment, measures, missing groups, and data flows. It also maintains privacy and participant influence because the public benefit of a study does not automatically authorize every secondary use.
