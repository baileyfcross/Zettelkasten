# Challenger Accident

The Challenger accident was the loss of a Space Shuttle and its seven-person crew during launch in January 1986 after a solid rocket booster failure. It halted shuttle flights until September 1988 and fundamentally changed the program's role.

Commercial satellite launches moved to expendable rockets, and later national-security payloads did the same. Shuttle missions increasingly centered on science, Hubble servicing, and space stations. The disaster also exposed how a high-profile reusable system could normalize warning signs and operate without a crew escape option, issues echoed by the later Columbia loss.
