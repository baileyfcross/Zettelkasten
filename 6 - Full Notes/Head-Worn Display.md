# Head-Worn Display

A head-worn display is a [[Visual Display]] worn on the user's head so the imagery remains aligned with the eyes as the head moves. Opaque versions can immerse a user in [[Virtual Reality]], while see-through forms can overlay information for [[Augmented Reality]].

Head tracking lets the system render from the user's changing viewpoint. Because the display turns with the head, it can provide a 360-degree [[Field of Regard]] even when its instantaneous [[Field of View]] is much smaller.

Design tradeoffs include resolution, weight, comfort, optics, depth-cue conflicts, and [[Latency]].

For AR, a head-worn display may use an [[Optical See-Through Display|optical see-through]] combiner or a [[Video See-Through Display|video see-through]] camera pipeline. Monocular, bi-ocular, and binocular arrangements provide different [[Ocularity|ocularity]], while eye-to-display calibration and focus determine how well imagery aligns with and can be comfortably viewed beside physical objects.

The optical chain also constrains exit pupil, eye relief, image distortion, and the supported [[Interpupillary Distance]]. Enlarging the image source or adding corrective optics can widen the view but usually adds size, mass, and alignment sensitivity. A headset is therefore a coupled human-centered design: display panel, optics, tracker, rendering pipeline, and the wearer's visual system cannot be optimized independently.
