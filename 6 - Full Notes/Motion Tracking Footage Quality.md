# Motion Tracking Footage Quality

Accurate tracking begins with footage designed for post-production. Useful source material records the camera, keeps lighting stable, preserves clean and sharp frames, and captures physical information about the scene.

Blur makes groups of pixels difficult to follow. Progressive frames, disabled autofocus, limited motion blur, and restrained depth of field provide clearer features; dramatic blur can be reintroduced later after reliable motion and depth have been recovered.

Translational camera motion should reveal parallax between foreground and background features so depth can be reconstructed. Trackable static corners should span both ranges, while zoom, compression artifacts, fast movement, reflections, moving foliage, and intersections between objects at different depths all make correspondence less reliable.
