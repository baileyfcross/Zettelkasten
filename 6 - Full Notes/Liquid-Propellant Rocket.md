# Liquid-Propellant Rocket

A liquid-propellant rocket stores one or more fluid propellants and feeds them into a combustion chamber to produce an expanding exhaust jet. A common arrangement keeps fuel and oxidizer in separate tanks so their flow and ignition can be controlled.

Early spaceflight theorists recognized that energetic combinations such as liquid oxygen and hydrogen could greatly exceed black powder. Robert Goddard demonstrated liquid-fuel flight experimentally, while later German work produced the [[V-2 Ballistic Missile]] and accelerated large-engine development. Liquid systems offer control and high performance but require tanks, pumps or pressurization, plumbing, combustion stability, and management of difficult cryogenic materials.
