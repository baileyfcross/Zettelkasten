# Fourier-Domain Quantum Optical Classification

A thin lens placed one focal length from the probe plane and the beam splitter applies a [[Spatial Fourier Transform]] to the probe. The resulting coincidence score compares the input's spatial field with the probe's momentum spectrum, so it mixes spatial modes rather than taking only a pointwise image-plane overlap.

Fourier-transform duality gives an equivalent configuration in which the lens transforms the input branch instead of the probe branch. This symmetry can simplify both training and numerical modeling because the same score can be written as an overlap between the Fourier-transformed input and the untransformed probe. In the paper's simulations, position-domain and Fourier-domain quantum optical neurons show similar convergence on binary MNIST and CIFAR-10 tasks, although their curves are not identical.
