# Classical Cipher

A classical cipher transforms symbols with a comparatively simple shared-key rule. The [[Caesar Cipher]] applies one substitution shift, the [[Vigenere Cipher]] changes the shift according to a repeated keyword, and the [[Hill Cipher]] transforms groups of symbols with matrix arithmetic.

These systems are educational models of [[Symmetric Encryption]] because the same secret information controls encryption and decryption. They also show why a mechanism cannot be judged only by whether it obscures text: restricted key spaces, language frequencies, repeated structure, and linear relations can make recovery practical.
