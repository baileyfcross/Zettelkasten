# Model Incompleteness

Model incompleteness is the unavoidable gap between a model and the real phenomenon it represents. Some variables, influences, distinctions, or relationships are omitted because they are unknown, unobservable, or impractical to include.

Those hidden influences prevent the model from reproducing or predicting every behavior exactly. Bayesian programming treats the resulting uncertainty as something the model should express explicitly rather than as a defect that can always be eliminated by adding detail.
