# Probability Distribution

A probability distribution assigns a likelihood to each possible outcome in a range. A uniform distribution gives outcomes equal chances, while combining independent events can create a nonuniform shape such as the bell-like distribution of two dice.

The distribution's mean, mode, median, and range describe different aspects of its shape. Choosing a distribution changes the character of uncertainty, so an attribute value has different meaning under a flat range than under a clustered one.

In Bayesian reasoning, a probability distribution assigns normalized plausibility across the alternatives of a variable. Joint distributions describe several variables together, conditional distributions incorporate known information, and marginal distributions remove variables that are not part of the question.
