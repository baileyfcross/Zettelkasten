# Automated Profiling

Automated profiling uses data to infer or predict characteristics, preferences, risks, or likely behavior associated with a person or group. The profile can influence advertising, policing, employment, insurance, credit, or access to services without being visible to the person described.

A profile is a statistical construction, not a complete identity. It may rely on [[Spurious Correlation]], historical patterns, or proxy variables and can become self-reinforcing when institutions act on it. Ethical use requires privacy protection, justification of the inference, attention to discrimination, and a meaningful way to contest consequential classifications.
