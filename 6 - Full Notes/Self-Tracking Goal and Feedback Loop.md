# Self-Tracking Goal and Feedback Loop

A self-tracking goal and feedback loop compares an observed state with a desired target and uses the difference to guide later action. Step targets, productivity totals, and habit streaks are common examples: the record makes a gap visible, and feedback is intended to reduce it.

The loop depends on how the goal and display are framed. Counting accomplishments can support a sense of progress, while emphasizing every missed day can produce discouragement even from the same behavior. Time scale also matters because a daily target, weekly total, and long-term trend direct attention differently.

The target should remain open to revision. A measure may be easy to collect without representing the person's actual purpose, and more of the measured quantity is not always better. The loop becomes useful through [[Personal Data Interpretation]], not through collection alone.
