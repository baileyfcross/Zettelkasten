# Civil-Military Space Program

A civil-military space program is an institutional arrangement in which civilian science and exploration share launch vehicles, sensors, expertise, facilities, or objectives with military and intelligence activity. The labels may separate budgets and public narratives without creating a clean technical boundary.

Early satellites studied the atmosphere and gravity while supporting knowledge important to missiles. Weather information served civilian forecasting and military reconnaissance, and navigation systems developed for armed forces became public infrastructure. A civilian agency can therefore produce peaceful missions within a wider system whose boosters and capabilities grew from national-security investment.
