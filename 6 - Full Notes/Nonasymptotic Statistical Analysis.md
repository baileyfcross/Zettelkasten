# Nonasymptotic Statistical Analysis

Nonasymptotic statistical analysis gives performance bounds that hold for explicit finite values of sample size and dimension. It avoids choosing a limiting relationship such as $p=n^2$ or $p=e^{n/5}$ before evaluating an estimator.

Such results usually bound risk or failure probability in terms of $n$, $p$, noise, and structural complexity. They are especially useful in a [[High-Dimensional Statistical Regime]], where several quantities grow together and fixed-dimension limit theorems may conceal important logarithmic or dimensional costs. [[Concentration Inequality|Concentration inequalities]] provide the main probability controls used in these finite-sample arguments.
