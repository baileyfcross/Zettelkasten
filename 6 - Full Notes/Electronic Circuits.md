# Electronic Circuits

A combinatorial logic circuit connects AND, OR, and NOT gates without feedback loops. Each wire carries one of two states, interpreted as true and false or as one and zero, and each output wire represents a [[Compound Propositions|compound proposition]] in the circuit's inputs.

[[Boolean Algebra]] can replace that proposition with a simpler equivalent formula and thereby reduce the number of required gates. Circuits with feedback are different because they can store state and do not correspond to a single acyclic proposition.
