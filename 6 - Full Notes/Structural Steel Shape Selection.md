# Structural Steel Shape Selection

Steel shapes are selected by matching geometry to force path and fabrication needs. Wide-flange shapes are efficient as beams and columns; channels serve lighter flexural and edge-framing roles; angles work as braces, truss members, lintels, and connection elements; structural tees can form chords or strengthening pieces; and plates create stiffeners, gussets, and built-up members.

Hollow structural sections provide closed geometry with useful torsional behavior and are common in columns and bracing. Pipes, open-web joists, and built-up plate girders occupy more specialized roles. A designation communicates nominal depth or outside dimensions, weight, and sometimes wall thickness, but final selection must also consider availability, connection access, local slenderness, fireproofing, corrosion exposure, and architectural constraints.
