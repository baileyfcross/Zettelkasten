# Asimov Three Laws of Robotics

Isaac Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics are fictional priorities: avoid harming humans, obey human orders unless doing so causes harm, and preserve the robot when that does not conflict with the first two rules. A later Zeroth Law gave protection of humanity priority over protection of any individual.

The laws organize story conflicts, but they are not an implementable safety specification. A robot would need reliable perception, contextual understanding, prediction, and a workable definition of harm before it could apply them. Their continuing influence shows how fiction supplied an ethical vocabulary before robotics had mature professional institutions. [[Responsible Robotics Laws]] shifts the emphasis back to accountable human deployment.
