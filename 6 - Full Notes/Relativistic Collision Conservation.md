# Relativistic Collision Conservation

Relativistic collision conservation requires the total [[Four-Momentum]] before an isolated interaction to equal the total four-momentum afterward. Energy and each spatial momentum component are therefore conserved together as one covariant statement.

An inelastic collision can convert kinetic energy into internal energy, heat, or binding energy. The resulting composite may have an [[Invariant Mass]] greater than the sum of the original rest masses even though no four-momentum is lost. This replaces the Newtonian practice of conserving momentum while treating “lost” kinetic energy separately.

The [[Center-of-Momentum Frame]] makes the bookkeeping especially clear because total spatial momentum is zero on both sides. The total energy in that frame determines the invariant mass available to the products, constraining which reaction outcomes are possible.
