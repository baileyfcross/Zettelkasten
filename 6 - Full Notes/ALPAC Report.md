# ALPAC Report

The 1966 ALPAC report evaluated whether United States investment in machine translation was producing useful Russian-to-English output at acceptable cost. It concluded that human translation was then faster, cheaper, and better, and that fully automatic high-quality translation was not foreseeable in the near term.

Its scope reflected the needs of defense and government purchasers more than the long-term scientific value of language research. Funding had already declined, but the report reinforced the contraction of American machine-translation programs while encouraging work on computational linguistics and human translation aids. Its history shows how evaluation criteria and sponsor assumptions can redirect an entire research field.
