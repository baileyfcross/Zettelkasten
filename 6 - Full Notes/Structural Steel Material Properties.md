# Structural Steel Material Properties

Structural steel combines high strength with ductility, toughness, and a predictable elastic modulus. Its stress-strain response begins with an approximately linear elastic range, reaches yielding, develops substantial inelastic strain, and then rises toward tensile rupture. Yield strength governs many member strength checks, while tensile strength becomes important when fracture rather than yielding controls.

Design also depends on properties that strength tables alone do not capture. Residual stress from rolling and welding influences buckling, notch toughness affects resistance to brittle fracture, fatigue matters under repeated loading, and thermal expansion can create large restraint forces. Carbon and alloy content change strength, corrosion resistance, toughness, and weldability, so material selection must coordinate mechanical performance with fabrication.
