# Collective Empiricism

Collective empiricism is the production of practical knowledge when people share observations, methods, and interpretations from their own cases. Patient groups and self-tracking communities can compare experiences, refine questions, and identify patterns that no member's record could establish alone.

The process lies between isolated personal experimentation and formal research. It can generate hypotheses, expose device problems, and direct attention to neglected symptoms, while still facing selection bias, inconsistent measurement, and uncertain generalization.

Its strength comes from combining [[Patient Expertise]] with transparent discussion of how evidence was produced. Formal researchers and clinicians can learn from these questions without treating community agreement as automatic clinical proof.
