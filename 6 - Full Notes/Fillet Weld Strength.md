# Fillet Weld Strength

A fillet weld's effective throat is the shortest distance from its root to the face of the idealized triangular weld, so nominal resistance is based on effective throat area rather than leg area. Strength depends on electrode classification, weld size, effective length, and the direction of force relative to the weld axis, while base-metal limit states must also be checked.

Minimum weld size helps provide adequate heat input, and maximum size near a plate edge avoids an ambiguous or damaged edge condition. Effective length rules prevent very short welds from being credited unrealistically. Intermittent and end-return details affect force transfer and corrosion exposure. A longer weld is not always efficient if deformation makes load distribution nonuniform, so weld layout should carry force through a direct, balanced path.
