# Temperature Readings versus Intervals

An absolute temperature reading on the Fahrenheit scale requires both an offset and a scale change to become Celsius: Pryor's example uses $T_{\mathrm C}=(T_{\mathrm F}-32)/1.8$, taking 50 °F to 10 °C. A temperature difference does not need the 32-degree offset; an interval of 18 °F corresponds to 10 °C. The book also notes that one kelvin interval and one Celsius-degree interval are equal in size. Applying a multiplicative interval factor to an absolute reading therefore gives a wrong result. See [[Reciprocal Conversion Factors]].
