# Roof Snow Drift Loading

Roof snow is shaped by exposure, thermal condition, roof slope, importance, and local geometry rather than by ground snow depth alone. Wind can scour one region and deposit snow beside parapets, higher roofs, projections, or changes in elevation, producing a drift surcharge whose depth and extent may greatly exceed the balanced roof load.

Drift is a localized load pattern, so it can govern a beam, girder, joist, or connection even when the total snow on the roof is not maximal. Sliding snow and rain-on-snow can create additional cases, and partial loading can produce unfavorable moment patterns. The roof framing model should therefore place drift where accumulation is physically credible and evaluate the resulting reactions along the entire support path.
