# Geometry Dimensionality Reduction

A three-dimensional thermal problem can sometimes be represented in two dimensions when variation along the omitted direction is negligible. Likewise, a long uniform section may permit a one-dimensional treatment along its dominant heat-flow direction.

Axisymmetric geometry retains radial and axial variation while replacing a full body of revolution with a two-dimensional cross-section. Reduction lowers mesh and solver cost, but it is valid only when geometry, materials, sources, and [[Thermal Boundary Condition|conditions]] share the assumed invariance.
