# Proper Acceleration

Proper acceleration is the acceleration measured by an accelerometer carried with an object. It is evaluated in the object's instantaneous rest frame, where the object is momentarily at rest even if its velocity changes from one instant to the next.

Proper acceleration differs from the coordinate acceleration assigned by a distant inertial observer. For motion parallel to the velocity, the outside observer's coordinate acceleration is reduced relative to the proper acceleration by a factor involving $\gamma^3$. Consequently, continued finite thrust can maintain proper acceleration while the coordinate speed approaches but never reaches $c$.

The magnitude of the object's [[Four-Acceleration]] encodes proper acceleration independently of the inertial coordinates used to describe the motion. It is therefore the physically local measure of how strongly a worldline departs from inertial motion.
