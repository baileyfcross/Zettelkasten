# Cox's Theorem

Cox's theorem derives the probability rules from requirements for consistent reasoning about plausibility. Plausibility is represented numerically, equivalent ways of calculating the same claim must agree, and changes in support for a proposition must relate consistently to its negation and conjunctions.

Under these requirements, plausible reasoning follows normalization and the conjunction rule. A calculus that violates those rules contradicts at least one of the stated consistency requirements.
