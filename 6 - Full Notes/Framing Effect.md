# Framing Effect

The framing effect occurs when logically equivalent choices produce different behavior because they are described or contextualized differently. A late fee and an early discount can create different responses even when the payment schedule is identical.

Words, comparison options, reference prices, and surrounding cues all participate in the frame. Designers can use framing to make a decision legible, but should recognize that presentation can steer behavior independently of the underlying payoff.
