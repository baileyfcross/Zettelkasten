# Tetradic Color Scheme

A tetradic scheme uses four hues arranged as two complementary pairs. It offers greater variety than a triad but also creates more opportunities for colors to compete for attention.

Choosing one dominant family and controlling the temperature, value, and saturation of the remaining colors makes the structure usable. The scheme is most effective when its pairs support distinct environmental roles rather than being distributed randomly.
