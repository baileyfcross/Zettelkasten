# Composite Beam Action

A composite beam uses shear connectors to make a concrete floor slab and steel beam act together in bending. Under positive moment, the slab supplies a broad compression region while much of the steel section works in tension, increasing strength and stiffness compared with the bare beam. The steel web continues to resist the primary vertical shear.

Horizontal interface shear would otherwise make the slab slip relative to the beam. Headed studs transfer that shear and determine whether action is full or partial; adding studs beyond the force that either material can develop does not add useful flexural capacity. Effective slab width, transformed-section properties, deck orientation, concrete strength, steel strength, and construction sequence all shape the composite resistance and deflection.
