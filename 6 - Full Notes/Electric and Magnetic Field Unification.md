# Electric and Magnetic Field Unification

Electric and magnetic fields are observer-dependent components of one electromagnetic field. A charge that is stationary relative to one observer can produce a purely electric field for that observer, while another observer moving relative to the charge measures both electric and magnetic components.

The effect can be seen with a current-carrying wire. Differences in length contraction alter the measured densities of moving electrons and stationary lattice charges, so a force described as magnetic in one frame can be described as electrostatic in another. The measurable force remains consistent because the fields and the charge's velocity transform together.

The [[Electromagnetic Field Tensor]] packages this unity into a covariant object. A [[Lorentz Transformation]] changes its electric–magnetic decomposition without changing the underlying field or the covariant form of the [[Lorentz Force Law]].
