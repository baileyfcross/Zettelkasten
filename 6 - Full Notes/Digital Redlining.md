# Digital Redlining

Digital redlining is the use of data-driven classification to produce or reinforce exclusion from opportunities and services. Personal and inferred data can divide people into risk or value categories that affect health, employment, housing, credit, insurance, or consumer treatment.

The discrimination may not name a protected group directly. Location, purchasing behavior, health patterns, and social connections can act as proxies, and linking several datasets can make a classification more consequential than any single record.

Self-tracking expands the possible inputs to such systems. Preventing harm requires limits on secondary use, tests for unequal outcomes, visibility into consequential profiles, and a way for affected people to contest the classification.
