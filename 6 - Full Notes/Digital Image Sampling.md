# Digital Image Sampling

A digital image turns a continuous visual scene into measurements on a discrete grid. Each sample records the scene over a finite area rather than at an infinitely precise point, so the sampling density limits the spatial detail that the representation can retain.

Increasing width and height creates more samples and therefore more potential detail, but it also increases uncompressed pixel count, decoded memory, and usually encoded size. Resizing for delivery is therefore a performance decision as well as a visual one. See [[Display Dimensions and Decode Cost]] and [[Raster Scaling and Resolution Dependence]].
