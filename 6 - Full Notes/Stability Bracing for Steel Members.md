# Stability Bracing for Steel Members

Stability bracing restrains the displacement or twist associated with a potential buckling mode. Point bracing acts at discrete locations, while continuous bracing restrains along a length. Beams may need compression-flange or torsional restraint, columns need lateral restraint about relevant axes, and beam-columns can require a combination matched to their interaction of axial force and bending.

A brace must possess both strength and stiffness. A very flexible brace may attract little nominal force yet permit enough movement for the member to buckle, while a connection located away from the intended restrained point may introduce twist rather than suppress it. Deck, joists, cross frames, diaphragms, and secondary framing can serve as bracing only when their complete force path and attachments are designed for that role.
