# XQUATH and Average-XEB Hardness

The Linear Cross-Entropy Quantum Threshold Assumption (XQUATH) states, in effect, that no polynomial-time classical algorithm can predict an ideal random-circuit output probability with a non-negligible mean-squared advantage over the uniform baseline. Reductions use that assumption to argue that achieving nontrivial average XEB is classically hard.

For the paper's self-averaging estimator, the probability-prediction advantage in the XQUATH expression is exactly its average XEB. Because the partitioned estimator retains positive average XEB efficiently in one-dimensional and modified broader architectures, it supplies a counterexample to correspondingly modified XQUATH claims. This does not settle every random-circuit hardness conjecture; it shows that a useful conjecture must exclude the structure exploited by [[Self-Averaging XEB Spoofing]].
