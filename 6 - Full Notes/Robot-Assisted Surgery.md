# Robot-Assisted Surgery

Robot-assisted surgery uses a surgeon-controlled system to translate console input into precise movement of instruments inside a patient. Sensors and articulated arms can improve reach, dexterity, visualization, and the mapping between the surgeon's hand and the operative field without making the machine autonomous.

The label robot can encourage assumptions of superior outcomes that must be tested rather than inferred. Equipment purchase, consumables, service contracts, training, and procedure volume shape institutional incentives. The appropriate comparison is clinical outcome and total risk against alternative techniques, not the technological sophistication of the device alone.
