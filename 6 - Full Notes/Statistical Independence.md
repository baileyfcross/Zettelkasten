# Statistical Independence

Variables are statistically independent when their joint distribution factors into their marginal distributions: $P(X,Y)=P(X)P(Y)$. Learning one variable's value then does not change the distribution assigned to the other.

Independence is a strong claim and should not be substituted automatically for conditional independence. Two measurements can be dependent overall while becoming independent after the common state that influences both is known.

The source connects independence to causal structure and symmetry rather than treating it as a pattern justified only by observed frequencies. Determining whether factors operate independently requires considering the collective conditions that generate their outcomes.
