# Autonomous Vehicle Liability

Autonomous vehicle liability shifts attention from a driver's momentary mistake toward the companies that designed, manufactured, supplied, programmed, maintained, or operated the system. A crash may involve software, sensors, maps, remote services, human supervision, and road conditions rather than one visible driver.

This distribution can improve accountability for systemic defects, but large and uncertain judgments can also slow deployment. A workable regime needs records of system state, defined control responsibilities, inspection standards, and rules for transitions between automated and human operation. Liability is therefore part of the vehicle architecture, not merely an after-the-fact legal issue.
