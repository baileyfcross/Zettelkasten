# Logistic Model Deviance

Logistic model deviance is a likelihood-based measure of discrepancy between a fitted logistic model and a saturated model. Residual deviance can be compared with null deviance to assess how much the predictors improve on an intercept-only specification.

Differences in deviance between nested models support likelihood-ratio comparisons when their assumptions apply. Deviance is not an absolute measure of causal quality and should be interpreted with degrees of freedom, diagnostics, and scientific plausibility.
