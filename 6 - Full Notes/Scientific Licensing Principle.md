# Scientific Licensing Principle

The scientific licensing principle holds that legal burdens on disseminating, sharing, using, and reusing a research compendium should be minimized and imposed only for a strong reason. The objective is to align legal permissions with scientific norms of verification and cumulative inquiry.

Applying the principle means choosing licenses appropriate to each object, avoiding unnecessary restrictions, and planning permissions before collaborators or institutions create incompatible claims. It favors broad access but does not erase privacy, confidentiality, patent, or third-party constraints. Those limits should be explicit so readers know what part of the research can actually be reused.
