# Patient Expertise

Patient expertise is knowledge developed through living with and managing a condition. It includes detailed awareness of symptoms, routines, device limitations, treatment effects, and practical constraints that a professional observes only intermittently.

Self-tracking can make this expertise more communicable by preserving episodes and comparisons. Patients may identify questions that research has not asked or design modifications that make a device usable in daily life. Their knowledge complements clinical training rather than simply replacing it.

Recognizing patient expertise requires access to the relevant data and a way to bring interpretation into care. Dismissing a patient as an illegitimate user of data produced by their own body prevents potentially useful learning and weakens shared decisions.
