# Interpupillary Distance

Interpupillary distance is the distance between the centers of a person's pupils. A binocular viewing system uses it to align lenses and eye images with the actual positions of the viewer's eyes.

An incorrect setting can move an eye away from the intended optical axis, producing distortion, eye strain, headaches, or difficulty seeing through a small exit pupil. It can also change the effective separation of the two rendered views, alter [[Vergence]], and make a virtual object appear nearer or farther than intended.

Because IPD varies among people, a stereoscopic [[Head-Worn Display]] needs a supported range and a reliable adjustment or calibration process. Mechanical adjustment can move each lens and display together; software adjustment can shift rendered eye views, but it cannot by itself correct every physical lens-alignment problem.
