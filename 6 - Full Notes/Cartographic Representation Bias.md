# Cartographic Representation Bias

Cartographic representation bias arises when a map's projection, boundaries, classes, colors, symbols, omissions, or commercial priorities make one interpretation easier than another. A state-by-state election map can visually exaggerate sparsely populated territory, while broad choropleth intervals can hide meaningful variation.

Bias can be accidental or strategic. Paid listings may crowd out public information, and institutional boundary changes can alter who appears to face a flood or political risk. Because maps simplify reality while carrying authority, readers should ask what was selected, what was excluded, which quantity controls each mark, and whose decision the representation supports.
