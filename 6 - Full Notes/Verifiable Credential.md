# Verifiable Credential

A verifiable credential is a digitally signed statement made by an issuer about a subject and presented to a verifier. Examples include proof of employment, education, licensure, or age. The signature lets the verifier check authenticity and integrity, while a status mechanism can show whether the issuer has revoked the credential.

The lifecycle has three distinct roles: an issuer creates the credential, a holder stores and presents it, and a verifier decides whether the issuer and claim satisfy its policy. Selective disclosure can reveal only the needed claim rather than a whole identity document. Open data models improve interoperability, but semantic trust still depends on governance—who the issuer is, what the claim means, when it expires, and how compromise or revocation is handled.
