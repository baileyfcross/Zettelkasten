# Four-Acceleration

Four-acceleration is the proper-time derivative of [[Four-Velocity]], $A=dU/d\tau$. It combines the change in an object's temporal and spatial velocity components into one [[Four-Vector]].

Because the norm $U\cdot U=c^2$ is constant, differentiating it gives $U\cdot A=0$. In Minkowski geometry this orthogonality means that acceleration changes the direction of four-velocity while preserving its norm; it should not be interpreted by importing an ordinary Euclidean right-angle picture without regard to the metric signature.

In the object's instantaneous rest frame, the spatial part of four-acceleration is the [[Proper Acceleration]] and its time component vanishes. Other observers assign components containing powers of the [[Lorentz Factor]], consistent with the direction-dependent [[Relativistic Acceleration Transformation]].
