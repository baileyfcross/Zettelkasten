# Effect Size

An effect size quantifies the magnitude of a difference or relationship independently of the sample's p-value. It may be expressed as a standardized mean difference, a raw mean difference, a difference in proportions, a correlation, or a model-specific measure.

Power calculations require an assumed effect size because a study cannot be sized for significance in the abstract. Plausible values should come from scientific relevance, prior evidence, or a transparent sensitivity range rather than from the observed study result alone.

In the source's two-group examples, the raw difference in sample means is the observed effect. Reporting it with a [[Confidence Interval]] prevents a small p-value from being mistaken for a scientifically important result when a large sample detects a negligible difference.
