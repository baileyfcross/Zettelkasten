# Axiomatic Research Method

The axiomatic research method studies an information-system problem by defining objects and assumptions, then deriving conclusions through deductive proof. It is valuable when exhaustive empirical testing is impossible, as with an enormous or infinite space of database states, inputs, or executions.

An axiomatic investigation can establish whether an algorithm exists, specify its behavior, compare expressive power, prove termination or correctness, characterize semantics, analyze efficiency, or guide construction and implementation. The strength of the conclusion depends on the clarity and appropriateness of the definitions and axioms.

Deduction and empirical work can complement each other. A proof establishes a property under formal assumptions, while experiments test whether an implementation meets the specification and whether the assumptions describe the practical setting.
