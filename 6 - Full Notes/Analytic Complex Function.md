# Analytic Complex Function

An analytic complex function has a complex derivative throughout a neighborhood of each point in its domain. Because the derivative must be independent of the direction from which the increment approaches zero, complex differentiability imposes stronger conditions than ordinary differentiability of two real variables.

Writing the function as real and imaginary components produces the Cauchy-Riemann equations. Where the needed derivatives are continuous, satisfying those equations establishes analyticity and gives powerful consequences such as local power-series representations.
