# Aharonov-Bohm Effect

The Aharonov-Bohm effect is a phase shift produced by an electromagnetic potential even when the charged particles travel through regions where the local electric and magnetic fields vanish. It shows that the potential cannot always be treated as a disposable calculation aid with no physical consequences.

In the electromagnetic description, fields are derivatives of the [[Electromagnetic Four-Potential]]. A region can therefore have zero local field while the potential retains global information about enclosed electromagnetic structure. Paths passing on different sides can accumulate different phases and interfere differently when recombined.

The effect complements [[Gauge Invariance in Electromagnetism]]: the potential is not unique, but gauge-invariant phase differences can be observable. What matters is not an arbitrary potential value at one point but a relational quantity preserved under the allowed gauge transformation.
