# Ordinary and Singular Points of a Differential Equation

For a linear differential equation, an ordinary point is a location where the coefficient functions remain analytic after the equation is placed in standard form. An ordinary power series centered there can be expected to represent local solutions.

A singular point occurs where one or more normalized coefficients cease to be analytic. Regular singular points still permit a controlled generalized power series, while more severe irregular singularities require other local methods and can produce qualitatively different behavior.
