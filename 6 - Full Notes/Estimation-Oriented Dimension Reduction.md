# Estimation-Oriented Dimension Reduction

Estimation-oriented dimension reduction searches for a low-dimensional representation that is useful for a specified inferential or predictive task. This differs from unsupervised reduction, which may preserve the largest overall variation without preserving the directions that distinguish outcomes.

For example, principal components can summarize the dominant variation in measurements yet fail to separate classes, while a supervised discriminant subspace can expose the distinction directly. The best representation is therefore relative to the target: a subspace useful for reconstruction is not automatically the best one for classification or regression.
