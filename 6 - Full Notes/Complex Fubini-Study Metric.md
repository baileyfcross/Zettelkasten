# Complex Fubini-Study Metric

Optical quantum circuits naturally mix real parameters, such as phase and Kerr strengths, with complex parameters, such as displacement and squeezing amplitudes. A metric written only for a real parameter vector must therefore be transformed into a basis containing each complex parameter and its conjugate.

After this change of basis, the Fubini-Study tensor becomes a [[Hermitian Matrix|Hermitian]] metric $f$ rather than a real symmetric one. It reduces to the usual real Fubini-Study metric when every parameter is real, so the complex form is an extension rather than a separate geometry.

The Hermitian structure ensures that the metric represents real infinitesimal distances while retaining the conjugate relationships required by complex parameters. It is the matrix used to precondition complex quantum-circuit gradients.
