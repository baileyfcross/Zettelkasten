# Euler Buckling of Steel Columns

Euler buckling describes the elastic instability of an ideal straight, homogeneous column under concentric compression. For a pin-ended member, critical load is proportional to elastic modulus and the least moment of inertia and inversely proportional to the square of length. End restraint is represented by replacing physical length with effective length.

The inverse-square relationship explains why a long column can fail at stress far below material yield. Real steel columns contain residual stress, initial crookedness, load eccentricity, and inelastic behavior, so design strength curves modify the ideal result and transition between yielding-dominated short columns and elastic-buckling-dominated slender columns. Euler theory remains the conceptual base for understanding how stiffness, bracing, axis selection, and unbraced length govern compression strength.
