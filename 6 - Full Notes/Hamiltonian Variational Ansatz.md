# Hamiltonian Variational Ansatz

A Hamiltonian variational ansatz decomposes a many-body Hamiltonian into terms and turns their alternating time evolutions into a parameterized quantum circuit. If the Hamiltonian is written as a sum of components, one block applies an exponential generated by each component, and several blocks repeat this sequence with independently trainable evolution times.

This construction connects variational optimization to simulated Hamiltonian dynamics. For local lattice Hamiltonians whose components are sums of commuting local Pauli strings, the circuit can be interpreted as a piecewise time-dependent local evolution. That interpretation supports the [[Floquet-Magnus Approximation of a Hamiltonian Variational Ansatz]] used to analyze its gradients.

An HVA is not automatically trainable. Unrestricted random parameters can produce [[Barren Plateau Gradient Scaling]], whereas an [[Inverse-Qubit Hamiltonian Ansatz Parameter Budget]] keeps the circuit in a short-time regime with provably large gradients. The restriction can reduce expressivity, motivating the [[Repeated-Parameter Hamiltonian Variational Ansatz]].
