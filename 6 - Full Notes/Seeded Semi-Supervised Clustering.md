# Seeded Semi-Supervised Clustering

Seeded semi-supervised clustering begins with a small set of labeled or trusted example points for some groups. Seeds initialize representatives or influence assignments while unlabeled observations provide the remaining structure.

Seeds can align clusters with known categories using far less labeling than supervised classification. Incorrect, unrepresentative, or uneven seeds can steer the entire partition, so sensitivity to seed choice should be tested.
