# Battlefield Robot Communications

Battlefield robots depend on communications for commands, position, video, sensor data, coordination, and oversight. The radio environment can be crowded by encrypted traffic, radar, GPS, jamming, and competing systems, while damaged infrastructure and hostile interference make laboratory performance unreliable.

Encryption protects control and imagery but adds processing and bandwidth overhead. A platform must define what it can safely do when contact degrades, who can authenticate commands, and how captured hardware or intercepted links are contained. Communication resilience is part of autonomy because disconnection changes which decisions remain possible.
