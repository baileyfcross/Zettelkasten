# Magnetic Resonance Imaging

Magnetic resonance imaging places hydrogen nuclei in a strong magnetic field, excites them with radio-frequency pulses, and detects signals as their magnetization relaxes. Gradient fields encode position, allowing reconstruction of soft-tissue images. MRI does not use ionizing radiation, but the strong magnetic field imposes strict safety constraints on ferromagnetic objects and implants.
