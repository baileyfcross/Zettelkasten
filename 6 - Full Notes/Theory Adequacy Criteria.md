# Theory Adequacy Criteria

Theory adequacy criteria evaluate whether an explanation is useful as a scientific account rather than merely compatible with one observation. Testability requires implications that evidence could challenge, and consistency requires the claims to cohere internally and with established facts unless the conflict is explicitly resolved.

Scope asks how broadly the theory organizes phenomena; predictive power asks whether it successfully anticipates outcomes; fruitfulness asks whether it generates new questions, findings, or applications. Parsimony favors the account that explains the evidence without unnecessary assumptions or machinery.

The criteria can trade off. A narrow theory may predict precisely, while a broad theory may explain more at the cost of qualifications. Evaluation should state which virtues matter for the research problem instead of collapsing adequacy into a single score.
