# Quantum Link Models and D-Theory

D-theory formulates a lattice field theory with discrete quantum degrees of freedom and recovers the target continuum behavior through dimensional reduction from an additional short dimension. Its gauge theories use quantum link models, where finite-dimensional generalized spins replace the continuous classical variables of Wilson-type lattice links while preserving exact gauge symmetry.

The representation can map naturally to quantum hardware. A spin-$1/2$ U(1) quantum link is a single qubit. The simplest SU(2) quantum link uses a four-dimensional embedding representation that can be stored in two qubits per link, and an exact dual description can sometimes reduce it to one qubit-valued height variable. Related spin ladders regularize CP$(N-1)$ field theories and reproduce vacuum-angle or finite-density behavior through the ladder geometry.

This approach is valuable because its fundamental variables are already quantum and finite-dimensional. It can support both analog simulators and gate-based circuits without first approximating an infinite local Hilbert space in the same way as a conventional field discretization. [[Linear Gauge Protection in Quantum Simulators]] addresses how such models can remain close to their intended gauge sector on imperfect hardware.
