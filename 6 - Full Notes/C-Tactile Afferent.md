# C-Tactile Afferent

A C-tactile afferent is a slowly conducting sensory fiber in hairy skin that responds preferentially to gentle, slow stroking. Its firing rate correlates with reported pleasantness, linking a class of mechanical skin stimulation to affective rather than primarily discriminative touch.

These afferents are absent from [[Glabrous Skin]] and conduct too slowly to guide the rapid corrections needed during precise manipulation. Their properties support a distinction between touch used to identify and control objects and touch that contributes to comfort, bonding, and interpersonal experience.
