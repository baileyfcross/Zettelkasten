# Thomson Effect

The Thomson effect is heat released or absorbed along a single current-carrying material that also has a temperature gradient. Its direction changes when current reverses, so the source treats it as reversible. This distributed heat exchange differs from the junction-localized [[Peltier Effect]] and from irreversible [[Joule Heating]]. It matters when accounting for thermal flow through thermoelectric legs.
