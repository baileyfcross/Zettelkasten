# Mechanoreceptor Receptive Field

A mechanoreceptor receptive field is the region of skin within which stimulation can change the activity of the receptor's associated sensory fiber. Small type-I fields can be only a few millimeters across, whereas type-II fields may extend across much larger areas.

Dense receptors with small fields support fine localization and spatial discrimination at the fingertips. Larger fields integrate events over broader regions but cannot preserve the same positional detail. Receptive-field geometry therefore connects the anatomy of a [[Cutaneous Mechanoreceptor]] to behavioral measures such as [[Two-Point Threshold]].
