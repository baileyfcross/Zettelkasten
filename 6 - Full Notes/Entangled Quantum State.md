# Entangled Quantum State

An entangled pure state cannot be written as a tensor product of independent subsystem states. Its amplitudes describe the composite system directly, so assigning a complete local pure state to each component loses information present in the whole.

Entanglement produces correlations that persist across different measurement bases, but it does not permit faster-than-light communication. Each observer's local outcome can remain random even when the joint outcomes are strongly correlated. [[EPR Pair Measurement Correlations]] gives the simplest two-qubit example.
