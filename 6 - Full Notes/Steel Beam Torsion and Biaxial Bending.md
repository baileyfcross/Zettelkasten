# Steel Beam Torsion and Biaxial Bending

Load applied away from a beam's shear center creates torsion in addition to bending. Open sections resist twist through Saint-Venant torsion and warping, which can generate longitudinal stresses and forces at restraints. Closed hollow sections usually have greater torsional efficiency, so section choice and load placement can be more effective than attempting to strengthen an open shape after eccentricity is established.

Biaxial bending occurs when moment acts about both principal axes, as with eccentric connections, sloped framing, or lateral load on a gravity member. Design combines the resulting strong- and weak-axis demands with any axial force. Bracing and end details must be consistent with assumed torsional restraint, because preventing twist can increase warping forces that the connection must carry.
