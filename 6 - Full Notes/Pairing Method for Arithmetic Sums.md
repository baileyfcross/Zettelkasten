# Pairing Method for Arithmetic Sums

The pairing method for arithmetic sums combines terms equally far from opposite ends of an [[Arithmetic Progression]]. Each pair has the same total.

For (1+2+\cdots+100), pairing (1) with (100), (2) with (99), and so on gives fifty pairs of (101), so the sum is (50\cdot101=5050). Equivalently, write the sum forward and backward, add the two rows to obtain one hundred copies of (101), and divide by two. The method exposes structure that direct accumulation hides.
