# Allowable Strength Design

Allowable Strength Design compares service-level load effects with nominal strength divided by a safety factor. The same underlying nominal member strength used in LRFD can support an ASD check, but the two methods place safety on different sides of the comparison: LRFD factors loads and reduces resistance, whereas ASD combines unfactored service loads and reduces resistance by an allowable-strength factor.

ASD safety factors vary with the governing failure mode but do not assign separate amplification factors to every load type. This makes the method familiar and convenient for many building designs, yet it produces less uniform reliability across different ratios of dead, live, and environmental load. A project should use one method consistently in its load combinations, member checks, connection design information, and construction documents.
