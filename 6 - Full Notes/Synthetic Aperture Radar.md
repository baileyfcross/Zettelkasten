# Synthetic Aperture Radar

Synthetic-aperture radar, or SAR, is an active remote-sensing method that transmits microwave pulses from a moving aircraft or spacecraft and combines the returned measurements to obtain finer spatial information. Its frequencies are comparatively insensitive to visible light and many weather conditions, allowing observation when ordinary optical imagery is obscured.

SAR can compare terrain before and after a storm or earthquake, monitor polar ice and land use, and support defense observation. The resulting measurements still require processing and interpretation; all-weather sensing improves availability but does not by itself explain what caused a detected change.
