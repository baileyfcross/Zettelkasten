# Game Music Loop

A game music loop repeats an audio region so a finite recording can cover gameplay of uncertain duration. Synchronized loops preserve a shared musical clock across files or layers, whereas nonsynchronized loops can prioritize an inaudible seam without maintaining alignment with other material.

A seamless boundary depends on both composition and editing. The end must lead naturally back to the beginning in tempo, harmony, density, and phrase shape; the waveform should meet at compatible zero crossings and continue in the same direction. Reverb tails can be moved to the front of a loop, and the final compressed asset must be tested in the game because encoding can change the boundary.
