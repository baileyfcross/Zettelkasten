# Integral Bounds from Inequalities

An integral can be bounded by replacing a difficult integrand with functions known to lie above or below it on the entire integration interval. Integrating the pointwise inequality preserves the ordering when the limits have their usual orientation.

The usefulness of the estimate depends on choosing a bound that is both integrable and sufficiently close to the original function. A crude uniform maximum may be easy to integrate, while retaining more of the integrand's variable dependence often produces a sharper bound.
