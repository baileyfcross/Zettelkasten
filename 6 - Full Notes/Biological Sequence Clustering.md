# Biological Sequence Clustering

Biological sequence clustering groups DNA, RNA, or protein strings from alignment scores, edit distances, shared motifs, k-mer profiles, or probabilistic models. The appropriate representation depends on whether the target is homology, function, taxonomy, or another relationship.

Large sequence collections require approximate indexing or representative-based methods because all-pairs alignment is costly. Thresholds translate continuous sequence similarity into groups and can split gradual evolutionary relationships into artificial boundaries.
