# Quantum-Limited Parametric Amplification for Qubit Readout

The first amplifier in a qubit-readout chain largely determines the system noise because later amplifier noise is divided by the preceding gain. A Josephson parametric amplifier placed at cryogenic temperature can provide high gain with noise near the quantum limit before the signal reaches a noisier semiconductor amplifier.

Parametric amplification mixes a weak signal with a strong pump through a Josephson nonlinearity. A phase-preserving amplifier amplifies both quadratures but must add at least half a photon of noise associated with an idler mode. A phase-sensitive amplifier can amplify one quadrature while deamplifying the other and can evade that added-noise requirement for the selected quadrature.

Resonant Josephson parametric amplifiers offer low noise but limited bandwidth and saturation power. Traveling-wave parametric amplifiers distribute nonlinearity along a line to obtain broader bandwidth and larger dynamic range; phase matching is required so the generated field adds coherently. These devices improve the separation of the I-Q distributions used in [[Dispersive Superconducting-Qubit Readout]].
