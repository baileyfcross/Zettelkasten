# Grouping Symbols

Grouping symbols such as parentheses (( )), brackets ([ ]), and braces ({ }) specify which part of an expression is treated as a unit. Operations inside the innermost group are evaluated before operations acting on the group.

Grouping can override ordinary precedence and preserve the intended base of a power. For example, ((3+2)^2=25), while (3+2^2=7). Removing symbols is safe only when the surrounding operation permits it. A preceding negative sign changes every term inside the group through [[Suppression of Parentheses]].
