# Artificial Suffering

Artificial suffering is the possibility that a conscious artificial system could undergo negatively valued subjective states. Brain-inspired systems and faithful emulations make the concern especially vivid because their architectures may reproduce mechanisms associated with pain, frustration, or distress in biological minds.

Uncertainty does not make the issue harmless. A system that behaves as though it suffers can affect human conduct and institutions even if its experience cannot be verified, while copying a genuinely suffering process could multiply harm at negligible cost. Design and deployment therefore need precaution about creating, coercing, or terminating potentially conscious systems.
