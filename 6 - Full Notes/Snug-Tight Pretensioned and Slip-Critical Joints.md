# Snug-Tight Pretensioned and Slip-Critical Joints

A snug-tight joint brings connected plies into firm contact and transfers shear by bolt bearing after any available slip. A pretensioned joint applies a specified clamping force and is used where cyclic loading, tension in certain high-strength bolts, or other conditions demand it. A slip-critical joint uses pretension plus a qualified faying surface so service-level force is transferred by friction without slip.

Pretension does not automatically increase the post-slip bearing strength; it changes joint behavior before bearing begins. Slip-critical resistance depends on clamping force, surface class, number of slip planes, and hole type. The designer must specify the joint category and required surface preparation, while installation and inspection verify the intended pretension through an approved method such as turn-of-nut, calibrated wrench, tension-control bolts, or direct tension indicators.
