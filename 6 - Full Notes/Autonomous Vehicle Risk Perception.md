# Autonomous Vehicle Risk Perception

People evaluate autonomous-vehicle risk differently from ordinary driving risk. Familiar hazards under apparent personal control can feel safer than rare machine failures, even when automation lowers total deaths or injuries.

A single unusual robotic crash can therefore influence trust and regulation more than a much larger background of human-caused collisions. Naming and imagery also matter: robocar, self-driving car, and driver assistance imply different degrees of agency. Adoption depends on making comparative risk, system limits, and control handoffs understandable rather than presenting safety only as an aggregate statistic.
