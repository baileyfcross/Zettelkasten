# Contextual and Behavioral Authentication

Authentication can consider more than a memorized secret or physical token. The source describes location and behavior as signals that can support an identity decision: an expected geographic context, typing pattern, device interaction, or other habitual behavior may increase confidence, while a substantial deviation can justify another challenge.

These signals are generally contextual evidence rather than replacements for strong credentials. Behavior can change, location can be misleading, and both can create privacy concerns. A sound design combines them with independent [[Authentication Factor Categories|authentication factors]], applies proportional checks, and avoids treating an opaque risk score as unquestionable proof. [[Passkey Authentication]] supplies phishing-resistant cryptographic evidence that contextual checks can complement.
