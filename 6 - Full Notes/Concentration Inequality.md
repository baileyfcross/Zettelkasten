# Concentration Inequality

A concentration inequality bounds how far a random quantity can deviate from its typical value at a finite sample size. Unlike an asymptotic convergence statement, it supplies an explicit tail probability that can be inserted directly into a risk proof.

Gaussian concentration, for example, controls a Lipschitz function of a Gaussian vector by combining its Lipschitz constant with the noise scale. In high-dimensional statistics these inequalities replace many fixed-$p$ limit arguments and make simultaneous control over large model collections possible, though the price often includes a complexity term such as $\log p$.
