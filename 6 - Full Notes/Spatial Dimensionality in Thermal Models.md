# Spatial Dimensionality in Thermal Models

Heat-transfer geometry may be expressed in one, two, or three Cartesian dimensions, or in cylindrical and spherical coordinates when those systems match the shape. The chosen dimensionality determines which temperature gradients and heat-flux components the model can represent.

A lower-dimensional approximation is justified when variation in an omitted direction is negligible compared with the dominant heat-flow directions. This physical argument, not computational convenience alone, is the basis of [[Geometry Dimensionality Reduction|dimensionality reduction]].
