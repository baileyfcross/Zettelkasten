# T1 and T2 Superconducting-Qubit Decoherence

$T_1$ characterizes energy relaxation: an excited qubit exchanges energy with its environment and returns toward thermal equilibrium. In the qubit frame this is driven by transverse noise near the transition frequency, because such fluctuations connect $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$.

$T_2$ characterizes loss of phase coherence in the transverse plane. It includes the phase loss caused by energy relaxation plus pure dephasing from longitudinal frequency fluctuations. For exponential Markovian decay, the rates satisfy $1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+1/T_\phi$, so energy relaxation alone bounds $T_2$ by $2T_1$.

The simple rate equation is not universal. Low-frequency $1/f$ noise can produce a Gaussian or otherwise nonexponential envelope, making a single $T_\phi$ dependent on the experiment and fitting model. [[Ramsey and Hahn-Echo Coherence Characterization]] separates free-evolution sensitivity from refocused coherence, while [[Superconducting-Qubit Noise Spectroscopy]] connects decay to the underlying spectral density.
