# Compact Noncompact and Slender Steel Sections

Flexural sections are classified by the width-to-thickness ratios of their compression elements. A compact section can develop and sustain its plastic moment before local buckling. A noncompact section can reach first yield but local buckling prevents full plastic capacity, while a slender element buckles elastically and requires a larger strength reduction.

Flanges are generally unstiffened elements with one free longitudinal edge, whereas an I-shape web is stiffened along both longitudinal edges. Their limiting ratios differ and depend on elastic modulus and yield stress. The most unfavorable element controls the section classification for a given bending axis. Classification connects plate geometry to usable moment strength and is distinct from the lateral-torsional buckling of the member as a whole.
