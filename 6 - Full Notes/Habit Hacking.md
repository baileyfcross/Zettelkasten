# Habit Hacking

Habit hacking uses observation and environmental design to create a new routine or change an old one. Instead of relying only on willpower, it identifies the cue that starts a behavior, the routine that follows, and the reward that helps sustain it.

A new action can be made specific and easy, then attached to an existing cue. Flossing one tooth can lower the barrier to beginning; doing an exercise immediately after making coffee can chain the new behavior to an established routine. Tracking can show consistency, reveal disruptive circumstances, and provide feedback while the routine is forming.

Habits are embedded in social and material life. Moving bedtime may also require changes to commuting, dinner, or childcare, and illness or travel can interrupt a streak. A useful project therefore adapts the environment and expectations rather than treating every lapse as an isolated failure of discipline.
