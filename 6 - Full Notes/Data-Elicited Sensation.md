# Data-Elicited Sensation

A data-elicited sensation is a bodily or environmental awareness cultivated by repeatedly comparing a recorded signal with felt experience. The record acts as a prosthetic for attention: it does not replace sensation, but helps a person notice and name something that was previously difficult to perceive.

Someone might compare glucose readings with feelings of energy or shakiness, or use repeated ovulation measurements while attending to bodily cues. Over time, the person can form hypotheses about the circumstances that accompany the signal and sometimes learn to anticipate it.

The process requires movement in both directions. Data guides attention toward sensation, while sensation helps question or contextualize the data. Treating the sensor as automatically correct would remove the interpretive learning that makes this practice valuable.
