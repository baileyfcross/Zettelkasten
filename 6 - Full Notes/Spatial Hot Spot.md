# Spatial Hot Spot

A spatial hot spot is an area where the concentration of an event or attribute is substantially higher than outside it. John Snow's map of cholera deaths around the Broad Street water pump illustrates how geocoded cases can reveal a localized cluster and direct investigation toward a possible source.

Hot spots need not be circular. Roads, transit lines, closing-time activity around a bar, and environmental boundaries can produce lines, rings, or irregular shapes. A visible concentration is a lead rather than proof: significance tests, alternative explanations, and field evidence help separate meaningful clusters from a [[Spatial Chance Pattern]].
