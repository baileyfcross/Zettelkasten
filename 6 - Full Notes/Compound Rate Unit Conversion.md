# Compound Rate Unit Conversion

A rate written as a quotient must convert numerator and denominator in positions that leave the target units. For example, a volume-per-day value needs a volume ratio and a day-to-second ratio oriented inversely because time sits below the fraction line. Pryor's flow, velocity, power, and fuel-efficiency examples all use this same unit algebra even though their physical meanings differ. Checking cancellation prevents a factor of 60 or 3,600 from being accidentally applied in the wrong direction. See [[Conversion-Factor Chains]].
