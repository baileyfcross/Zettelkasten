# Positive Result Publication Bias

Positive result publication bias occurs when significant, consistent findings are more likely to be pursued, reported, or published than null, inconsistent, or ambiguous findings. The visible literature can then exaggerate effect sizes and conceal limits on generalizability.

The bias is not limited to deliberate misconduct. Flexible stopping, selective outcomes, and motivated judgments about which studies are successful can all make supportive evidence easier to retain. A preregistered [[Replication Protocol Standardization|replication protocol]], adequate [[Replication Power Planning]], and inclusion of all attempted results help separate study quality from whether the outcome is attractive.
