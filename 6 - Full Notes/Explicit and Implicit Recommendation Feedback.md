# Explicit and Implicit Recommendation Feedback

Explicit recommendation feedback is a deliberate statement such as a rating, review, like, or ranked preference. Implicit feedback is inferred from behavior such as a click, purchase, skip, replay, dwell time, or completion. Both describe user-item interaction, but they differ in effort, ambiguity, and scale.

Explicit signals are easier to interpret but costly enough that users provide relatively few of them. Implicit signals arrive continuously and make a [[Streaming Recommender System]] responsive, yet the same action can have several explanations: a long view may indicate interest, distraction, or an unattended device. A reliable system evaluates behavioral evidence in context instead of treating every recorded action as an equally strong preference.
