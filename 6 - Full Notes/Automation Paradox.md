# Automation Paradox

The automation paradox is the tendency for increasingly reliable automation to reduce human attention and practiced skill while leaving the person responsible for rare situations the automation cannot handle. Routine performance improves, but the operator may be least prepared precisely when an unusual and dangerous failure demands rapid intervention.

Greater complexity can lower the frequency of incidents while increasing their severity. Confusing alerts, incomplete mental models, and conflict between machine logic and human intuition can turn a recoverable fault into a cascade. Safe automation therefore needs legible state, appropriate authority, realistic training, and handoff designs that preserve the operator's ability to diagnose and act.
