# Financial Sentiment as an Asset-Pricing Signal

Financial sentiment analysis treats investors' positive, neutral, or negative language about companies and markets as a potential signal about their valuations. Because asset prices emerge from supply and demand, opinions expressed at scale on social platforms can contribute information relevant to price forecasting.

The signal is indirect. A sentence classifier estimates linguistic polarity, not future return, and the paper evaluates only sentiment classification rather than a trading strategy or a causal link between language and prices. The finance setting supplies an application domain for [[Sentiment Analysis]] rather than evidence of profitable prediction.

The case study compares quantum-language models on synthetic financial statements. [[Grammar-Constrained Synthetic Financial Sentiment Data]] explains why those statements were generated, while [[QLSTM-DisCoCat Benchmark Comparability]] limits what their classification results establish.
