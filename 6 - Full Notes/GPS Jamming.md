# GPS Jamming

GPS jamming overwhelms the weak satellite signal at a receiver with stronger radio energy in or near the navigation band. Spread-spectrum correlation provides processing gain, but the signals arrive below the ordinary noise floor after traveling from medium Earth orbit, so a nearby low-cost transmitter can deny service over a useful area. Detection, adaptive filtering, protected signals, directional antennas, and independent navigation methods reduce the effect without eliminating the underlying power imbalance.

The risk extends beyond a driver losing a map. GPS also supplies time to networked and public infrastructure, so interference near highways or airports can disrupt systems whose dependence on the signal is otherwise invisible. Jamming denies reception; [[GPS Spoofing]] presents a more deceptive failure by preserving an apparently valid solution.
