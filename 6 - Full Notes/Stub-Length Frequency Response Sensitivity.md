# Stub-Length Frequency Response Sensitivity

Stub-length frequency response sensitivity is the change in a microwave tuner's passband when one [[Tuned Stub|stub]] is lengthened while the rest of the model is held fixed. It connects a geometric adjustment directly to a measurable matching response.

The book increases each stub height separately by a factor of $9/8$, rebuilding the geometry and mesh before recomputing. Relative to the baseline high-pass response, lengthening the first stub produces a 2.4 to 3.0 GHz band-pass region, the second produces about 2.3 to 3.2 GHz, and the third produces about 2.35 to 3.15 GHz.

The different bands show that the three positions are not interchangeable. Sensitivity must be evaluated per parameter, and comparison requires the same sweep, materials, ports, boundary conditions, and [[Voltage Standing Wave Ratio|VSWR]] definition across all variants.
