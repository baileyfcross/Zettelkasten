# Effective Length of Steel Columns

Effective length converts the buckling behavior of a restrained column into the length of an equivalent pin-ended column. The factor K reflects rotational restraint at member ends, whether frame sidesway is inhibited, and the stiffness of adjoining beams and columns. Bracing can shorten the buckling segment, while a sway-permitted frame can produce a larger effective length.

Idealized end-condition values are useful concepts but do not automatically represent a building joint. Alignment charts estimate K from relative member stiffness under restrictive frame assumptions, while modern stability methods can account for system behavior more directly. The chosen method must remain consistent with the analysis model, connection assumptions, and bracing. Treating a nominally framed joint as perfectly fixed can unconservatively inflate column capacity.
