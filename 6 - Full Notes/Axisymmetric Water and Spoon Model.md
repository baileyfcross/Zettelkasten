# Axisymmetric Water and Spoon Model

The water-and-spoon case uses a two-dimensional axisymmetric geometry to represent a spherical stainless-steel object submerged in hot water. Rotational symmetry preserves radial and vertical temperature variation while avoiding the cost of a full three-dimensional mesh.

Conduction-only and conjugate variants separate the effect of resolved water motion. Their point temperatures can be compared with the [[Lumped-Capacity Cooling of Hot Water|lumped calculation]] and with experiments, showing how added spatial physics changes the predicted equalization.
