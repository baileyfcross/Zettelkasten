# Inverse Geometric Transformation

An inverse geometric transformation recovers an original point or object from its transformed coordinates. If a forward transformation multiplies a point by a nonsingular matrix T, applying T inverse undoes the effect.

Elementary inverses have direct geometric meanings: negate a translation vector, negate a rotation angle, and replace nonzero scale factors by their reciprocals. For a composite transformation, both the individual operations and their order must be reversed because matrix multiplication is not commutative. This reversal principle is captured by [[Transformation Inversion by Reverse Order]].
