# Haptic Spatial Acuity

Haptic spatial acuity is the ability to distinguish the locations, orientations, or fine features of mechanical stimulation on the skin. It is greatest where [[Cutaneous Mechanoreceptor|mechanoreceptors]] are dense and their receptive fields are small, especially at the fingertips, and lower on areas such as the palm or forearm.

Measures include [[Two-Point Threshold]], point localization, grating-orientation discrimination, and detection of small raised features. Acuity is therefore a property of a body location and task, not of touch in general; a tactile display must match its spacing to the skin site where it will be used.
