# Body-Powered Hand Prosthesis

A body-powered hand prosthesis uses a shoulder harness and cable to transmit body movement to an artificial elbow or terminal device such as a mechanical hook. Shoulder flexion or abduction pulls the cable, while the intact hand may adjust wrist orientation.

The mechanism is simple and robust and supplies incidental [[Kinesthesia|kinesthetic]] feedback: cable tension and shoulder motion reveal load and device position. Users can reproduce surprisingly small grasp forces despite the limited hand mechanism. The trade-off is awkward body movement and reduced control when the terminal device is positioned outside the harness's effective range.
