# Post-Quantum Cryptography

Post-quantum cryptography develops classical cryptographic algorithms from mathematical problems intended to remain difficult for quantum computers. The source contrasts this approach with systems based on integer factorization, discrete logarithms, and elliptic-curve discrete logarithms, whose assumptions are threatened by quantum algorithms.

Candidate families include lattice-based encryption, hash-based signatures, code-based cryptography, and multivariate constructions. Their adoption introduces practical costs such as larger keys, greater storage, and additional processing. [[Lattice-Based Cryptography]] and the [[NTRU Cryptosystem]] illustrate the book's detailed path from a quantum-resistant hardness problem to an implementable public-key system.
