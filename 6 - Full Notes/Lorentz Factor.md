# Lorentz Factor

The Lorentz factor is

$$\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}},$$

where $v$ is the relative speed between inertial frames. It equals one at zero relative speed, changes very little when $v\ll c$, and grows without bound as $v$ approaches the speed of light.

Gamma is the time-axis coefficient in a [[Lorentz Transformation]]. It is also $\cosh\chi$ when the boost is parameterized by [[Rapidity]] $\chi$, while $\gamma v/c=\sinh\chi$. These identities make the preserved difference of squares explicit.

The factor controls [[Time Dilation]], [[Length Contraction]], relativistic energy and momentum, and the relation between proper and coordinate acceleration. Its repeated appearance is one consequence of using the same hyperbolic geometry for every inertial change of frame.
