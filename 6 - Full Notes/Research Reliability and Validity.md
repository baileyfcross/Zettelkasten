# Research Reliability and Validity

Research reliability is the consistency of a measurement or finding under relevant repetition, while validity asks whether the procedure measures or establishes what it claims. A stable instrument can reliably measure the wrong construct, so reliability supports but does not guarantee validity.

Internal validity concerns whether the observed relationship is credible within the study rather than an artifact of selection, history, measurement, or another uncontrolled cause. External validity concerns how well the result generalizes to other people, organizations, systems, settings, or times. Strong control can improve the first while narrowing the second.

Information-systems research may also judge utility and usage. Utility concerns whether a result improves prediction, explanation, or control; usage concerns whether a method or system is actually adopted. These practical criteria complement rather than replace evidence about reliability and validity.
