# Map Generalization

Map generalization simplifies geographic information so a display remains readable at its scale and for its purpose. Features may be selected, classified, symbolized, displaced, or collapsed: two neighboring cities can become one dot when zoomed out, and a river can appear as a line without depth or flow direction.

Generalization is necessary because a map cannot carry every property of the world. Its quality depends on preserving the relationships relevant to the reader while making the omissions visible through scale, legend, and context. A simplified representation becomes misleading when users treat it as a complete or geometrically exact record.
