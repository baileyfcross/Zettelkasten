# Manual Self-Tracking

Manual self-tracking records observations through deliberate entry rather than automatic sensing. A person might mark a paper chart, rate a meal, describe a mood, take a photograph, or enter symptoms in a spreadsheet.

Manual entry requires effort, but it can capture meanings that a sensor cannot. The act of naming a sensation may itself improve attention, and the categories can change as understanding develops. It also forces a choice about time: an entry may describe the present moment, the previous few hours, or an entire day. Stating that interval makes later [[Personal Data Interpretation]] more reliable.
