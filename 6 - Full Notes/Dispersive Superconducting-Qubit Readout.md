# Dispersive Superconducting-Qubit Readout

In dispersive readout, a qubit is detuned from a microwave resonator so that they do not intentionally exchange real excitations. Virtual interaction instead shifts the resonator frequency by a qubit-state-dependent amount. A probe reflected from or transmitted through the resonator therefore acquires a state-dependent amplitude and phase.

The two responses appear as separated clusters in the integrated I-Q plane. Longer integration initially improves their signal-to-noise ratio, but resonator ring-up, amplifier noise, qubit relaxation during measurement, and state-preparation error limit the achievable single-shot fidelity. Heterodyne detection is especially compatible with frequency-multiplexed resonators.

The measurement is approximately quantum nondemolition only in the low-photon dispersive regime where the effective interaction commutes with the measured qubit observable. Large photon populations can invalidate the dispersive approximation, while residual photons cause ac-Stark shifts and dephasing. Faster readout also widens the resonator and can increase radiative decay, motivating a [[Purcell Filter for Fast Qubit Readout]].
