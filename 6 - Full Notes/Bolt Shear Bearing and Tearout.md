# Bolt Shear Bearing and Tearout

In a bearing-type connection, load can be limited by shear fracture of the bolt, bearing deformation or fracture of the connected material, and tearout from a hole to a nearby edge or adjacent hole. Bolt shear strength depends on bolt material, cross-sectional area, number of shear planes, and whether threads lie in those planes.

Bearing and tearout resistance depend on plate thickness, tensile strength, bolt diameter, clear distance in the load direction, and the permitted deformation level. The connection strength is the least applicable resistance multiplied by the number of effective bolts only when load sharing is justified. A larger bolt does not correct inadequate edge distance, and a thicker plate does not correct an insufficient bolt group; bolt and connected material must be checked together.
