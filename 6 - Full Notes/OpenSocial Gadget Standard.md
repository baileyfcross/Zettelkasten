# OpenSocial Gadget Standard

OpenSocial defined common APIs and a gadget model intended to let applications work across multiple social-network containers. Google initiated it with providers including MySpace, Ning, and Plaxo. Apache Shindig became its principal open-source implementation, serving and consuming gadgets and social data.

Adoption fragmented. Major platforms declined support, some enterprise vendors retained it, Google moved away from its own standard, and Apache retired Shindig in 2015. The source also notes security concerns around easily embedded widgets. OpenSocial therefore illustrates that an interoperability standard needs durable provider participation, maintained implementations, and a defensible security model—not only a portable API. Some portal products still expose legacy gadget support through features such as [[Liferay Sharing and OpenSocial Gadgets]].
