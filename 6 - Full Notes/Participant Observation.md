# Participant Observation

Participant observation is field research in which the investigator takes part in the activity or social setting being studied while systematically recording it. Participation can provide access to tacit routines, practical constraints, and meanings that are difficult to elicit through direct questions.

The same closeness creates methodological risks. The researcher's role can change the setting, relationships can shape what is disclosed, and familiarity can make unusual practices seem invisible. A detailed field journal should distinguish observed events, participant accounts, and the researcher's interpretations.

Role disclosure, consent, confidentiality, and withdrawal require special attention because participation blurs the boundary between ordinary interaction and data collection. Reflexivity about access and influence is therefore evidence about the method, not an optional personal aside.
