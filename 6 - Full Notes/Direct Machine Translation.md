# Direct Machine Translation

Direct machine translation maps source-language forms to target-language forms with little intermediate analysis. A bilingual dictionary supplies candidate words, and local rules reorder or inflect them—for example, moving a French noun-adjective sequence into English adjective-noun order.

Its short processing path makes direct translation practical in a restricted domain, but it stays close to word-for-word substitution and handles ambiguity poorly. In the [[Vauquois Triangle]], it occupies the base: little analysis is required, yet every language pair needs its own mappings and the output has limited access to sentence-level meaning.
