# Cutaneous Mechanoreceptor

A cutaneous mechanoreceptor converts pressure, indentation, stretch, vibration, or hair movement at the skin into neural activity. Its contribution depends on the size of its [[Mechanoreceptor Receptive Field]], its depth, the stimulus frequencies to which it responds, and whether it adapts quickly or continues signaling during sustained contact.

Populations of these receptors support texture and shape perception, detect incipient slip, and regulate grasp. Their density and receptive-field size vary across the body, so identical physical stimulation need not produce identical spatial detail at the fingertip, palm, forearm, and back.
