# Research Conceptual Framework

A research conceptual framework is an organized account of the concepts and relationships that connect a research interest to a testable study. It narrows a broad domain into a problem, identifies relevant constructs, and explains why particular variables, hypotheses, and observations belong together.

The framework coordinates the entire project: [[Research Problem Formulation]], hypotheses, design, instruments, sampling, procedures, analysis, and interpretation should all trace back to it. A variable with no role in the framework is difficult to justify, while a central construct with no measurement reveals a gap in the design.

Framework development and [[Continuous Background Review]] proceed together. Literature changes the framework, and the framework exposes what literature must be sought next. The result is a revisable map of the reasoning, not merely a diagram placed in the final report.
