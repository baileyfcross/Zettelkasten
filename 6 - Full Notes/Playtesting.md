# Playtesting

Playtesting observes other people interacting with a game to evaluate the experience rather than to find code defects. It reveals whether players form workable mental models, understand their goals, make meaningful decisions, and remain engaged.

Tests should begin early, use clear questions and appropriate participants, and avoid coaching or defending the design. Observations, concise surveys, and player comments identify problems; the designer analyzes recurring evidence and decides how to solve them.

Playtesting and iteration also reveal whether a game's expressive conversation produces the intended response. That response need not be conventional fun; the test can examine whether the designed system communicates a serious, difficult, or reflective experience clearly.
