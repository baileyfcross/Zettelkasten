# Collective Neutrino Oscillation Qubit Mapping

Dense neutrino environments such as supernovae and neutron-star mergers require more than independent flavor oscillations. Neutrino-neutrino scattering couples the particles into a many-body system, while vacuum mixing and interactions with background electrons add one-body terms. Mean-field treatments omit direct correlations that may affect flavor evolution.

Under a two-flavor approximation, each neutrino becomes a two-level system and maps directly to one qubit. The Hamiltonian contains a vacuum term, a matter term responsible for the MSW effect, and an all-to-all two-body interaction whose strength depends on neutrino density and relative propagation angle. Time-dependent densities turn the problem into non-equilibrium evolution under a changing Hamiltonian.

The direct mapping avoids a separate data-loading problem, but it does not make evolution shallow. Realistic initial correlations, three-flavor extensions, long evolution times, and dense connectivity all raise the cost. The mapping is therefore a clean test of quantum many-body simulation, while [[Collective Neutrino SWAP-Network Simulation]] addresses how its nonlocal interactions fit restricted device layouts.
