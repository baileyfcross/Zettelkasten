# Single-Image Depth Ambiguity

Single-image depth ambiguity arises because every image point corresponds to an entire ray of possible three-dimensional locations. Scaling a point's lateral position and depth by the same factor leaves its projected position unchanged, so one photograph alone cannot uniquely recover absolute scene geometry.

This ambiguity explains how a small nearby object can resemble a large distant one and why reconstruction requires additional constraints. Known dimensions, parallelism, support surfaces, anatomical proportions, or multiple views can narrow the family of scenes consistent with the image.
