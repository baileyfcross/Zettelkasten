# Seismic Base Shear for Steel Buildings

Seismic loading represents inertial force generated when ground motion accelerates a building's mass. In the equivalent lateral force procedure, design base shear is obtained by multiplying effective seismic weight by a response coefficient derived from mapped spectral acceleration, site effects, occupancy risk, structural period, and the response modification characteristics of the selected lateral system.

The response modification factor reduces elastic force to a design level by relying on ductility, overstrength, and detailing, so it cannot be chosen independently of the framing and connection provisions. Minimum and maximum coefficient limits prevent unrealistic forces. Base shear is only the starting resultant; it must be distributed vertically and horizontally through diaphragms, collectors, frames, and foundations while accounting for torsion and irregularity.
