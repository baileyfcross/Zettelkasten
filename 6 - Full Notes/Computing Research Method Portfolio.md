# Computing Research Method Portfolio

A computing research method portfolio is the set of complementary ways an information-systems question can be investigated. It includes implementation-driven research, mathematical proof, controlled experiments, field observation, surveys, case studies, conceptual analysis, instrument development, literature review, and simulation.

The research question should determine the method. A proof can establish a formal property but not whether users adopt a system; a field study can reveal use in context but may not isolate causes; a laboratory experiment strengthens control but can weaken realism. Combining methods can therefore address different parts of one claim rather than merely repeat the same evidence.

Method selection belongs inside [[Research Design]] because it determines the observations, controls, analysis, and conclusions the project can support. The choice should be justified explicitly instead of inherited from the researcher's preferred tool.
