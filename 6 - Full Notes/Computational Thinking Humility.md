# Computational Thinking Humility

Computational thinking requires humility about what computers can understand and accomplish. Machines execute specified operations quickly, but they do not supply the meaning of their inputs or automatically correct mistakes that their designers failed to anticipate.

Humility also means recognizing impossible and intractable problems, the need for domain knowledge, and the risks created when speed amplifies an error. It turns limits into design constraints instead of hiding them behind claims that every problem is computational.
