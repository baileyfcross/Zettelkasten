# Digital Person Copying and Divergence

Copying a digital person produces multiple instances with a shared history up to the copying event. Each instance then receives different inputs, makes different choices, and accumulates a distinct perspective, so numerical duplication quickly becomes psychological divergence.

The shared origin creates continuity without permanent interchangeability. Consent given by the predecessor may not govern every later copy, and deleting one instance is not made morally trivial by the survival of another. Institutions must treat the descendants as separate centers of action while still accounting for their common past.
