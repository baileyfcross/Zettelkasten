# Self-Serving Bias

Self-serving bias attributes success to personal ability and failure to external conditions. A player may treat a win as evidence of skill while treating a loss as evidence that the game cheated, the random generator was unfair, or the controls failed.

The design consequence is not that every loss must feel pleasant, but that feedback should make causation legible. When players can connect outcomes to their own decisions, they are less likely to devalue the game by assigning every failure to the system.
