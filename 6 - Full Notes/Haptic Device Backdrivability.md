# Haptic Device Backdrivability

Haptic device backdrivability is the ability of an actuator and mechanism to follow a user's movement with little unintended resistance. A backdrivable interface feels transparent when no virtual force is commanded, allowing small contacts and free motion to remain perceptible.

High inertia, gearing friction, cable friction, and backlash reduce backdrivability and can mask a rendered surface. The property must be considered with force capacity, bandwidth, workspace, and mass: a powerful actuator that constantly resists the user's hand may reproduce large forces while failing to render delicate interactions.
