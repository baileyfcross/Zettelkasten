# IoT Labor Displacement

IoT labor displacement occurs when connected sensing, machine intelligence, and robotics allow a task to be performed with fewer people or without direct human labor. Automated checkout, inventory robots, connected factories, autonomous vehicles, and robotic food preparation extend automation from isolated machinery into coordinated services.

The effect is uneven rather than a simple disappearance of all work. Technology can complement some roles and create specialized jobs while placing routine production, transportation, administrative, and service work at greater risk. Because the pace of change can outrun retraining and geographic mobility, productivity gains do not guarantee that displaced workers share in their benefits.
