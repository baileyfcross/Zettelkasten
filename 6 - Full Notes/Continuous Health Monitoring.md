# Continuous Health Monitoring

Continuous health monitoring repeatedly observes physiological or behavioral measures outside occasional clinical encounters. It can reveal gradual change, transient events, and deviations from a person's usual pattern that a single measurement would miss.

Continuous sampling also creates long, noisy streams that clinicians cannot inspect manually. Analytics must summarize trends, identify actionable anomalies, and route alerts through an appropriate response pathway. Battery life, adherence, communication failures, and privacy are part of the measurement system and must be considered when interpreting missing or abnormal data.
