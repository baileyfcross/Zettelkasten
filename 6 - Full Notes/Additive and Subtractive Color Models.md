# Additive and Subtractive Color Models

Additive color models create displayed color by combining emitted light. Red, green, and blue contributions add toward white, which makes RGB a natural model for screens and digital image capture pipelines.

Subtractive models describe pigments or inks that remove portions of incident light. Cyan, magenta, yellow, and often black are therefore useful for print. A web workflow must recognize that converting between additive and subtractive spaces changes how numeric channel values map to visible color, especially when a source master was prepared for print.
