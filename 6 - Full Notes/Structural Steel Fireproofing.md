# Structural Steel Fireproofing

Structural steel does not burn, but elevated temperature reduces its strength and stiffness and causes thermal expansion. Fire-resistance assemblies delay heating long enough to meet the building's required rating. Common approaches include spray-applied material, board or enclosure systems, intumescent coatings, and concrete encasement.

Protection thickness and continuity depend on member size, exposure, assembly testing, and required duration. Connections, deck interfaces, openings, impact damage, moisture, and later alterations can interrupt the protective envelope. Shop primer compatibility matters because some fireproofing systems require a particular substrate or adhesion condition. Fire design therefore coordinates structural details with architectural finish, inspection, and maintenance instead of treating protection as an interchangeable coating.
