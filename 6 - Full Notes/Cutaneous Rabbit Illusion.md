# Cutaneous Rabbit Illusion

The cutaneous rabbit illusion, or sensory saltation, occurs when short pulses delivered successively at a few separated skin locations feel like a stimulus hopping smoothly through intermediate locations. Those intermediate contacts are perceived even though no actuator stimulated them.

The illusion depends on pulse count, timing, spacing, and body site; sufficiently long intervals restore accurate localization. Because real and illusory intermediate taps can feel alike, the effect offers a way to create apparent motion with sparse hardware. It also warns that timing errors can change the spatial meaning of a tactile message.
