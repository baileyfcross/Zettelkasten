# Quantum Heuristic Evaluation

Near-term quantum optimization algorithms often lack proven speedups, so they must be evaluated as heuristics. Relevant goals differ: exact optimization, approximation with a guarantee, good solutions without a guarantee, fair portfolio sampling, or sampling from a target distribution.

Analytic study, classical simulation, and hardware experiments answer different questions. A useful evaluation specifies objective quality, query and shot budgets, classical optimization, compilation overhead, noise, and competitive classical baselines. Running a quantum circuit and obtaining a plausible solution is evidence of execution, not by itself evidence of advantage.
