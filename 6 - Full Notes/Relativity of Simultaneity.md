# Relativity of Simultaneity

Relativity of simultaneity is the result that two spatially separated events judged simultaneous in one inertial frame need not be simultaneous in another. A frame defines simultaneity by assigning the same time coordinate to events along one of its constant-time slices. A [[Lorentz Transformation]] tilts those slices when the frame changes velocity.

Einstein's train example illustrates the operational issue. Light from two track strikes can reach a platform observer together while reaching an observer moving with the train at different times. Because every inertial observer measures the same light speed, the disagreement is attributed to the timing of the strikes, not to different propagation speeds.

This effect does not overturn [[Causal Order in Special Relativity]]. Only spacelike-separated events can reverse temporal order between frames, and such events cannot exchange a signal at or below light speed.
