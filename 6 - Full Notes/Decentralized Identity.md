# Decentralized Identity

Decentralized identity gives a subject control over an identifier and the credentials associated with it instead of requiring every relying service to maintain a separate central identity record. A user can store credentials in a wallet and present only the proof required for a transaction.

A decentralized identifier resolves to material that supports authentication and trusted service endpoints, while issuers cryptographically sign [[Verifiable Credential|verifiable credentials]] about the subject. The approach can reduce repeated disclosure and central stores of personal information, but it shifts responsibility toward wallet recovery, issuer governance, revocation, interoperability, and the verifier's trust policy. Decentralization changes where trust is expressed; it does not eliminate trust.
