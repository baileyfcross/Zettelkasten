# Wind Pressure on Steel Buildings

Wind produces positive pressure on windward surfaces and suction on leeward walls, side walls, and much of a roof. The velocity-pressure basis makes demand proportional to the square of wind speed, with adjustments for height, exposure, topography, directionality, elevation, gust response, building enclosure, and the pressure coefficient associated with surface shape and location.

The resulting pressures act normal to building surfaces and must be separated into main-force-resisting-system and component-and-cladding effects. Internal pressure can increase or decrease net surface pressure, while prescribed load cases introduce torsion by shifting the resultant away from the center of resistance. Design therefore follows wind from local cladding attachment through diaphragms and frames to foundations, checking both strength and serviceability.
