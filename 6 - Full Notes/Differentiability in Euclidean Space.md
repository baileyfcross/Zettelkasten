# Differentiability in Euclidean Space

A function $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is differentiable at $a$ when a linear map $Df(a)$ gives its first-order change:

$$f(a+h)-f(a)=Df(a)h+o(\|h\|).$$

The remainder must become negligible relative to $\|h\|$ in every direction. This is stronger than merely possessing all [[Partial Derivative|partial derivatives]], and it implies [[Multivariable Continuity|continuity]] at the point.
