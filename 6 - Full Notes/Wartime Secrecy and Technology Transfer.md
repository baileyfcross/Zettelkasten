# Wartime Secrecy and Technology Transfer

Military secrecy shaped which computing innovations entered public knowledge and commercial production after World War II. British codebreaking machines remained hidden, while American projects more readily seeded companies, government laboratories, and university programs.

Secrecy protected continuing cryptographic capabilities but distorted historical credit and slowed reuse. Technology transfer depended as much on institutions and policy as on the technical quality of a machine.
