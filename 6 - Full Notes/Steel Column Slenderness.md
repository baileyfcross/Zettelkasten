# Steel Column Slenderness

Column slenderness is expressed by the effective-length ratio KL/r, where K represents end-restraint and frame behavior, L is unbraced length, and r is radius of gyration about the possible buckling axis. The larger ratio generally governs, so a wide-flange column often buckles about its weak axis unless bracing or framing changes the effective lengths.

Slenderness locates a member between material yielding and instability. Stocky columns can approach squash or yield strength, intermediate columns buckle inelastically, and long columns buckle elastically. It is distinct from element slenderness: KL/r concerns global member behavior, whereas width-to-thickness ratios determine whether a flange or web buckles locally. Both checks are required because local and global instability can reduce strength independently.
