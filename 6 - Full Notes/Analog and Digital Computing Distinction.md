# Analog and Digital Computing Distinction

Analog computing represents a quantity through a continuously varying physical quantity, while digital computing represents it with discrete symbols. A mechanical cam shaped like an equation is analog; a numerical procedure executed with pulses is digital.

The categories were not sharply named until the 1930s and often coexisted in early systems. Digital methods prevailed because discrete states were easier to copy, control, and combine reliably at electronic speed.
