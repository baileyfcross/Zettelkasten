# Lidar-Based Autonomous Driving

Lidar measures distance by sending laser pulses and timing their return, producing a dense three-dimensional description of nearby surfaces. Autonomous vehicles can use that point cloud to locate lanes, curbs, obstacles, and free space more precisely than coarse positioning alone.

Rotating multi-beam units demonstrated strong performance in early autonomous-vehicle competitions but were initially more expensive than the base cars carrying them. Falling component cost changes what can move from research to mass deployment. Lidar remains one part of an [[Autonomous Vehicle Sensor Suite]] because weather, classification, and coverage still require complementary sensing.
