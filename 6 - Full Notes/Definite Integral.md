# Definite Integral

A definite integral accumulates a function between specified lower and upper limits. If $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$, then $\int_a^b f(x)dx=F(b)-F(a)$, so the arbitrary constant in an indefinite antiderivative cancels.

Geometrically, the value is signed area between the graph and the axis: portions below the axis contribute negatively. Reversing the limits reverses the sign, and splitting an interval at an interior point expresses the whole integral as the sum over its subintervals.
