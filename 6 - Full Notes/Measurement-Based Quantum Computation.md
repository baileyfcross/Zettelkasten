# Measurement-Based Quantum Computation

Measurement-based quantum computation begins with a highly entangled resource such as a cluster or graph state, then performs a sequence of single-qubit measurements. Later measurement bases can depend on earlier outcomes, and classical postprocessing interprets the final record.

The model has computational power equivalent to the standard circuit model, but it allocates the work differently: entanglement is prepared up front and consumed by measurement, so it is also called one-way quantum computing. More entanglement is not automatically a better resource; the state's structure must support the required adaptive measurement patterns.
