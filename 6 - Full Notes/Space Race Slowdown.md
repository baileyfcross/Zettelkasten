# Space Race Slowdown

The Space Race slowdown followed the first lunar landing as the central prestige question had been settled and other national problems competed for resources. NASA spending had already peaked, the United States rejected an immediate sequence of stations, Moon bases, and Mars expeditions, and the Soviet Union redirected effort toward orbital stations.

The change revealed how strongly rapid human-spaceflight progress had depended on geopolitical rivalry rather than on a self-sustaining technical timetable. Science and satellite infrastructure continued because their institutions and utility supported them, while human travel beyond low Earth orbit stopped after 1972.
