# GPS Timing Infrastructure

GPS timing infrastructure uses the constellation's disciplined time scale as a common reference for distributed systems. A stationary receiver that already knows its location can focus on clock synchronization rather than navigation, delivering precise timing to telecommunications and other networks. This role is largely invisible to end users but makes loss or corruption of satellite signals an infrastructure problem even where no one is actively viewing a map.

The constellation's atomic clocks and ground corrections let widely separated receivers share time without a physical connection. Electrical grids, communications systems, banks, airports, and consumer clocks can inherit that reference, which means a bad timing upload can affect services that appear unrelated to navigation.
