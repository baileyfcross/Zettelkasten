# Employer-Imposed Tracking

Employer-imposed tracking occurs when an organization requires or strongly pressures workers to collect data about themselves. The person may perform the acts of wearing, charging, or entering data, but the employer defines the purpose and can attach consequences to participation.

This differs from [[Self-Initiated Self-Tracking]] because control over the question, data access, and response is unequal. A voluntary label is insufficient when refusal could affect work opportunities, benefits, or how commitment is judged.

The practice can also move information across contexts. A fitness record gathered through a wellness program may become employment data, creating privacy and labor concerns that would not arise from a private exercise diary.
