# Probability Sampling Design

Probability sampling design selects cases through a known random mechanism so that inclusion probabilities can support population inference. It requires a target population and a sampling frame that identifies the units from which the sample can actually be drawn.

Simple random sampling gives eligible units an equal chance of selection. Stratified sampling draws within defined subgroups to ensure their representation or improve precision. Cluster sampling selects naturally occurring groups, systematic sampling takes units at a fixed interval after a random start, and double sampling gathers inexpensive information first before subsampling for costlier measurements.

Random selection does not cure a defective frame, nonresponse, or measurement error. The design and its analysis must preserve unequal probabilities, strata, clusters, and other features that affect estimates and standard errors.
