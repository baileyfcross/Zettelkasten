# Space Astronomy

Space astronomy observes celestial sources with instruments carried above Earth's atmosphere. It avoids atmospheric turbulence and opens wavelength ranges that the atmosphere blocks, from high-energy gamma rays and X-rays through ultraviolet and parts of the infrared.

Initial sounding-rocket observations produced crude surveys because instruments were small and pointing was limited. Orbiting observatories later held telescopes on targets for extended periods and transformed astronomy across the electromagnetic spectrum. The field depends on precise attitude control, sensitive detectors, telemetry, and long-lived spacecraft as much as on the telescope optics themselves.
