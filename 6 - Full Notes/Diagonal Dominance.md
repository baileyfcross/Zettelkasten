# Diagonal Dominance

A square matrix is diagonally dominant by rows when each diagonal magnitude is at least the sum of the magnitudes of the other entries in that row. Strict dominance replaces the inequality with a strict one.

Dominance makes the diagonal a natural part to isolate when deriving the [[Jacobi Method]]. Stronger dominance conditions can guarantee convergence of Jacobi or [[Gauss-Seidel Method|Gauss-Seidel]] iterations, although dominance is not necessary for every convergent system.
