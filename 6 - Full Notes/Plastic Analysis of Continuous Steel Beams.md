# Plastic Analysis of Continuous Steel Beams

Plastic analysis allows a statically indeterminate steel beam or girder to redistribute moment after a section reaches plastic capacity. Additional load produces plastic hinges at other critical sections until enough hinges form to convert the structure into a collapse mechanism. The required plastic moment can then be found from equilibrium or by equating external and internal work for an assumed mechanism.

The method requires compact sections, adequate rotational ductility, and close enough compression-flange bracing to prevent lateral-torsional buckling before redistribution occurs. Each plausible mechanism must be considered because the one requiring the largest member strength governs. Plastic analysis changes how demand is calculated, while the member's design moment strength is still established through the applicable steel strength provisions and factored load combinations.
