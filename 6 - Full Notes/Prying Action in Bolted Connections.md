# Prying Action in Bolted Connections

Prying action is additional bolt tension produced when a flexible connected plate bends and bears against its supporting surface. The plate behaves like a lever: applied tension pulls near the bolt line while contact near the plate edge creates a reaction that amplifies bolt force beyond the externally assigned share.

Thin plates, large distances between the bolt and supported line, and short edge projections can make prying significant. Increasing plate thickness, changing bolt layout, reducing lever arms, or adding stiffening can suppress it. A connection checked only by dividing external tension among bolts can therefore be unconservative. Design must consider bolt tensile resistance, plate yielding mechanism, and the interaction between them so the intended ductile behavior occurs before bolt rupture.
