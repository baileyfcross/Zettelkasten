# Law Reducible to Linear Form

A nonlinear-looking empirical law is reducible to linear form when substitutions transform it into $Y=mX+c$. For example, $y=ax^n$ becomes $\log y=\log a+n\log x$, and $y=ae^{bx}$ becomes $\log y=\log a+(b\log e)x$.

After transformation, straight-line graphical or least-squares methods estimate the new slope and intercept. The original constants are recovered by reversing the substitutions, with the transformed-error model kept in mind.
