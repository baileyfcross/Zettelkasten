# Quaternion Matrix Representation

A quaternion can be represented by a structured real $4\times4$ matrix or by a complex $2\times2$ matrix. Matrix multiplication then reproduces the noncommutative multiplication rules of [[Quaternion Algebra]], turning the signs and ordering of $i$, $j$, and $k$ into ordinary row-by-column operations.

In the real representation used by the source, the quaternion's scalar part appears along the diagonal and the three imaginary components occupy paired off-diagonal positions with opposite signs. Exponentiating a pure-imaginary quaternion matrix produces a four-dimensional rotation matrix whose entries depend on the direction and magnitude of its imaginary part.

The representation makes the relationship to [[SU(2) and Unit Quaternions]] visible and helps compare quaternion commutators with the generators used by the [[Lorentz Group]]. A matrix notation changes the bookkeeping, not the underlying quaternion algebra.
