# 3D Travel

3D travel is the motor component of [[Navigation]]: moving from the current location to a target or moving in a desired direction.

Travel can be physical or virtual and actively controlled or system-controlled. Common task types are exploration, goal-directed search, and precise local maneuvering. Their requirements differ: exploration benefits from interruptible continuous control, search can use target-based movement, and maneuvering needs fine adjustment.

Technique choice should also consider distance, path curvature, target visibility, required [[Degrees of Freedom]], accuracy, and other tasks performed during movement. Because travel often supports a more important task, it should demand little unnecessary attention.

In VR, natural walking best preserves the one-to-one relation between bodily and virtual motion but is limited by the physical tracking space. Teleportation extends range with relatively little continuous visual motion at the cost of disorientation. Vehicle frames reduce optic flow and provide a stable reference, physical techniques map actions such as jogging or climbing to movement, and thumbstick locomotion offers freedom while creating the greatest risk of [[Cybersickness]]. Comfort settings should match the audience and remain optional when they materially reduce immersion.
