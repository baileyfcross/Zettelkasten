# Optical Tracking

Optical tracking estimates the position or orientation of cameras, objects, or users from captured light. A system may observe prepared markers, active light sources, reflective targets, or natural image features.

Cameras are readily available on mobile AR devices and can relate measurements directly to visible scene structure. Performance depends on illumination, visibility, feature quality, camera calibration, computation, and the ability to recover after the target leaves view.

An outside-in arrangement places cameras in the environment to observe the user or tracked targets; an inside-out arrangement places cameras on the moving device and estimates its relation to external features. Active emitters and retroreflective targets can simplify detection, while natural-feature methods reduce prepared infrastructure. All remain vulnerable to line-of-sight loss and scene conditions, so optical evidence is often paired with inertial sensing.
