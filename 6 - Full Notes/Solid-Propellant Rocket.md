# Solid-Propellant Rocket

A solid-propellant rocket contains fuel and oxidizer mixed into a solid grain. Modern formulations can combine rubber-like binders, oxygen-bearing perchlorates, and powdered aluminum. Combustion proceeds along a shaped internal channel, whose geometry helps regulate how surface area and thrust change during the burn.

Solid motors are compact and can remain ready for use, which made them attractive for military missiles and later as launch-vehicle boosters. Once ignited, however, their thrust is harder to stop or adjust than that of many liquid systems. They descend historically from the [[Black-Powder Rocket]] but use far more energetic and controllable materials.
