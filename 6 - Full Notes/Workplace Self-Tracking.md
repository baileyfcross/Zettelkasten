# Workplace Self-Tracking

Workplace self-tracking uses records about movement, location, time, productivity, or health within an employment relationship. It extends older time-and-motion measurement through wearable sensors, location systems, productivity software, and wellness programs.

The practice can support safety or voluntary reflection, but the employer's power changes the meaning of consent. Data may affect supervision, scheduling, evaluation, insurance costs, or access to benefits, and employees may be unable to refuse without practical consequences.

Work and personal life can become difficult to separate when a device records activity continuously. A sound program states what is collected, who can see it, how long it is retained, and which employment decisions are prohibited from using it.
