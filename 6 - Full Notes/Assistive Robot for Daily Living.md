# Assistive Robot for Daily Living

An assistive robot supports activities such as lifting, feeding, mobility, reminders, or household work so a person can preserve independence. The preferred division of labor is personal: people may welcome robotic cleaning while wanting human help with bathing, feeding, or other intimate care.

Function is only part of the design. Controls must fit the person's abilities, the device must work in a home rather than a laboratory, and its form can affect dignity and social participation. Assistance should strengthen the user's agency and relationships rather than treating reduced staffing as the only measure of success.
