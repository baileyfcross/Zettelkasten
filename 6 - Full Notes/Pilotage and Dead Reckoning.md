# Pilotage and Dead Reckoning

Pilotage determines position from visible features such as roads, towns, water, railways, towers, and terrain. Dead reckoning extends that observation with calculated direction, groundspeed, elapsed time, and distance. Used together, they let a pilot compare the expected route with the world outside and correct a weak or single [[Lines of Position in Navigation]].

Dead reckoning accumulates error because each new estimate begins from the preceding estimate. Unmodeled wind, current, speed, or timing error can therefore move the predicted position progressively farther from the actual route. Periodic landmarks or external position fixes reset that drift rather than merely making the next extrapolation more precise.
