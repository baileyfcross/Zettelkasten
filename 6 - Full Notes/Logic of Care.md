# Logic of Care

The logic of care treats health as an ongoing, situated practice of adjusting treatment to a person's changing body and circumstances. Good action emerges through repeated observation, professional judgment, patient experience, and practical coordination rather than through a single isolated choice.

Self-tracking data can contribute by showing patterns between visits, but it does not dictate the response. A clinician and patient still decide which signal matters, whether the measure is trustworthy, and how an intervention fits other needs.

This differs from the [[Logic of Choice]], which can frame technology as presenting information to an autonomous consumer who then selects the correct option. Care makes the continuing work and relationships behind that decision visible.
