# Conditional Proposition

The conditional proposition p → q states that if p is true, then q is true. It is false only when its antecedent p is true and its consequent q is false, and it is [[Logically Equivalent]] to ¬p ∨ q.

Its converse q → p is generally different, while its contrapositive ¬q → ¬p is equivalent to the original conditional. This equivalence supports [[Proof by Contrapositive]].
