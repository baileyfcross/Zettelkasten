# Social Networking Services

A social networking service provides a shared online environment for identities, relationships, messages, media, and continuing updates. Its value grows from the interactions and content contributed by participants.

These services descend from bulletin boards, chat rooms, Usenet, and commercial online communities. Centralized platforms combine those functions at scale while controlling access to much of the resulting data.

The enterprise-social source separates this broad idea into general-purpose networks, professional networks, messaging services, media-sharing platforms, blogs and discussion communities, review sites, metaverse environments, and narrower vertical communities. The categories overlap, but they help an application identify whether it is integrating identity and relationships, publishing, real-time conversation, media, or reputation. Provider-neutral standards and federated protocols can reduce dependence on any one centralized service, although the application still needs to accommodate the capabilities of each network.
