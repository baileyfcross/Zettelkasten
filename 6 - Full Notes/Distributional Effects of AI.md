# Distributional Effects of AI

The distributional effects of AI are the ways a system's benefits, burdens, errors, and risks are allocated across people and groups. A system can improve average efficiency while concentrating surveillance, unemployment, unsafe conditions, or false accusations among those with less power.

Ethical evaluation therefore asks "improvement for whom?" rather than relying only on total performance. Stakeholders include people who never chose to use the system but are classified or affected by it. Distributional analysis connects technical measures to [[AI and Economic Inequality]] and reveals when a claimed social benefit depends on transferring costs to a vulnerable population.
