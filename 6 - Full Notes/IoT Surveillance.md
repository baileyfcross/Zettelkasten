# IoT Surveillance

IoT surveillance uses connected cameras, location systems, wearables, phones, sensors, and recognition algorithms to observe people and relate their activity to identities. The network can make monitoring persistent and nearly invisible because collection occurs through ordinary infrastructure rather than a single conspicuous checkpoint.

Combining facial recognition, augmented-reality displays, and remote databases can identify a person in real time. When the resulting records affect travel, access, policing, employment, or social standing, errors and selective enforcement become material exercises of power. The central question is not only whether the sensing works but who can observe, combine, challenge, and act on the data.
