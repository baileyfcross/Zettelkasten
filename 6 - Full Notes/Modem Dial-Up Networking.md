# Modem Dial-Up Networking

A dial-up modem translated computer data into audio tones that could travel over ordinary telephone lines and converted received tones back into bits. Users called a local service number from a personal computer.

The connection reused widespread voice infrastructure but was slow and often billed by time or distance. It gave households access to databases, bulletin boards, and online services before broadband.
