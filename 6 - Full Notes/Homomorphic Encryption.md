# Homomorphic Encryption

Homomorphic encryption allows a party to compute on ciphertext without first exposing the underlying plaintext. An evaluation algorithm combines encrypted inputs under a public evaluation key, and decryption of the resulting ciphertext yields the value of the corresponding operation on the original messages.

The source focuses on addition and multiplication because logical or arithmetic circuits can be assembled from those operations. Schemes differ in which operations they preserve and how many times the operations may be composed, producing [[Partially Homomorphic Encryption]], [[Somewhat Homomorphic Encryption]], and [[Fully Homomorphic Encryption]].
