# N-of-1 Experiment

An n-of-1 experiment is an intentional study in which one person is the unit being observed and compared. The aim is usually to learn what happens for that individual under particular conditions, not to estimate an average effect for a population.

A useful experiment defines a question, records an outcome, and deliberately varies a possible influence. Repeated observations can help distinguish a persistent pattern from a single coincidence. Designs such as a [[Case-Crossover Self-Experiment]] or an [[A-B-A-B Self-Experiment]] make the comparison more explicit.

Personal usefulness does not automatically establish biomedical causation or generalize to others. The result may still guide a person's next question, reveal a plausible trigger, or support a conversation with a clinician.
