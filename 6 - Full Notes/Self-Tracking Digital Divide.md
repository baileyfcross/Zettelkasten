# Self-Tracking Digital Divide

The self-tracking digital divide is unequal ability to obtain, maintain, understand, and benefit from tracking technologies. Cost is only one barrier; access can also depend on a compatible phone, connectivity, technical literacy, time, language, physical ability, and confidence dealing with institutions.

The divide shapes both services and evidence. Products designed around affluent early adopters may overlook the routines of older, poorer, chronically ill, or otherwise under-served people. Datasets collected from those products then make the same narrow population appear to be the normal user.

Addressing the divide requires designing with affected communities and considering whether the tool reduces an access problem or merely adds a technical layer to it.
