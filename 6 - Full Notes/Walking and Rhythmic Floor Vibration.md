# Walking and Rhythmic Floor Vibration

Walking applies repeated footfall forces whose first several harmonics can excite a floor even when the basic step frequency is below the floor's natural frequency. Evaluation combines floor frequency, effective panel weight, damping, and an occupancy-specific force model to estimate peak acceleration, which is then compared with a comfort threshold for the space.

Rhythmic activities such as dancing, aerobics, jumping, or coordinated exercise can involve larger synchronized forces and sustained resonance. Their acceptance criteria depend on the activity and on neighboring occupancies that may be more sensitive. A dining or office area beside an exercise space can therefore control the design. Monumental stairs and pedestrian structures also require attention because their low mass and repetitive excitation can make perceptible response likely.
