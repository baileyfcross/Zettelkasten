# Flight Simulation Training

Flight simulation training uses a controlled representation of an aircraft and its operating environment to develop skills that would be expensive or dangerous to practice in live flight. Scenarios can be repeated, varied, and reset while preserving records for instruction and after-action review.

Ground simulators reproduce instruments, controls, visuals, and sometimes motion, but they cannot perfectly recreate every acceleration, buffet, and vestibular cue. A mixed-reality approach can instead preserve the actual cockpit and aircraft motion while adding virtual terrain, traffic, or targets outside the windows. Such a system requires precise [[Head-Worn Display]] tracking and registration with avionics data so generated objects remain stable as the pilot and aircraft move.

Simulation is strongest when its sensory compromises are explicit. Training should preserve the cues needed for the target skill without making display realism a proxy for operational validity.
