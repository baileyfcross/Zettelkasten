# Ground Truthing

Ground truthing checks an analytical or remote-sensing result by collecting evidence at the actual location. After a model flags an anomalous traffic sensor, for example, staff can inspect the site to determine whether the reading reflects real congestion or failed equipment.

The practice connects data products back to conditions that preprocessing, imagery, and algorithms may not capture. It is often expensive in travel, labor, and time, so it may follow statistical checks and domain-expert review rather than replace them. Its value is decisive confirmation when an action depends on whether a spatial pattern is real.
