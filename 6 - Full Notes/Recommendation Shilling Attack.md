# Recommendation Shilling Attack

A recommendation shilling attack injects strategic ratings, reviews, profiles, or interactions to make a target item appear more or less desirable. Because collaborative systems learn from aggregated behavior, fabricated evidence can distort the neighborhoods and correlations used to rank future recommendations.

The attack is economically tempting precisely when recommendations influence valuable decisions. A platform must decide whether paid or self-serving prominence is clearly disclosed rather than allowed to masquerade as independent relevance. A system that quietly favors the manipulator may gain from one transaction while sacrificing the trust on which its future recommendations depend.
