# User-Centered Design

User-centered design places intended users, their goals, capabilities, and contexts at the center of design decisions.

It begins by understanding the people, tasks, and environment involved rather than treating the technology as the only source of requirements. [[Human Factors]], [[Requirements Analysis]], and [[Task Analysis]] help establish this understanding. Designs are then represented as [[Prototype]]s, examined through [[User Interface Evaluation]], and revised iteratively.

The approach does not imply that every preference becomes a feature. It uses evidence about users to make and evaluate tradeoffs against system goals and constraints.
