# Autonomous Vehicle Infrastructure Effects

Autonomous vehicles can change infrastructure because coordinated, highly utilized cars use roads and land differently. Shorter safe following distances may increase capacity, while shared fleets can reduce parking demand and free urban land for other uses.

The effects extend beyond streets. Fewer crashes affect hospitals, insurers, repair shops, and organ donation; reduced private ownership affects dealerships and loans; different travel behavior changes retail, radio, policing, and municipal revenue. The vehicle is therefore a platform transition whose consequences propagate through institutions built around human-driven ownership.
