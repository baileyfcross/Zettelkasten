# GNSS Constellation Redundancy

GNSS constellation redundancy uses independent providers and extra satellites so that a receiver can continue navigating when one vehicle or system is unavailable. GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou offer different operational control and orbital populations, improving availability and providing some political independence. Their shared reliance on weak L-band signals, spaceborne clocks, and similar orbits means they can still suffer common-mode interference, so multiplicity is not the same as a dissimilar backup.
