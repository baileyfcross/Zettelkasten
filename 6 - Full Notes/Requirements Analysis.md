# Requirements Analysis

Requirements analysis investigates intended users, their activities, and their environment before a new design is built. Here, work includes employment, learning, play, or any activity the system is meant to support.

Contextual inquiry gathers evidence through observation, interviews, recordings, and existing work artifacts. Contextual analysis organizes and interprets that evidence into models of users, tasks, relationships, artifacts, and physical settings. Requirements are then extracted from the problems and opportunities found.

This work grounds [[User-Centered Design]] in actual context and supplies inputs to [[Task Analysis]], design, and [[Prototype|prototyping]].

Architectural analysis also classifies statements into behavior and quality constraints, resolves conflicts, and tests feasibility against schedule, budget, technology, and the expected operating environment.

LLMs can organize large interview, questionnaire, document, and feedback collections; expose repeated or conflicting needs; and draft structured requirements and candidate tasks. This reduces manual synthesis but cannot repair missing stakeholder evidence, so analysts must confirm interpretations and resolve ambiguity with the people affected.
