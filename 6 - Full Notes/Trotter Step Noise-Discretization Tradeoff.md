# Trotter Step Noise-Discretization Tradeoff

Product-formula simulation approximates a Hamiltonian evolution by alternating evolutions generated by simpler Hamiltonian terms. Dividing a fixed total time into more Trotter steps decreases the noncommutation error of the decomposition, and higher-order formulas can improve its asymptotic accuracy further.

On noisy hardware, refinement has an opposing cost. Each additional step repeats entangling gates, so gate infidelity accumulates even while the discretization error falls. In the report's first-order model, the total error contains a term that decreases quadratically with step duration and a term that grows with the number of steps and two-qubit operations. The useful step count is therefore finite: an optimal $n^*$ minimizes the combined numerical and hardware error.

This tradeoff prevents "more steps" from serving as a universal accuracy rule on near-term devices. The optimum depends on Hamiltonian commutators, circuit compilation, gate quality, and total evolution time. Error mitigation may reduce measurement bias, but it adds sampling overhead and does not create unlimited coherent depth. The same constraint shapes [[Collective Neutrino SWAP-Network Simulation]] and lattice-gauge dynamics.
