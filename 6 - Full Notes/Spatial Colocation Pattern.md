# Spatial Colocation Pattern

A spatial colocation pattern identifies types of features or events that repeatedly occur near one another in continuous space. Businesses, species, diseases, players, and environmental conditions can colocate without belonging to a transaction or sharing an administrative region.

Ordinary association rules count items within discrete transactions, but forcing spatial observations into counties or other boundaries can split nearby pairs or double-count patterns. Colocation analysis instead uses proximity among point or geometry data and measures how consistently participating feature types appear together. The result indicates association, not necessarily causation.
