# Invariant Mass

Invariant mass is the frame-independent magnitude associated with a particle's or system's [[Four-Momentum]]. It is defined by

$$m^2c^4=E^2-p^2c^2.$$

An observer may measure different energy and spatial momentum after a change of frame, but the combination above is unchanged. In a particle's rest frame, spatial momentum is zero and invariant mass is its rest energy divided by $c^2$.

For a system of particles, invariant mass belongs to the total four-momentum and can include internal kinetic energy, binding energy, or heat. It is therefore not always the arithmetic sum of the constituents' individual rest masses. This distinction is central to [[Relativistic Collision Conservation]] and avoids the ambiguity of the [[Relativistic Mass Convention]].
