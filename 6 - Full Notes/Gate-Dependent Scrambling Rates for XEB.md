# Gate-Dependent Scrambling Rates for XEB

The diffusion-reaction mapping separates how quickly a two-qubit gate moves correlations from how quickly it changes their local multiplicity. The reported $(D,R)$ values are $(2/3,2/3)$ for CZ, $(4/5,3/5)$ for Haar-random two-qubit gates, and $(1,1/3+\sqrt{3}/6)$ for fSim under Haar-random single-qubit gates.

Faster diffusion and reaction drive the particle distribution toward equilibrium before weak noise distorts it, improving agreement between XEB and fidelity. A modified fSim* gate keeps $D=1$ while raising $R$ to $2/3$, minimizing the tested discrepancy. Google's discrete single-qubit ensemble also scrambles faster than a generic Haar choice in the relevant model and makes simple partition spoofing less effective, showing that benchmark robustness depends on the complete gate ensemble rather than connectivity alone.
