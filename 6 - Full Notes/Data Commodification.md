# Data Commodification

Data commodification treats records as assets that can be accumulated, exchanged, analyzed, or used to produce revenue. Self-tracking companies may sell devices and subscriptions, personalize paid services, target advertising, analyze aggregate patterns, or exchange access with business partners.

The commodity view can obscure how value was jointly produced. A person's repeated activity and interpretation create the record, while the firm controls the infrastructure and contracts. It can also encourage collection in anticipation of an unspecified future use merely because a large stockpile is imagined to be valuable.

Not every economic use is inherently harmful, but it changes incentives around access, retention, and disclosure. Governance must ask who bears the privacy risk, who can refuse a secondary use, and how contributors share in the benefits.
