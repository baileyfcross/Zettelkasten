# Scalar Field

A scalar field assigns one numerical value to every point in a region of space. Temperature, mass density, and electrostatic potential are examples because each location is described by magnitude alone rather than by a direction.

Spatial derivatives reveal how the field varies. In particular, the gradient of a scalar field points in the direction of greatest local increase, while level surfaces collect points at which the field has the same value.
