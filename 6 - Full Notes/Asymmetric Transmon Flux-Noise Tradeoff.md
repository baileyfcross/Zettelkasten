# Asymmetric Transmon Flux-Noise Tradeoff

An asymmetric transmon uses two SQUID junctions with unequal Josephson energies. Unlike a symmetric split transmon, its effective Josephson energy does not vanish at half a flux quantum, so its transition frequency remains bounded within a narrower interval.

The restricted tuning range can be advantageous. Because the frequency changes less across the available flux span, the device has lower flux susceptibility and is less vulnerable to flux-noise dephasing. A modest range may still be enough to compensate fabrication variation, satisfy a desired frequency arrangement, or activate a specific gate while avoiding frequency crowding.

The asymmetry parameter therefore encodes an explicit engineering choice: more tunability gives greater routing and gate freedom, while less tunability improves coherence. The right value depends on the intended interaction scheme, including whether the processor relies on flux excursions, fixed-frequency [[Cross-Resonance Gate]] operations, or a separate tunable coupler.
