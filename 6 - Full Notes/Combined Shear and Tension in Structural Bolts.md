# Combined Shear and Tension in Structural Bolts

A structural bolt may resist shear and axial tension simultaneously in an end plate, bracket, moment connection, or eccentrically loaded joint. Because the same fastener capacity cannot be independently used in full for both actions, an interaction relationship reduces the allowable or design tension as shear demand increases.

The force assigned to each bolt comes from the direct load plus any moment-induced distribution across the group. Pretension and prying can further affect tension demand, while thread location affects shear resistance. Connection design therefore separates joint behavior from bolt strength: slip may be checked at one load level, bearing and shear at another, and combined bolt fracture with the specification's interaction equation.
