# Inertial Reference Frame

An inertial reference frame is a coordinate system in which a body free of forces moves at constant velocity along a straight [[Worldline]]. Any observer moving uniformly relative to such a frame can use another inertial frame, and special relativity requires the laws of physics to have the same form in all of them.

No inertial frame is an absolute state of rest. Two inertial observers can each regard themselves as stationary and the other as moving, provided their measurements are related by a [[Lorentz Transformation]]. Acceleration marks a departure from a single inertial frame because the observer's velocity—and therefore the orientation of the observer's spacetime axes—changes.

An inertial frame is a physics-specific form of a [[Reference Frame]]: it supplies coordinates and clocks while imposing the additional condition of unaccelerated motion.
