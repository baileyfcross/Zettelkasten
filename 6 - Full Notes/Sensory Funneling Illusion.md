# Sensory Funneling Illusion

The sensory funneling illusion occurs when several brief, simultaneous, closely spaced skin stimuli are perceived as one stronger stimulus at a central location. The experienced point need not correspond to any physical actuator.

Funneling reveals that tactile location and intensity are inferred from a population of inputs rather than assigned independently to each contact. It can be used in a tactile display to create an apparent intermediate position with fewer actuators, but uncontrolled overlap can also collapse signals that were intended to remain distinct.
