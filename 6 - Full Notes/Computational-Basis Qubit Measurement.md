# Computational-Basis Qubit Measurement

Measuring $|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle$ in the computational basis returns classical result $0$ with probability $|\alpha|^2$ and result $1$ with probability $|\beta|^2$. Repetition estimates these probabilities; one shot reveals only one outcome.

The measurement does more than read a hidden classical value. It projects the post-measurement state onto the basis vector associated with the observed result, so later operations act on $|0\rangle$ or $|1\rangle$ rather than on the original superposition.
