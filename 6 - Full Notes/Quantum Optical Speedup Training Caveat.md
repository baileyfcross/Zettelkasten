# Quantum Optical Speedup Training Caveat

The strongest resource claim applies to classifying one new object after an effective probe has already been learned. Finding that probe is a separate problem. Classically simulating the full optical apparatus can require exponential resources, although it may reduce laboratory iteration, and an unseen task still needs training examples, optimizer steps, and parameter updates.

Direct experimental training can preserve the optical implementation of overlaps, but its total cost depends on the number of epochs, the complexity of the pattern, gradient or coordinate measurements, and detector repetitions. Parameters transferred from a related classical model may reduce this effort when such a model is available. Optical absorption adds another constant factor tied to object reflectivity. Consequently, [[Post-Training Resolution-Independent Optical Inference]] should be reported as an inference scaling result rather than an unconditional end-to-end complexity bound.
