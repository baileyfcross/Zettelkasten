# Second-Reader Computer-Aided Diagnosis

Second-reader computer-aided diagnosis presents algorithmic findings after a clinician has performed an initial image interpretation. Its purpose is to reduce missed lesions by directing attention to suspicious candidates, not to replace the clinician's judgment.

Clinical value depends on more than sensitivity. Too many false positives consume review time and can reduce acceptance, while poor integration into the radiologist's workflow can nullify an accurate algorithm. The operating point should therefore be selected for the reading task, and candidate presentation must help the reader distinguish genuine findings from false alarms efficiently.
