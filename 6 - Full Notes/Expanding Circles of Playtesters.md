# Expanding Circles of Playtesters

Expanding circles of playtesters move a game from the designer's own repeated tests to trusted friends, less familiar acquaintances, and eventually a broad online audience. Each circle trades familiarity and control for a wider range of first impressions, backgrounds, devices, and behaviors.

Self-testing is fastest for early mechanics but cannot provide a naïve reaction. Trusted people can tolerate unfinished work, acquaintances expose shared social biases, and internet testing supplies scale only after the build and data collection are ready. The circle should widen as the questions and operational risks change.
