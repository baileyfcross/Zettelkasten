# Embodied Interface for Brain Emulation

An emulated brain needs an interface that translates simulated neural activity into actions and converts environmental events into sensory signals. Reproducing a brain without its body omits the peripheral nervous system, bodily regulation, and sensorimotor loops on which its learned organization depended.

One approach maps the emulation onto a robot and lets both sides adapt through practice, much as a person learns to operate an unfamiliar prosthesis. Behavioral similarity then depends on the joint system rather than the brain simulation alone. The body is not merely an output device; it participates in stabilizing and interpreting cognition.
