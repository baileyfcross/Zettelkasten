# Turing Universality and Programmability

Alan Turing described a formal machine that could simulate any other rule-governed computation when supplied with an appropriate description. This made programmability, rather than a fixed mechanical purpose, the defining quality of a general computer.

The model used an unbounded symbolic tape and elementary state transitions, not practical hardware. Its importance was to show that one finite mechanism could realize an open-ended range of procedures.
