# Remote Sensing Spatial Resolution

Remote-sensing spatial resolution is the smallest ground area represented by a measurement, commonly a pixel. Finer resolution can distinguish smaller features, while coarser resolution combines more of the surface into each value. Resolution also varies across a sensor's field of view: measurements near the point directly below a satellite are generally more accurate than those farther away.

The appropriate resolution depends on the question. Monitoring global land cover does not require the centimeter detail needed to inspect individual crops, and demanding finer pixels increases the volume of data that must be transmitted, stored, and processed.
