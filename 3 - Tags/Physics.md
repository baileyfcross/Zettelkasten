# Physics

Physics is the chapter-level topic for measurement, mechanics, matter, heat, waves, fields, relativity, quantum theory, cosmology, and medical applications. Full Notes link to the focused child topics below rather than directly to this page.

## Overview Chapter

Physics builds quantitative explanations by connecting measured patterns to mathematical models. A useful law does more than attach numbers to observations: it identifies which quantities matter, states how they relate, predicts unfamiliar cases, and remains open to experimental testing. The subject therefore moves continually between measurement and abstraction. Units and uncertainties constrain what an experiment can claim, while conservation laws, fields, waves, and state variables let the same reasoning recur across very different systems.

### Measurement and evidence

[[Physical Measurement and Experimental Analysis]] supplies the language needed before any physical result can be trusted. The [[SI Base Unit|SI base units]] define independent standards, and a [[Derived SI Unit]] combines them for quantities such as force, pressure, energy, and power. [[Dimensional Analysis]] checks whether an equation is homogeneous and can narrow the possible structure of a proposed law. [[Significant Figures]] and [[Measurement Uncertainty]] prevent a calculated value from claiming more precision than its data support.

Experimental variation must also be interpreted rather than hidden. [[Random Error]] produces scatter that can be reduced through repetition, whereas [[Systematic Error]] shifts results consistently and requires calibration or a changed method. An [[Error Bar]] places uncertainty directly on a graph. Transformed plots then test candidate relationships: [[Direct Proportionality]] produces a line through the origin, while an [[Inverse-Square Law]] becomes linear when plotted against reciprocal distance squared.

### Motion, force, momentum, and energy

[[Kinematics and Newtonian Motion]] describes how position changes before explaining why. [[Displacement]] is directed position change, [[Linear Velocity]] is its time derivative, and [[Acceleration]] is the derivative of velocity. The [[Constant-Acceleration Equations]] connect these quantities when acceleration is uniform. [[Projectile Motion]] separates horizontal and vertical components, while [[Free Fall]] identifies the special case in which gravity alone acts.

Forces enter through a [[Free-Body Diagram]], which isolates one object and every external interaction acting on it. [[Newton's First Law]] defines inertial behavior, [[Newton's Second Law]] relates resultant force to momentum change, and [[Newton's Third Law]] pairs the forces two bodies exert on each other. These laws apply to each chosen system, so correct boundaries and force assignments matter as much as algebra.

[[Mechanical Equilibrium, Momentum, and Energy]] extends that analysis to whole interactions. A [[Resultant Force]] controls translational acceleration, while the [[Center of Gravity]] and [[Torque]] govern stability and rotation. The [[Principle of Moments]] requires both zero net force and zero net turning effect for static equilibrium. [[Static Friction]] adjusts up to a limiting value rather than always equaling its maximum.

[[Linear Momentum]] and [[Impulse]] express how forces accumulate through time. [[Conservation of Linear Momentum]] follows when external impulse is absent, even if collision energy changes form. [[Work]] instead accumulates force through displacement and connects directly to kinetic-energy change. [[Mechanical Energy Conservation]] applies when conservative forces exchange kinetic and potential energy; dissipative processes preserve total energy while transferring some mechanical energy into internal forms.

### Fluids, solids, and heat

[[Fluid Mechanics and Material Properties]] connects distributed forces to material response. [[Hydrostatic Pressure]] grows with depth because of fluid weight, and [[Archimedes' Principle]] turns the resulting pressure difference into buoyancy. [[Dynamic and Kinematic Viscosity Units|Viscosity]] resists shear and helps determine whether flow remains [[Laminar Flow|laminar]] or becomes [[Turbulent Flow|turbulent]]. The [[Continuity Equation for Fluid Flow]] preserves mass through a changing cross-section, while the [[Bernoulli Equation]] balances pressure, kinetic, and gravitational energy density along an ideal streamline.

Solids respond to load through deformation. [[Hooke's Law]] describes the initial linear restoring relation, [[Young's Modulus]] measures stiffness through stress divided by strain, and a [[Stress-Strain Curve]] distinguishes proportional, elastic, yielding, ultimate-strength, and fracture behavior. These material quantities remove specimen dimensions so different samples of the same substance can be compared.

[[Thermal and Statistical Physics]] links macroscopic state variables to microscopic motion. [[Thermal Equilibrium]] permits temperature comparison. [[Specific Heat Capacity]] measures energy needed for temperature change, whereas [[Specific Latent Heat]] describes energy transferred during a phase change without temperature change. The [[Ideal Gas Equation]] combines pressure, volume, amount, and absolute temperature, while the [[Kinetic Theory of Gases]] explains pressure through molecular impacts and [[Temperature as Molecular Kinetic Energy]].

The [[First Law of Thermodynamics]] treats heat and work as energy transfers that alter internal energy. The [[Second Law of Thermodynamics]] restricts which energy-conserving changes occur spontaneously. [[Entropy]] describes that direction both through reversible heat transfer and through the multiplicity of microscopic arrangements. A [[Heat Engine]] illustrates the consequence: some absorbed heat can become work, but some must be rejected to a colder reservoir.

### Oscillations, waves, and sound

[[Oscillations and Rotational Dynamics]] develops repeated motion and its rotational analogues. [[Simple Harmonic Motion]] follows when acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed toward equilibrium. A [[Mass-Spring Oscillator]] and [[Simple Pendulum]] realize the model under ideal or small-angle conditions. [[Damping]] removes energy, while [[Resonance]] produces a large driven response near a natural frequency.

Rotation uses quantities parallel to linear mechanics. [[Angular Velocity]] measures rotational rate, and [[Centripetal Acceleration]] is required whenever velocity changes direction around a circle. [[Moment of Inertia]] replaces mass as resistance to angular acceleration, [[Angular Momentum]] changes under torque, and [[Conservation of Angular Momentum]] holds when external torque is absent.

[[Wave Motion and Geometric Optics]] describes disturbances that transport energy without transporting matter over the full path. A [[Transverse Wave]] oscillates across its direction of travel, while a [[Longitudinal Wave]] oscillates along it. The [[Traveling Wave Equation]] combines spatial and temporal phase, and [[Wave Intensity]] connects amplitude to power per area. Boundaries produce [[Reflection]] and refraction governed by [[Snell's Law]]. At sufficiently large incidence angle, [[Total Internal Reflection]] confines light, while [[Polarization]] reveals transverse orientation. The [[Thin Lens Equation]] models image formation, and the [[Doppler Effect]] relates observed frequency to relative motion.

[[Interference, Diffraction, and Acoustics]] begins with [[Wave Superposition]], where overlapping disturbances add. [[Double-Slit Interference]] and a [[Diffraction Grating]] convert path differences into bright and dark directions. [[Single-Slit Diffraction]] sets a finite spread even for an ideal beam, and the [[Rayleigh Criterion]] turns that spread into a resolution limit. Oppositely traveling waves form a [[Standing Wave]], whose allowed modes depend on boundaries. Sound intensity is compared on the [[Decibel Scale]], [[Resonance in Air Columns]] produces musical harmonics, [[Fourier Analysis of Sound]] separates complex timbre into frequencies, and [[Ultrasound]] extends acoustic methods beyond human hearing.

### Electric and magnetic fields

[[Electrostatics and DC Circuits]] begins with conserved, quantized [[Electric Charge]]. [[Coulomb's Law]] gives the inverse-square force between point charges, [[Electric Field Strength]] expresses force per unit charge, and [[Gauss's Law]] relates closed-surface flux to enclosed charge. [[Electric Potential]] expresses energy per unit charge and offers a scalar alternative to vector field addition.

In circuits, [[Electric Current]] is charge flow rate. [[Kirchhoff's Current Law]] applies charge conservation at a junction, while [[Kirchhoff's Voltage Law]] applies energy conservation around a loop. [[Electrical Resistance]] relates voltage and current while accounting for material and geometry, and [[Electrical Power]] gives the rate at which circuits transfer energy.

[[Capacitance and Magnetic Fields]] joins stored electric fields to magnetic forces. [[Capacitance]] measures charge storage per voltage, and a [[Parallel-Plate Capacitor]] makes its geometric dependence explicit. [[Capacitor Energy]], [[Capacitor Charging]], and [[Capacitor Discharging]] connect field storage to exponential RC behavior. [[Magnetic Flux Density]] measures magnetic force capacity, while the [[Lorentz Force]] acts perpendicular to a moving charge. The [[Biot-Savart Law]] builds magnetic fields from current elements and [[Ampere's Law]] simplifies highly symmetric cases. An [[Electric Motor]] turns the force on current into sustained torque.

[[Electromagnetic Induction and AC Circuits]] treats changing fields. [[Magnetic Flux]] records field through area, [[Faraday's Law]] makes changing flux linkage produce emf, and [[Lenz's Law]] fixes the opposing direction required by energy conservation. [[Self-Inductance]] describes a circuit's response to its own changing current, while [[Mutual Inductance]] couples two circuits and enables the [[Transformer]]. In [[Alternating Current]] systems, [[RMS Voltage]] compares heating power with DC. [[Inductive Reactance]] grows with frequency, and [[Electrical Impedance]] combines resistance and phase-dependent reactance.

### Gravity, relativity, atoms, and the universe

[[Gravitation and Relativity]] moves from Newtonian action at a distance to spacetime geometry. [[Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation]] gives an inverse-square attraction, [[Gravitational Field Strength]] gives force per unit mass, and [[Gravitational Potential]] gives energy per unit mass. [[Escape Velocity]] and [[Orbital Motion]] follow from energy and centripetal requirements. The [[Equivalence Principle]] connects gravity with acceleration, and [[Gravitational Time Dilation]] shows that clock rate depends on gravitational potential.

[[Special Relativity]] preserves the laws of physics and light speed across inertial frames by making measured space and time frame-dependent. [[Time Dilation]] changes elapsed time between frames, and [[Length Contraction]] changes dimensions parallel to relative motion. Both effects preserve a common spacetime interval rather than representing mechanical distortions of matter.

[[Atomic and Nuclear Physics]] probes matter below the atomic scale. [[Rutherford Scattering]] locates atomic mass and positive charge in a compact nucleus. [[Ionizing Radiation]], [[Radioactive Decay]], and [[Half-Life]] describe spontaneous nuclear emissions statistically. [[Nuclear Binding Energy]] connects mass defect to stability; [[Nuclear Fission]] and [[Nuclear Fusion]] release energy by moving nuclei toward greater binding per nucleon. At a deeper level, [[Lepton]]s and [[Quark]]s supply the matter content organized by the [[Standard Model]].

[[Quantum Physics and Cosmology]] begins where classical predictions fail. [[Blackbody Radiation]] motivates quantized energy, and the [[Photoelectric Effect]] reveals photons. The [[de Broglie Wavelength]] assigns wave behavior to matter, confirmed by [[Electron Diffraction]], while [[Wave-Particle Duality]] prevents either classical picture from standing alone. The [[Schrodinger Equation]] evolves probability amplitudes, the [[Bohr Model]] anticipates discrete atomic energies, and the [[Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle]] limits simultaneous spreads of conjugate quantities. On cosmic scales, [[Hubble's Law]] reveals expansion and [[Big Bang Evidence]] connects that expansion with a hot early universe.

### Physics in medical imaging

[[Medical Imaging Physics]] applies these principles to diagnosis and treatment. [[Medical Ultrasound Imaging]] forms echoes from acoustic-impedance boundaries, [[Doppler Ultrasound]] measures blood motion, and [[Lithotripsy]] focuses pressure pulses to fragment stones. An [[X-Ray Tube]] converts accelerated-electron energy into radiation, [[X-Ray Attenuation]] determines transmission through tissue, and [[Radiographic Image Formation]] balances contrast against dose and scatter.

[[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]] encodes hydrogen-nucleus signals with magnetic-field gradients without ionizing radiation. A [[Radioactive Tracer]] instead places a detectable source inside a physiological process. A [[Gamma Camera]] maps emitted photons, while [[Positron Emission Tomography]] reconstructs coincident annihilation events. These systems show that practical physics requires more than a governing equation: sources, interactions, detectors, uncertainty, safety, and reconstruction all shape what a measurement means.

Across the discipline, the same habits recur. Define the system, identify conserved quantities and interactions, choose a representation, track units and signs, state approximations, and compare prediction with evidence. Physics gains its unity not because every phenomenon looks alike, but because this method lets motion, heat, waves, fields, nuclei, and galaxies be examined through explicit relationships that can fail as well as succeed.
