# Attention

Attention selects some information for further processing while inhibiting other information. It includes orienting to sensory events, detecting signals for focused processing, and maintaining alertness.

Selective attention chooses what to process, focused attention maintains processing despite distraction, and divided attention attempts to process more than one event at once. Moving attention requires disengaging from one location, shifting, and reengaging elsewhere.

Attention is limited. Overload, peripheral vision limits, rapid events, or divided tasks can cause missed changes and errors. An interface can direct attention toward urgent information, but competing cues may also distract from the primary task.

Color, size, motion, light, sound, orientation, paths, and arrows can direct a player's focus. A designer who can predict where the player looks can also place a reveal outside that focus, while repeated [[Task-Switching Cost|task switching]] makes learning slower and more error-prone.
