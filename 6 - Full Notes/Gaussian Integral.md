# Gaussian Integral

The Gaussian integral evaluates the area under $e^{-x^2}$ over the real line. Because this function has no elementary antiderivative, the usual derivation squares the integral, interprets the product as a double integral, and changes from Cartesian to polar coordinates.

Rotational symmetry then separates the angular and radial integrations, yielding $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}dx=\sqrt{\pi}$. Scaling the exponent extends the result to Gaussian functions with other widths, which recur in probability, diffusion, and quantum mechanics.
