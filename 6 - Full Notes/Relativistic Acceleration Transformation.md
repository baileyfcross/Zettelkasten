# Relativistic Acceleration Transformation

Relativistic acceleration transformation relates the acceleration measured in an object's instantaneous rest frame to the acceleration assigned by another inertial observer. Because both time intervals and longitudinal lengths transform, acceleration is not invariant between frames.

For acceleration parallel to the relative velocity, the coordinate acceleration seen by the outside observer is reduced by a factor involving $\gamma^3$. For acceleration perpendicular to the motion, the transformation has a different power of gamma. This direction dependence distinguishes acceleration from a simple scalar rate.

As the observed speed approaches $c$, a finite [[Proper Acceleration]] corresponds to a coordinate acceleration that approaches zero. The limiting speed therefore follows from spacetime kinematics; it does not require the heuristic claim that an object's mass must become infinite.
