# Relativistic Mass Convention

Relativistic mass is an older convention that defines a velocity-dependent quantity $m_{rel}=\gamma m$, allowing momentum to be written as $\mathbf{p}=m_{rel}\mathbf{v}$ and total energy as $E=m_{rel}c^2$.

The convention reproduces correct numerical relations, but it can obscure which quantity is invariant. The particle's [[Invariant Mass]] $m$ does not change between inertial frames; energy and momentum are the frame-dependent components of [[Four-Momentum]]. Modern treatments usually keep mass invariant and write $\mathbf{p}=\gamma m\mathbf{v}$ and $E=\gamma mc^2$.

The source uses relativistic mass while also distinguishing it from rest mass. Reading its statements about mass increasing with velocity therefore requires recognizing the convention rather than interpreting the invariant mass itself as changing.
