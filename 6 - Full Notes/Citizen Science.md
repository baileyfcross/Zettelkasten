# Citizen Science

Citizen science is research in which people who are not acting as professional scientists participate in collecting, interpreting, or using evidence. In self-tracking, participants can contribute personal observations, investigate shared conditions, and formulate questions that established institutions have overlooked.

Participation ranges from supplying data to helping define the problem and method. A project that only recruits inexpensive data providers offers less public influence than one in which participants can inspect results, challenge categories, and shape decisions.

Citizen science can widen discovery, but it does not remove problems of measurement, selection, privacy, or unequal access. Its value depends on transparent methods and a fair relationship between contributors and the organizations that aggregate their work.
