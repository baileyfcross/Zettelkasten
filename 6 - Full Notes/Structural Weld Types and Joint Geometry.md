# Structural Weld Types and Joint Geometry

Structural joints may be lap, butt, corner, or tee configurations, and the selected weld must match access, force direction, preparation, and inspection needs. Fillet welds join surfaces meeting at an angle and are widely used because they need little edge preparation. Plug and slot welds transfer shear through holes, while groove welds join prepared edges.

Groove welds may have partial or complete joint penetration. Complete penetration can develop the connected material across the joint but requires more preparation, filler metal, and inspection; partial penetration can be more economical when its calculated throat is sufficient. Flat welding is generally easiest, while vertical and overhead positions add difficulty. Joint geometry therefore controls both resistance and the practical probability of producing the specified weld quality.
