# Low Earth Orbit

Low Earth orbit is the near-Earth orbital region commonly defined from about 100 to 1,200 miles in altitude. Objects circle Earth in roughly ninety minutes to a few hours, and missions can use many inclinations to obtain different ground coverage.

Most operational spacecraft in the region fly between about 200 and 600 miles. Lower trajectories decay more quickly because of drag from the tenuous outer atmosphere, while higher parts encounter stronger radiation and longer-lived debris. Earth observation, reconnaissance, scientific satellites, human spacecraft, and large constellations all use this crowded region.
