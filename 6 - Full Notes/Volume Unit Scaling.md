# Volume Unit Scaling

A cubic unit contains three powers of length, so a length-scale change must affect volume by the cube of that ratio. Pryor's conversion table includes cubic feet, cubic meters, liters, and acre-feet; his acre-foot example first reaches cubic meters and then liters. This two-step route makes clear that a unit may encode a special area-times-length construction while still describing volume. Writing $\mathrm{m^3}$ or L explicitly helps detect a mistaken area or length factor. Liquid and dry variants also require the right [[Unit Name Qualifiers in Conversion Tables|unit-name qualifier]]. See [[Area Unit Scaling]] and [[Volumetric Flow Rate Conversion]].
