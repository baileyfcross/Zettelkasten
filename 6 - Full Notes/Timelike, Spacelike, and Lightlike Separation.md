# Timelike, Spacelike, and Lightlike Separation

The sign of the [[Invariant Spacetime Interval]] classifies the separation between two events. With the convention $\Delta s^2=c^2\Delta t^2-\Delta\mathbf{x}^2$, a positive interval is timelike, a zero interval is lightlike, and a negative interval is spacelike.

Timelike-separated events can be joined by a massive object's [[Worldline]], so every inertial observer agrees which event comes first. Lightlike-separated events can be connected only at light speed and form the boundary of the [[Light Cone]]. Spacelike-separated events are too far apart for a light-speed or slower influence during the available time; different inertial frames may reverse their coordinate-time order without reversing a causal process.

Because Lorentz transformations preserve the interval, an event pair cannot move from one class to another merely because the observer changes velocity.
