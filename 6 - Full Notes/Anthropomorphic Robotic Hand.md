# Anthropomorphic Robotic Hand

An anthropomorphic robotic hand adopts a human-like arrangement of articulated fingers and an opposing digit. This geometry is useful when the robot must use human tools, share human workspaces, or be controlled by a human master device with an intuitive movement mapping.

Copying appearance does not reproduce human capability. The biological hand combines many degrees of freedom, remote forearm muscles, compliant skin, and thousands of sensors within low mass. A robotic hand may exceed human positional precision while remaining inferior at robust distributed touch and adaptive manipulation.
