# Fast-Adapting Mechanoreceptor

A fast-adapting mechanoreceptor responds strongly when the skin begins or stops moving but becomes quiet during an unchanging indentation. It therefore emphasizes change: first contact, slip, motion across the skin, and vibration.

Different fast-adapting units cover different frequency ranges. Units with small receptive fields contribute to detecting local movement and grip slip, while deeply located units with large fields are especially sensitive to high-frequency vibration. Their transient response complements the sustained information supplied by a [[Slowly Adapting Mechanoreceptor]].
