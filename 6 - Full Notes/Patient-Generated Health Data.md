# Patient-Generated Health Data

Patient-generated health data are observations recorded by patients or their tools during everyday life rather than produced solely during a clinical encounter. They can include symptoms, activity, mood, medication, environmental context, and physiological sensor readings.

These records can reveal episodes and routines that a short appointment misses. They are especially relevant to chronic conditions managed mainly at home, where treatment, work, food, sleep, and social circumstances interact continuously.

Everyday relevance does not make the data automatically clinical. Devices may be unvalidated, intervals inconsistent, and labels personally defined. Productive use requires documenting how the observations were produced and deciding whether the question calls for personal insight, a clinical decision, or formal research evidence.
