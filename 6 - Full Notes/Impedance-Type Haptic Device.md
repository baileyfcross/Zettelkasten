# Impedance-Type Haptic Device

An impedance-type haptic device measures the user's position or movement and returns a force calculated from the environment. A stylus entering a virtual object, for example, produces a resisting force that increases according to the modeled contact and deformation.

This architecture is common because position sensing and force actuation can be implemented relatively simply. Stable interaction still requires a rapid [[Haptic Rendering]] loop, low friction, and appropriate force bandwidth. Its inverse is an [[Admittance-Type Haptic Device]], which senses applied force and commands position.
