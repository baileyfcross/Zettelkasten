# Human Spaceflight in Low Earth Orbit

Human spaceflight in low Earth orbit is the post-Apollo pattern in which crews remained within a few hundred miles of Earth rather than traveling to the Moon or deeper space. Shuttles and stations made longer stays, repeated access, research, assembly, and cooperation possible within this relatively reachable region.

The pattern shows the difference between sustaining capability and extending distance. Since the final lunar mission in 1972, human programs preserved institutions and accumulated long-duration experience but did not overcome the cost, radiation, life-support, and political barriers of deep-space travel. Robotic exploration advanced much farther during the same period.
