# Gauge Invariance in Electromagnetism

Gauge invariance in electromagnetism is the freedom to change the electromagnetic potentials in a coordinated way without changing the electric and magnetic fields. In three-dimensional language, adding the gradient of a scalar function to the [[Vector Potential]] does not change its curl, and the scalar potential can be adjusted so the electric field also remains unchanged.

The freedom means the [[Electromagnetic Four-Potential]] is not a unique description of the field. Different potential functions related by a gauge transformation represent the same observable [[Electromagnetic Field Tensor]] and satisfy the same [[Maxwell's Equations]].

The source connects this local phase freedom to rotations in the complex plane and the group $U(1)$. Requiring the laws to remain invariant when the phase varies from point to point introduces the electromagnetic potential into the description.
