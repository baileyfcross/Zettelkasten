# Indoor Positioning Systems

Indoor positioning combines local signals and motion estimates when satellite navigation is blocked by roofs, walls, urban canyons, soil, or water. Wi-Fi routers and Bluetooth beacons can act as known reference points, signal strength can serve as an imperfect proxy for distance, and infrared sensors or pedestrian dead reckoning can bridge gaps.

Unlike GPS, these systems lack one authoritative global inventory of transmitters and their reliability. They therefore depend on maintained local maps and often fuse several technologies. The result can support navigation through hospitals, campuses, malls, and high-rise buildings, but its accuracy and continuity vary with the density and quality of local infrastructure.
