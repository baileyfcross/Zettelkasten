# Deep-Space Human Health Risk

Deep-space human health risk is the combined threat created by long-duration low gravity or weightlessness, cosmic radiation, isolation, and distance from rapid medical support. Evidence from orbital stations documents bone and other physiological changes, but missions beyond Earth's protective environment add conditions not fully reproduced in low Earth orbit.

The risk grows with travel time and makes a Moon base or Mars expedition more than a larger version of an orbital mission. Exercise and other countermeasures can reduce some effects, while radiation and partial-gravity adaptation remain serious uncertainties. These biological limits reinforce the cost advantage and reach of robotic exploration.
