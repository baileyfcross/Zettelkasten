# Quantum Computational Supremacy Criterion

A quantum processor meets the operational “quantum supremacy” criterion when it performs a specific computation in practical time that the largest available classical computers cannot perform in practical time. The criterion can be satisfied by one narrow task even if that task has no immediate application.

This is distinct from broad usefulness or superiority on all computation. It establishes a boundary in computational capability under a stated benchmark and classical comparison. Changes in classical algorithms, hardware, accuracy targets, or cost accounting can move that boundary, so the claim must name the task and verification standard.
