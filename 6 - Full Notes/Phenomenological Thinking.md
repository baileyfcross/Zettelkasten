# Phenomenological Thinking

Phenomenological thinking treats observed events as separate phenomena without necessarily looking for a deeper structure connecting them. It is useful for noticing what occurs, but it can miss the interactions and loops that produce recurring behavior.

When an apparent event is an expression of a [[System]], a purely phenomenological view may address symptoms rather than causes. [[Systems Thinking]] extends observation by asking how parts and feedback generate the phenomenon.
