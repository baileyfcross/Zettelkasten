# Neutral Playtest Observation

Neutral playtest observation records what players do, say, attempt, and appear to misunderstand without coaching them toward the intended solution. The facilitator introduces the session, watches and listens, and reserves interpretation or discussion until it cannot alter the observed behavior.

Useful records distinguish direct events from inferences. "The player selected the door three times" is evidence; "the door is confusing" is a diagnosis to evaluate with the rest of the session.

If the participant asks a question, the facilitator normally responds with a neutral prompt or asks why rather than supplying the answer. Intervention is reserved for a point where the entire session would otherwise stop, and that give-up point becomes evidence itself.
