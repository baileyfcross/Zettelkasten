# Game Mechanic

A game mechanic is an identifiable interactive loop formed from [[Game Token|tokens]], [[Game Rule|rules]], the player, and the game. It may be brief, such as drawing a card, or contain many subsystems and take much longer, such as building an empire.

Mechanics can recur across otherwise different games because their loop structure is separable from a particular theme. Repeated central mechanics become [[Core Gameplay Loop|core gameplay]] and often help define a [[Game Genre]].

A mechanic becomes legible through a vocabulary of [[Game Verb|player verbs]], objects, and their relationships. Its quality depends less on the number of available actions than on how strongly those actions create choices and reinforce one another.

Mechanics are rules that govern use, while [[Game Attribute|attributes]] are values that influence those rules. Two objects can therefore share the same movement mechanic yet behave differently because one has a greater movement value. This separation lets a designer change gameplay substantially through data without changing the encoded or written rule itself.
