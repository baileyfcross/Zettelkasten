# Medical Compliance

Medical compliance is the degree to which a patient's behavior follows a prescribed treatment, schedule, or health recommendation. Self-tracking tools are often proposed as ways to measure adherence and provide reminders or incentives.

The term centers professional instructions and can hide why a regimen does not fit everyday life. Work, cost, transportation, caregiving, housing, food access, and side effects can make a recommendation difficult even when the patient understands it. More data about failure to comply does not remove those constraints.

Some recommendations protect both the person and the public, so adherence can matter greatly. The useful question is whether data supports care in context or merely makes the individual more visible while treating structural problems as personal shortcomings.
