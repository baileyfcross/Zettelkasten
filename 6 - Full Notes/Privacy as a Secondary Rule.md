# Privacy as a Secondary Rule

Privacy as a secondary rule treats privacy as a constraint on how legal powers are recognized, changed, and applied. It does not operate only as a primary command telling particular actors not to disclose information.

This view makes privacy part of the architecture of legitimate governance. In data-intensive societies, it limits the reach of observation and decision systems so that legal subjects retain the autonomy needed to participate in the legal order.
