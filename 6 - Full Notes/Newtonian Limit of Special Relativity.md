# Newtonian Limit of Special Relativity

The Newtonian limit of special relativity is the regime $v\ll c$, where relativistic expressions reduce to their classical approximations. In this limit the [[Lorentz Factor]] satisfies $\gamma\approx1$, time dilation and length contraction become negligible, and a [[Lorentz Transformation]] approaches a Galilean transformation.

The relativistic velocity rule becomes ordinary addition because $uv/c^2$ is tiny. Relativistic momentum approaches $m\mathbf{v}$, and the expansion of total energy separates into a constant rest-energy term plus the Newtonian kinetic energy $\tfrac12mv^2$.

This correspondence explains why Newtonian mechanics remains accurate for ordinary engineering speeds even though its assumptions are not exact. Special relativity replaces classical mechanics rather than making it unusable within the domain where its correction terms are too small to matter.
