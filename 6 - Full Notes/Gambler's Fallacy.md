# Gambler's Fallacy

The gambler's fallacy is the belief that an independent random process remembers earlier outcomes and will compensate for them. After several losses, a player may believe a win is due even though the probability of the next trial is unchanged.

The error confuses long-run proportions with short-run correction. If each encounter independently has a one-in-four chance of an event, an unusual early cluster does not reduce the event's probability for the remaining players.
