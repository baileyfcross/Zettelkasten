# Simulator Aftereffect

A simulator aftereffect is a perceptual or postural disturbance that continues after an immersive experience ends. Dizziness, difficulty focusing, disorientation, or instability may persist for minutes or hours even after the display is removed.

Repeated exposure can reduce nausea as a user adapts to an unusual sensory relationship, but adaptation does not guarantee immediate normal performance afterward. The learned compensation may itself produce [[Postural Instability Theory|postural instability]] when the person returns to ordinary visual and vestibular conditions.

Aftereffects make the end of a session part of system safety. A participant may feel able to stop using the display yet still be unready to drive, operate equipment, or walk through a hazardous area. Evaluation should therefore include recovery observation and conservative stopping procedures, not only symptoms reported during [[Virtual Reality]].
