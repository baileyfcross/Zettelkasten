# Natural Frequency of Steel Floor Systems

Natural frequency is the rate at which a floor tends to oscillate after displacement and release. It rises with stiffness and falls as participating mass increases. For a regular floor, simplified calculations relate frequency to the dead-plus-sustained-live-load deflection of the beam or joist and its supporting girder; a combined system is more flexible than either component considered alone.

Composite section properties are commonly used for vibration even when strength design treats a floor as noncomposite, because small dynamic interface forces can be transferred through deck attachment. Tall-building columns can add axial deformation to the modal flexibility. Natural frequency alone does not determine acceptability, but it identifies possible resonance with walking or rhythmic harmonics and supplies a key input to peak-acceleration checks.
