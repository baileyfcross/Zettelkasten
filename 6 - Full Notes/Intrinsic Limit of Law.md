# Intrinsic Limit of Law

An intrinsic limit of law is a boundary that follows from the conditions under which a legal order can govern persons, rather than from a discretionary policy choice. The source argues that privacy can function as such a limit in an age of extensive data collection.

If law reaches every aspect of thought and conduct, the autonomy required to form intentions and direct a life is weakened. The limit protects a domain in which people can exist as agents subject to law rather than merely as objects of observation.
