# Calculus as a Computational Method

Calculus made continuous phenomena computational by relating local change to accumulated behavior. Derivatives describe how a quantity changes near a point, while integrals combine many local contributions into a larger result.

For machine calculation, continuous time or space is replaced by a finite grid. Difference equations then approximate the relationships between neighboring points, with smaller steps generally improving fidelity while increasing computational cost.
