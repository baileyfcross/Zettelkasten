# Rubber Hand Illusion

The rubber hand illusion occurs when synchronized stroking of a visible artificial hand and a person's hidden real hand makes touch seem located on the artificial hand. The perceived position of the real hand also shifts toward the visible substitute.

The artificial hand must plausibly match the real hand in shape, size, and orientation, and temporal synchrony is critical. The effect shows that body representation integrates vision, touch, and [[Kinesthesia|proprioceptive position]] rather than treating anatomical ownership as fixed. It is therefore a tool for studying the malleability of the body schema.
