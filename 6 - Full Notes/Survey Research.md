# Survey Research

Survey research obtains evidence by asking people directly about facts, opinions, experiences, intentions, or behavior. Questionnaires and interviews can be delivered by mail, electronically, in groups, face to face, in focused sessions, or by telephone, with each mode changing cost, coverage, response conditions, and interviewer influence.

A survey is a research design rather than merely a list of questions. The target population, sampling frame, mode, contact procedure, nonresponse plan, instrument, and analysis must fit the claim. A large response count does not repair systematic exclusion or ambiguous measurement.

Surveys are especially useful when the constructs of interest are known to respondents but cannot be observed directly. Their evidence remains conditional on what people understand, remember, disclose, and choose to report.
