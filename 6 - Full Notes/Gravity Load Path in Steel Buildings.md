# Gravity Load Path in Steel Buildings

A gravity load path is the continuous sequence by which roof and floor loads reach the foundation. Deck or slab collects area load and delivers it to joists or beams; beams deliver reactions to girders; girders deliver reactions to columns or walls; and vertical elements carry accumulated force into foundations and soil. Each transfer creates a member demand and a connection demand.

Good framing makes this path direct and legible. Tributary width and tributary area translate distributed surface loads into line loads and reactions, while openings, transfer members, discontinuous columns, and cantilevers introduce concentrated or eccentric effects. A strong member cannot compensate for a missing connection or interrupted support, so structural redundancy and integrity depend on checking the path as a system rather than as unrelated components.
