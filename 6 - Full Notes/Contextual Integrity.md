# Contextual Integrity

Contextual integrity is a view of privacy in which information is appropriately protected when its flow fits the expectations of a social context. The relevant questions include what information is involved, who supplied and receives it, for what purpose it was collected, and how it is allowed to move.

A privacy violation can occur without publishing a person's name. Activity data shared for a personal wellness service may feel invasive when transferred to an employer, insurer, advertiser, or unrelated researcher because the actors and purpose have changed. Deidentification alone does not repair that broken expectation.

Context is not permanently fixed. Technologies and relationships change, so privacy requires continuing negotiation and clear limits on new uses rather than a one-time assumption that any technically accessible data may be reused.
