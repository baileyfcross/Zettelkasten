# Postural Instability Theory

Postural instability theory proposes that motion-sickness symptoms can arise when a person cannot maintain stable control of posture over time. In an immersive display, visual motion and altered spatial cues may repeatedly disturb the strategies normally used to keep the body balanced.

This ecological account differs from [[Sensory Conflict Theory]] by treating prolonged instability, rather than cue disagreement itself, as the central cause. It suggests that sway and other measures of posture may provide warning before a participant reports severe [[Cybersickness]].

The theory remains one proposed explanation rather than a complete account. Its practical implication is that evaluation should observe the body as well as the display: stance, support, locomotion method, session duration, and the possibility of lingering instability can matter even when the user is not physically walking through the virtual scene.
