# Computational Unsolvability

A problem is computationally unsolvable when no algorithm can produce the required correct answer for every permitted input. This is a limit on computability itself, not merely on available time, memory, or engineering skill.

The [[Halting Problem]] supplies the central example. Similar questions about whether programs halt on all inputs, agree on every input, or ever halt from a blank configuration inherit the same kind of impossibility.
