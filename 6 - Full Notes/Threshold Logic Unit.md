# Threshold Logic Unit

A threshold logic unit sums weighted inputs and emits a high or low output depending on whether that sum crosses a fixed threshold. Kelleher describes early neural models that used Boolean inputs and outputs to represent logical relations. The McCulloch–Pitts model could implement simple operations such as conjunction and disjunction with hand-set excitatory or inhibitory weights.

Adding a learning rule led toward the [[Perceptron]], but a single threshold unit still creates a linear decision boundary. Its abrupt [[Activation Function|threshold activation]] also lacks the derivative required by ordinary [[Backpropagation]]. These limits help explain why later multilayer training used smoother activations.
