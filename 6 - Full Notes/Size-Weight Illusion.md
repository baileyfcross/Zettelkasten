# Size-Weight Illusion

The size-weight illusion is the persistent perception that a larger object feels lighter than a smaller object of equal mass. Expectations initially change grip and lift forces because a larger object is anticipated to be heavier, but the illusion remains even after those forces adapt to the true mass.

This persistence shows that perceived heaviness is not a direct reading of muscle force. Volume, prior expectation, and multisensory object representation influence the judgment alongside mass. Related effects occur when temperature, shape, density, or surface friction changes while physical weight remains constant.
