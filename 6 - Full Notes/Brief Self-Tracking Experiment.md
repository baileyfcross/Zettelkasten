# Brief Self-Tracking Experiment

A brief self-tracking experiment is a deliberately limited project run for a few days or weeks before a person commits to long-term collection. Its purpose is to test whether the question, measure, and recording routine are useful in practice.

Beginning briefly makes hidden costs visible. A category may prove ambiguous, a device may not export the required measurements, or the recording schedule may demand more attention than the result deserves. The person can then revise the project, continue it, or stop without treating discontinuation as failure.

Focusing on one or two observations protects purposeful attention and reduces [[Tracking Burden]]. Longer records are valuable for some questions, but duration should follow the need rather than become a goal by itself.
