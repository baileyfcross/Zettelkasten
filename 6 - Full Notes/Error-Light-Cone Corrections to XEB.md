# Error-Light-Cone Corrections to XEB

The effect of a localized circuit error on XEB is controlled by the smaller of its forward light cone toward measurement and its backward light cone toward the input. Propagating a Pauli error to either boundary shows that only a small subset of resulting strings can preserve the computational-basis correlation used by XEB, so the correction decreases exponentially with the light-cone support.

This input-output symmetry differs from fidelity, which records any departure from the ideal state rather than only the portion visible to the sampled probability correlation. An error placed where one XEB light cone is small can therefore leave a larger benchmark signal than its fidelity would suggest. The single-error reasoning also sets up [[Correlated-Error Masking in XEB]], where overlapping light cones invalidate an independent-error picture.
