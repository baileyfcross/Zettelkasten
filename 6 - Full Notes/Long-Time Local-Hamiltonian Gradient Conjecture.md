# Long-Time Local-Hamiltonian Gradient Conjecture

Long-time evolution under a local Hamiltonian is more structured than a Haar-random unitary because conserved energy restricts the states that the dynamics can explore. The paper argues that this restriction can prevent gradient second moments from reaching the inverse-exponential values associated with unitary 2-designs.

Its lower-bound expression depends on infinite-time averages of squared commutators, closely related to out-of-time-order correlators. Numerical experiments with random translationally invariant local Hamiltonians through sixteen qubits do not show exponential suppression: two-local models exhibit essentially no decay in the reported sizes, while some three- and four-local cases decrease without establishing exponential scaling.

The general polynomial claim remains a conjecture rather than a theorem. It assumes thermalizing behavior and an effectively uniform exploration of a local-Hamiltonian vector space; integrable and many-body-localized systems may behave differently. The [[Repeated-Parameter Hamiltonian Variational Ansatz]] uses this long-time picture as an expressivity route, while [[Short-Time Local-Hamiltonian Gradient Protection]] supplies the rigorous short-time guarantee.
