# Cryptographic Identification Scheme

A cryptographic identification scheme lets a prover convince a verifier of an identity claim while resisting impersonation during and after the exchange. The interaction terminates with acceptance or rejection rather than transferring unrestricted authority to the verifier.

Identification can rely on something known, possessed, or inherited. Passwords and PINs provide weaker approaches exposed to replay, guessing, and dictionary attacks; one-time passwords reduce reuse; interactive zero-knowledge protocols provide stronger evidence without simply revealing the prover's secret. The [[Feige-Fiat-Shamir Identification Protocol]] is the book's detailed example, evaluated through [[Zero-Knowledge Identification Properties]].
