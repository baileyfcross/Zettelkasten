# Survey Instrument Design

Survey instrument design translates research constructs into questions, response formats, instructions, and an order that respondents can understand and complete consistently. Existing validated measures should be considered before inventing new items because their performance and interpretation have already been studied.

Questions should avoid unnecessary jargon, hidden assumptions, double meanings, and response choices that do not cover plausible answers. Interesting and nonthreatening items generally belong early, related items can be grouped into clear sections, and sensitive questions are better placed after trust and context have been established.

Every part of the instrument should be pretested, including wording, navigation, instructions, timing, coding, and data capture. A [[Research Pilot Study]] can reveal whether the intended variables are actually produced before the full sample makes correction expensive or impossible.
