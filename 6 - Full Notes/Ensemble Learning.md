# Ensemble Learning

Ensemble learning combines predictions from multiple models so their different errors can offset one another. The component learners may be trained on different samples, features, or stages of a procedure.

The combined system can be more accurate or stable than one learner, but its value must be established through evaluation. Additional models also increase computation and can make the final decision harder to explain.

Combination is most useful when each component is competent and their errors are meaningfully different. Models trained with distinct assumptions, data subsets, or information sources can complement one another; repeating nearly identical learners supplies little independent evidence. The analogy is a committee whose members contribute different expertise rather than the same judgment several times.
