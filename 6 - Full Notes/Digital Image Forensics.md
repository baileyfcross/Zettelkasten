# Digital Image Forensics

Digital image forensics examines a photograph for evidence that its recorded history or visible content has been altered. It does not require a watermark or a signature embedded at capture; instead, it models the physical and computational stages that produced the image and tests whether their expected traces remain mutually consistent.

Those traces can occur in [[Image Metadata]], scene lighting and geometry, pixel statistics, camera-sensor artifacts, or [[JPEG Compression]]. Because each test rests on assumptions and has failure modes, a forensic conclusion should describe the particular evidence found rather than claim certainty from one detector.
