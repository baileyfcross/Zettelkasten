# Classical Coherent-Interferometer Baseline

A classical Mach-Zehnder interferometer can evaluate an overlap optically and therefore avoid a digital $N$-term dot product. That computational shortcut does not automatically remove the photon cost of loading an $N$-pixel pattern. In the paper's baseline, Fourier optics splits a multimode coherent state across $N$ spatial modes; maintaining at least one photon per modulator pixel requires mean photon number $\Omega(N)$.

The quantum proposal contrasts this factorized coherent field with a single-photon state spread across the $N$ spatial modes. Combined with a two-photon coincidence measurement, that state represents the modulator pattern without allocating an independent photon to every pixel. The comparison isolates the claimed optical advantage: both systems can perform the overlap through interference, but only the single-photon construction is argued to keep photon use independent of spatial resolution.
