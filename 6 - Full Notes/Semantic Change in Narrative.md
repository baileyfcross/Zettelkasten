# Semantic Change in Narrative

The book uses two complementary views of narrative semantics. A Euclidean factor map expresses the web of relationships among scenes and words; a hierarchy over their sequence can mark novelty, anomaly, or a major turn in that web.

In its Casablanca example, adjacent scenes that merge only high in the sequence-constrained tree signal stronger change than scenes joined low down. The same principle applies to sub-scenes or tweets. A breakpoint is an analytical indication for interpretation, not proof that one unique human reading is correct.
