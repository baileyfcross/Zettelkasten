# Pole-and-Barn Paradox

The pole-and-barn paradox considers a pole that is longer than a barn in their respective rest frames. In the barn's frame, [[Length Contraction]] can make the moving pole short enough for both ends to be momentarily inside. In the pole's frame, the moving barn is contracted and cannot contain the pole at one instant.

The descriptions agree once the [[Relativity of Simultaneity]] is included. “Both doors close at the same time” is a frame-dependent statement. Door events that are simultaneous in the barn frame occur at different times in the pole frame, so the pole-frame account never requires the entire pole to fit inside a simultaneously closed shortened barn.

The paradox demonstrates that length measurements require simultaneous endpoint positions in the measuring frame. Comparing contracted lengths without transforming the timing of the endpoint events mixes incompatible slices of spacetime.
