# Puzzle Construction

Puzzle construction creates a bounded problem whose rules, goal, and available operations invite the player to discover and execute a solution. A strong puzzle makes entry easy, provides immediate and legible feedback, keeps the next experiment attractive, and supports an appropriate level of difficulty.

The designer controls both the problem and the learning path. Rules must be simplified enough to expose the core insight, levels must be tested because predicted difficulty is unreliable, and new concepts should be sequenced from isolated use toward combinations with familiar mechanics. Presentation then makes the structure perceivable without giving away the solution.
