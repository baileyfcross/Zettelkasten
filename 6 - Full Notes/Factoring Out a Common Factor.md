# Factoring Out a Common Factor

Factoring out a common factor uses the [[Distributive Property]] backward. If every term contains (g), then

$$ga+gb+gc=g(a+b+c).$$

The usual choice is the [[Greatest Common Factor of Terms]], which leaves the simplest integer coefficients and lowest remaining exponents. A negative common factor can be useful when it makes the leading term inside parentheses positive. Expansion provides an immediate check that no term or sign was lost.
