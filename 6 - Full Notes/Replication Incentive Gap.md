# Replication Incentive Gap

The replication incentive gap arises when scientific careers reward novel, positive findings more strongly than careful attempts to verify earlier work. Researchers then face a rational pressure to invest scarce time in new claims even when replication would improve the reliability of the literature.

The gap also affects journals: a venue that publishes work rejected elsewhere can acquire low status, weakening the reward it is meant to provide. Large collaborations can reduce each contributor's cost through [[Crowdsourced Research Modularity]], while explicit authorship, training, and service incentives make participation professionally and intrinsically worthwhile.
