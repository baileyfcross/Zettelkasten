# Doubled-Hilbert-Space XEB Representation

XEB and fidelity can both be written as contractions of two copies of a circuit: one copy follows the ideal evolution, while the other follows the noisy or spoofed evolution. Averaging local random gates over a unitary 2-design collapses the doubled single-qubit space to two relevant correlation sectors, represented as a vacuum state $I$ and a particle state $\Omega$.

The bulk transfer dynamics are shared, but the final boundary vectors are not. Fidelity weights $I$ and $\Omega$ equally, whereas XEB assigns unequal weights, conventionally proportional to $(2,2/3)$. That boundary difference is the formal source of their disagreement once errors move the doubled system away from its homogeneous equilibrium. [[Diffusion-Reaction Model of Random-Circuit XEB]] translates the transfer matrix into a classical stochastic process.
