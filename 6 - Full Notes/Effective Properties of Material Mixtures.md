# Effective Properties of Material Mixtures

When a mixture's measured thermal property is unavailable, a rule of mixtures can estimate it from constituent properties and mass fractions. This approximation is most defensible when the components are distributed finely enough that a representative sample has consistent composition.

Homogeneous, inhomogeneous, and heterogeneous materials do not justify the same averaging assumptions. Resolved layers or inclusions may be necessary when local paths dominate [[Fourier Heat Conduction Law|conduction]], while an effective property may suffice at scales much larger than the mixture structure.
