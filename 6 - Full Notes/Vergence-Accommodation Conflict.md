# Vergence-Accommodation Conflict

Vergence-accommodation conflict occurs when a stereoscopic display asks the eyes to converge on a simulated depth while they must accommodate to the fixed physical surface of the display. In natural viewing, [[Vergence]] and [[Accommodation]] are tightly coupled: attention to a nearer or farther object changes both eye rotation and lens focus.

In a conventional stereoscopic [[Head-Worn Display]], binocular disparity can place a virtual object in front of or behind the screen, but the light still originates at one focal distance. The resulting decoupling can contribute to eye-muscle fatigue, visual discomfort, difficulty fusing images, [[Cybersickness]], and errors in perceived size or distance.

The conflict is a property of the display geometry, not simply of poor content. Large stereoscopic projection systems can produce it as well, and reducing it requires controlling simulated depth, viewing duration, or the optical focal cues presented to the eyes.
