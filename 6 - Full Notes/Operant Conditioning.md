# Operant Conditioning

Operant conditioning changes behavior by making rewards or punishments contingent on what the subject does. In a game, an action can become more or less likely depending on the response that follows it.

The timing pattern matters as much as the reward itself. Different schedules produce bursts, pauses, steady activity, or persistent repetition, so reinforcement should be designed for the behavior and rhythm the game intends rather than added as a generic incentive.
