# Longitude by Timekeeping

Longitude can be derived by comparing local solar time with the time at a reference meridian. Earth completes 360 degrees of rotation in roughly twenty-four hours, so a one-hour difference corresponds to fifteen degrees of longitude. The historical difficulty was not observing local noon but carrying an accurate reference clock through a moving ship.

The marine chronometer made that comparison dependable at sea and replaced much of the cumulative uncertainty of [[Pilotage and Dead Reckoning]]. The same relationship between position and precise time later became central to satellite navigation, where signal travel time is converted into distance.
