# Electromagnetic Wave Equation

The electromagnetic wave equation follows from the source-free [[Maxwell's Equations]]. Taking a time derivative of one curl equation, substituting the other, and using the divergence constraints produces second-order wave equations for both the electric and magnetic fields.

In vacuum the fields satisfy equations of the form

$$\nabla^2\mathbf{E}-\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2}=0,\qquad
\nabla^2\mathbf{B}-\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{B}}{\partial t^2}=0,$$

with $c=1/\sqrt{\epsilon_0\mu_0}$. The equations identify light and other electromagnetic radiation as coupled field waves.

Their propagation speed is built into the field laws rather than specified relative to an emitting source or receiving observer. [[Lorentz Covariance of Maxwell's Equations]] makes that same speed compatible with every inertial frame.
