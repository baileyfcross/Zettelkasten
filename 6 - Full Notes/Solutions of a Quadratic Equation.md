# Solutions of a Quadratic Equation

The solutions of a quadratic equation are the values that make its two sides equal. A factorable equation such as

$$x^2-x=0$$

becomes (x(x-1)=0), giving the two solutions (x=0) and (x=1).

A quadratic may have two distinct real solutions, one repeated real solution, or no real solution. Each candidate is checked by [[Substitution]] into the original equation, especially when the equation originally contained denominators or was transformed from another form.
