# Feedback Loop

A feedback loop exists when changes produced by interacting parts return to influence the conditions that produced them. This circular causality turns a connected process into a [[Complex System]].

The two broad forms are the [[Reinforcing Loop]], which magnifies a direction of change, and the [[Balancing Loop]], which counteracts deviation. Complex behavior commonly arises from several such loops operating together at different levels and timescales.

A live product creates an operational loop when a team plans a change, develops and deploys it, measures player response, and uses that evidence to decide what follows. The value of speed lies in shortening the complete learning cycle rather than implementation alone.

In competitive play, positive loops magnify early success or failure and can make the rich get richer, while negative loops hinder the leader or support the trailing player and narrow differences. The two can be combined, but hidden balancing may feel unfair when players cannot connect outcomes to visible rules.
