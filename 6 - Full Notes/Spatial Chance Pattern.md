# Spatial Chance Pattern

A spatial chance pattern is an apparent cluster, association, or anomaly produced by random coincidence rather than a stable underlying process. Searching many places, time windows, and variables increases the opportunity to find a visually persuasive pattern that will not persist.

Confidence measures, support thresholds, independent evidence, and domain review reduce false positives. The social cost matters as well: an incorrect disease or crime hot spot can stigmatize a neighborhood and redirect resources. A detected pattern should therefore be treated as a hypothesis to validate, not a conclusion guaranteed by its appearance on a map.
