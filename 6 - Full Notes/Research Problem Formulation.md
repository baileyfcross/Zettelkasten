# Research Problem Formulation

Research problem formulation converts an area of interest into a precise, consequential, and feasible question. It begins with an ordinary-language account of the problem and its rationale, then uses prior work and practical experience to identify the unknown relationship or outcome that the study can address.

A well-formed problem yields hypotheses or other testable implications. Those implications specify what evidence would count for or against the proposed explanation and help distinguish the central question from background concerns. This makes formulation the bridge between a [[Research Conceptual Framework]] and a [[Research Design]].

Solvability also matters. Available time, access, equipment, participants, expertise, and analysis capacity constrain which version of the problem can be answered responsibly. Narrowing an infeasible question is part of rigorous formulation, not a retreat from ambition.
