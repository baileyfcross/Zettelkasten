# Public Health Geospatial Visualization

Public health geospatial visualization maps disease, mortality, exposure, or service data to location. Spatial displays can reveal clusters and geographic disparities and can connect events with environmental or infrastructure context.

Counts must be interpreted with the population at risk, and mapped areas vary in size and composition. Aggregation can shift apparent patterns, while precise points can disclose sensitive information. A responsible map defines its denominator, scale, time window, and privacy treatment and avoids treating spatial proximity as evidence of causation.
