# First Law of Geography

The First Law of Geography states that things are related, but nearby things tend to be more related than distant things. It summarizes why location changes the assumptions of analysis: neighboring temperatures, soil properties, names, traffic readings, and disease cases are often dependent rather than interchangeable random samples.

The law is a modeling guide, not a guarantee for every observation. [[Spatial Autocorrelation]] measures similarity associated with proximity, while [[Spatial Outlier|spatial outliers]] are interesting precisely because they depart from their neighbors. Distance, scale, barriers, and the phenomenon being studied determine what “nearby” means.
