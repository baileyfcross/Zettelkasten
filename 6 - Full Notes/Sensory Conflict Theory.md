# Sensory Conflict Theory

Sensory conflict theory explains [[Cybersickness]] as a response to incompatible information from perceptual systems. A virtual scene may create a compelling visual sensation of self-motion while the vestibular and proprioceptive systems report that the body is stationary.

Conflict can also arise when present cues disagree with patterns learned through prior experience. The visual system describes orientation relative to the seen world, the vestibular system reports acceleration and gravity, and proprioception reports limb and body position. When those estimates cannot be reconciled, nausea, dizziness, disorientation, and related symptoms may follow.

The theory makes system timing and scene behavior part of human factors. [[Latency]], unstable tracking, or visually dramatic motion without corresponding physical cues can enlarge the mismatch, although the theory does not imply that every user or every symptom has a single cause.
