# Continuous Background Review

A continuous background review treats literature and prior evidence as an activity that develops throughout a research project. Early reading identifies the domain and vocabulary; later searches refine variables, measures, hypotheses, design choices, and the interpretation of results.

The review is iterative rather than a preliminary stage that ends when data collection begins. New findings may expose a missing concept or an alternative explanation, sending the researcher back to the literature and revising the [[Research Conceptual Framework]]. Conversely, the evolving framework makes later searches more focused.

This practice also supports novelty claims. A project can state what is already established, where evidence conflicts or is incomplete, and how the proposed study changes the current body of knowledge.
