# Superconducting-Qubit Noise Spectroscopy

Noise spectroscopy uses a qubit as a frequency-selective sensor for its environment. The decay observed under a control sequence depends on the overlap between the environmental noise power spectral density and the sequence's filter function.

Energy relaxation probes transverse noise close to the qubit transition frequency. Dephasing experiments probe longitudinal fluctuations at much lower frequencies, with Ramsey emphasizing near-static components and multi-pulse sequences shifting sensitivity into controlled frequency bands. Quantum noise can be asymmetric in frequency: positive- and negative-frequency components correspond to the environment absorbing or supplying energy.

Inferring a spectrum requires a model of the qubit's coupling operator and susceptibility as well as the measured decay. A coherence curve alone does not uniquely identify whether the source is flux noise, charge noise, photon-number fluctuations, or quasiparticles. [[Dynamical Decoupling Filter Functions]] provide adjustable filters, while independent bias and temperature sweeps help distinguish mechanisms.
