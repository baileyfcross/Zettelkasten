# Algorithmic Accountability

Algorithmic accountability is the requirement that people and organizations using automated systems remain answerable for their design choices, decisions, and consequences. It includes identifying who can authorize use, who monitors performance, who explains an outcome, and who can provide correction or remedy.

Accountability is broader than [[AI Transparency]]. Information must reach an actor with both a duty to respond and the power to change the system. Clear roles, [[Algorithmic Auditability]], complaint procedures, and enforceable rules prevent distributed development from becoming a [[Responsibility Gap]].

For a recommender, accountability begins by making the beneficiary and objective explicit. A ranking optimized for platform revenue, user welfare, or time spent can produce different choices from the same interaction data. Disclosing promotion, testing for manipulation, and preserving a meaningful ability to decline connect the metric to the people affected by it.
