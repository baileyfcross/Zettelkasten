# Specific Heat Capacity

Specific heat capacity is the energy required to raise the temperature of one kilogram of a material by one kelvin. For a temperature change without phase change, $Q=mc\Delta T$. The property is material-dependent, so equal masses receiving equal energy can undergo different temperature changes.

Statistical mechanics derives heat capacity from the temperature dependence of average energy. A partition function therefore predicts how available microscopic modes contribute, while quantum restrictions can make the low-temperature result depart sharply from the classical value, as in the Debye treatment of lattice vibrations.
