# Kessler Syndrome

Kessler syndrome is a potential cascade in which collisions among spacecraft and debris create fragments that cause further collisions. As object density rises, the process can continue even without new launches and make an orbital region too hazardous for reliable use.

Donald Kessler described the risk in 1978. Large constellations, dead vehicles, and deliberate satellite destruction can all add collision opportunities. Prevention requires treating orbit as a shared environment through tracking, collision avoidance, responsible disposal, and limits on fragment-producing activity rather than assuming each mission's debris is isolated.
