# Elliptic Curve Cryptography

Elliptic curve cryptography is a branch of public-key cryptography built from the group of points on an elliptic curve. Point addition supplies the group operation, a point at infinity acts as the identity, and repeated addition produces scalar multiplication (Q=kP). The corresponding discrete-logarithm problem asks for (k) when (P) and (Q) are known.

The source emphasizes that elliptic-curve systems can provide a security level comparable to RSA with substantially smaller keys. That reduces storage, bandwidth, and processing requirements and makes the construction useful in signatures, secure protocols, and constrained devices.
