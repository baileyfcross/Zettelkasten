# Self-Tracking Data Business Model

A self-tracking data business model explains how a firm uses personal records to create and capture economic value. Data may be the product delivered to the customer, an input to premium coaching or personalization, a basis for targeted advertising, a source of aggregate analysis, or an asset exchanged with partners.

These models create different incentives. A device seller may prioritize attractive hardware, a coaching service needs repeated interpretation, and an advertising-supported platform benefits from broad collection and continued attention. A firm may also collect data before it has a defined use because future analysis is expected to become valuable.

Understanding the model helps explain choices about retention, access, interoperability, and privacy. The question is not only whether money is made, but whose activity produced the value and who bears the risk of reuse.
