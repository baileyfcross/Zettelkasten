# Multivariable Chain Rule

The multivariable chain rule differentiates a composite whose intermediate variables depend on one or more underlying coordinates. Each route by which the target depends on the chosen coordinate contributes a product of derivatives, and those contributions are added.

For $f(u)$ with $u=u(x,y)$, holding $y$ fixed gives $f_x=(df/du)u_x$. When $f$ depends on several intermediate variables, the derivative with respect to $x$ is the sum of each partial derivative of $f$ multiplied by the corresponding derivative of that intermediate variable.

For vector-valued maps, the same rule is expressed by multiplying [[Jacobian Matrix|Jacobian matrices]] in composition order. This is the matrix representation of composing the underlying linear derivatives.
