# Ground Speed at a Constant Heading

When aircraft heading is held fixed, wind changes both the magnitude and direction of the ground-velocity vector. A perpendicular wind adds a sideways component and can make the magnitude of ground velocity slightly greater than true airspeed, even though the resulting track drifts. This differs from [[Ground Speed on a Desired Track]], where heading is allowed to change to preserve course.
