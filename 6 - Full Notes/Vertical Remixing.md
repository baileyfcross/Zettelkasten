# Vertical Remixing

Vertical remixing changes a cue by adding, removing, or balancing synchronized layers while the same underlying timeline continues. A common design uses two or three [[Interactive Music Stem|stems]] whose instrumentation and density represent different intensity levels.

Because the cue does not have to restart, vertical remixing can react quickly without breaking the current phrase. Its tradeoff is that tempo and harmonic order generally remain fixed, and simultaneous layers consume more voices, memory, and buffering capacity. Each layer therefore needs a clear trigger, an intended mix role, and room for silence when the game calls for less musical activity.
