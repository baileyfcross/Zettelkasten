# Foveated Rendering

Foveated rendering allocates the highest image quality to the region a person is looking at and renders peripheral regions at lower detail. It uses [[Eye Tracking]] to follow the point of regard and exploits the fact that human vision has much finer spatial detail near the center of gaze than at the edges.

This unequal allocation reduces the graphical work required for a wide [[Field of View]], allowing limited processing capacity to support sharper focal imagery or a steadier frame rate. The transition must follow gaze quickly and remain visually unobtrusive; lag, a visible quality boundary, or an inaccurate gaze estimate can expose the optimization and disrupt the experience.

Foveated rendering therefore couples sensing and display performance. It does not improve the physical [[Spatial Resolution]] of the panel, but it can spend rendering effort where that resolution is perceptually most valuable.
