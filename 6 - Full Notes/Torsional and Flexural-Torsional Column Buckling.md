# Torsional and Flexural-Torsional Column Buckling

Compression instability is not always simple bending. Torsional buckling rotates a member about its longitudinal axis with little lateral translation, while flexural-torsional buckling couples twist with lateral bending. Singly symmetric, unsymmetric, open, and cruciform sections can be more vulnerable than compact doubly symmetric shapes.

Resistance depends on torsional stiffness, warping stiffness, shear-center location, symmetry, unbraced length, and the distribution of material around the axes. The controlling critical stress is compared with ordinary flexural buckling and local-buckling limits. Bracing that restrains only translation may leave twist or warping insufficiently controlled, so connection and brace geometry must restrain the deformation mode actually capable of occurring.
