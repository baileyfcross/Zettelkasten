# Improper Integral

An improper integral has an infinite interval or an integrand that becomes unbounded within the integration range. It is defined through a limit that replaces the problematic endpoint or singular point with a finite boundary.

The integral converges only when the defining limit exists and is finite. If a singularity lies inside the interval, the integral must be split and both one-sided limits must converge independently; cancellation across the singularity does not establish ordinary convergence.
